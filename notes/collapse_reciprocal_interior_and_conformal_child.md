## Abstract

Several mature lines of gravitational research describe different pieces of one possible causal architecture. Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse places an FLRW matter region behind a Schwarzschild exterior. Einstein–Straus and McVittie show how a locally Schwarzschild-like region can inhabit a larger FLRW universe. Limiting-curvature, regular-black-hole, Einstein–Cartan, and black-universe models replace the Schwarzschild singularity with a de Sitter core, a bounce, or an expanding cosmological region. Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology joins an infinite de Sitter-like future to a new Big Bang by conformal completion.

These results do not already establish CSBHI. They do, however, suggest a cleaner interpretation than a direct identification of the parent event horizon with the child Big Bang. The proposed geometry has three stages: a parent cosmological black hole; a reciprocal interior in which the parent areal radius approaches zero only at infinite infaller proper time; and a conformal crossover identifying that regular future boundary with the past boundary of a child FLRW universe. The event horizon begins the reciprocal history. It is not itself the child's Big Bang.

No reviewed construction combines all three stages with the CSBHI relation

\[
a_I(\tau)=\frac{1}{r_P(\tau)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(r_P\) is the parent-horizon-normalized areal radius assigned to the infalling trajectory, and \(a_I\) is the scale factor of the proposed reciprocal interior. The missing result is therefore precise: derive a spacetime whose parent collapse, reciprocal interior, and conformal child boundary share consistent causal, curvature, and stress-energy data.

## 1. The geometry under review

The candidate causal chain is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_{mathrm{FLRW}}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal B_P
\longrightarrow
\mathcal H_P
\longrightarrow
\mathcal I_{mathrm{rec}}
\longrightarrow
\Sigma
\longrightarrow
\mathcal C_{mathrm{FLRW}}
}.
\tag{2}
\]

The symbols denote:

- \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{FLRW}}\): the large-scale parent FLRW universe;
- \(\mathcal B_P\): a black-hole region that is locally well approximated by Schwarzschild or Kottler geometry;
- \(\mathcal H_P\): the parent black-hole event horizon;
- \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{rec}}\): the proposed reciprocal interior followed by Alice after horizon crossing;
- \(\Sigma\): the future conformal boundary of that reciprocal interior, considered as a possible crossover surface;
- \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{FLRW}}\): the child FLRW universe whose past boundary is represented by \(\Sigma\).

This proposal distinguishes two maps that earlier discussions often combined:

1. **The entry map:** parent collapse and horizon data determine the initial data of the reciprocal interior.
2. **The crossover map:** the reciprocal interior's infinite future is conformally represented as the child's finite past boundary.

Equation (1) supplies a scale relation inside the reciprocal patch. It does not by itself supply either complete map.

### A causal warning about infinity

The limit \(r_P\to\infty\) in a local black-hole chart is not the parent Big Bang. Spatial infinity, the cosmological particle horizon, the last-scattering surface, and the spacelike Big-Bang boundary are different objects. A parent observer learns about the early universe along a past light cone; looking farther away also means looking farther back, but it does not turn radial infinity into the Big Bang.

The proposed recursion must therefore relate complete causal boundaries, not merely exchange the endpoints of one radial coordinate.

## 2. Schwarzschild and Kruskal: the problem in its sharpest form

The Schwarzschild solution supplies the local exterior field of an isolated, nonrotating spherical mass. Its maximal Kruskal extension shows that the horizon is regular while the classical \(r=0\) singularity is a spacelike future boundary for every infalling timelike observer.

This establishes three facts that any replacement must respect:

- Alice crosses the event horizon without a local impulse;
- the horizon is a null causal boundary, not a material shell;
- ordinary vacuum Schwarzschild evolution ends at a curvature singularity in finite proper time.

The eternal Kruskal extension also contains a white-hole region and a second exterior. Those regions belong to the maximally extended eternal solution; they are not produced by realistic one-sided stellar collapse. CSBHI therefore cannot obtain its child universe merely by relabeling the unused Kruskal quadrants.

**Contribution to the collage:** the local exterior, the regular horizon, and the causal character of the trapped region.

**Missing piece:** a regular replacement for the spacelike singular boundary.

## 3. Oppenheimer–Snyder: FLRW already lives inside collapse

Oppenheimer and Snyder gave the canonical exact model of homogeneous pressureless collapse. A contracting FLRW dust interior matches a Schwarzschild vacuum exterior across the timelike surface of the star [1].

This is the strongest classical precedent for the parent side of the proposed construction:

\[
\text{contracting FLRW matter}
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{one exterior Schwarzschild mass}.
\tag{3}
\]

The result also clarifies the curvature accounting. Homogeneous FLRW matter can have zero Weyl curvature and large Ricci curvature while the exterior vacuum carries nonzero Weyl curvature and zero matter Ricci curvature. These are two regions of one matched spacetime, not two coordinate expressions for one local curvature tensor.

Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse nevertheless reaches a singularity. It provides neither a reciprocal expansion nor a child continuation.

**Contribution:** an exact FLRW–Schwarzschild collapse geometry and a low-Weyl matter source.

**Missing piece:** singularity resolution and outward child expansion.

## 4. Einstein–Straus and McVittie: a black hole can inhabit FLRW

Real black holes are not asymptotically flat objects detached from the universe. Two classic constructions make the local/global distinction precise.

The Einstein–Straus model inserts a spherical Schwarzschild vacuole into an FLRW dust universe. With a common cosmological constant, its corresponding vacuum region is Kottler. Under its assumptions, it is an exact smooth matching, but it is highly rigid: the static region must be spherical, and realistic nonspherical generalizations are strongly restricted [2].

McVittie's metric instead describes a central gravitating inhomogeneity directly within an expanding FLRW background. For late-time positive-\(\Lambda\) cosmologies, the relevant solutions contain regular cosmological black holes and approach Schwarzschild–de Sitter geometry near the horizon and in the future [3]. McVittie does not describe ordinary accreting collapse, and its global interpretation depends on the chosen scale factor and matter flow.

A recent matching analysis strengthens the caution. Generic attempts to match an isotropic FLRW source to a McVittie region require a hypersurface whose causal character can change; an everywhere-timelike isotropic source does not generally exist [4]. This does not forbid a parent cosmological black hole. It means that “locally Schwarzschild, globally FLRW” is a controlled approximation whose exact completion carries nontrivial matter and boundary data.

**Contribution:** the parent need not be asymptotically flat; a local black-hole sector can coexist with large-scale FLRW expansion.

**Missing piece:** neither construction supplies a child universe beyond the trapped region.

## 5. Kantowski–Sachs: the ordinary Schwarzschild interior is cosmological but anisotropic

Inside a Schwarzschild horizon, the areal-radius coordinate becomes timelike. Rewriting the trapped region as a cosmology gives a Kantowski–Sachs geometry: homogeneous, but not isotropic. The spherical directions and the longitudinal direction evolve at different rates [5].

This is why a bare Schwarzschild interior cannot be identified with the observed FLRW universe. A successful child construction must either

- change the interior dynamics before the child era;
- isotropize an initially Kantowski–Sachs region; or
- treat the Schwarzschild chart as only the parent-side projection of a different reciprocal geometry.

CSBHI chooses the third route at the level of its ansatz. It assigns Alice an isotropic reciprocal spatial scale rather than claiming that ordinary Schwarzschild coordinates secretly form an FLRW chart. The completed geometry must still explain why that assignment is dynamically correct.

**Contribution:** identifies the anisotropy that any direct Schwarzschild-to-FLRW claim must overcome.

**Missing piece:** the mechanism selecting the shear-free reciprocal geometry.

## 6. Pathria and finite FLRW black-hole universes

Pathria identified a closed Friedmann universe at maximum expansion with the interior of a Schwarzschild black hole whose horizon had the same radius [6]. The proposal made the horizon-scale relation explicit, but a later null-junction calculation found a surface pressure on the proposed matching hypersurface [7]. Equality of radii is not enough to establish a smooth spacetime.

Recent finite-cloud models developed by Gaztañaga and collaborators revisit the same broad architecture with an FLRW cloud, a Schwarzschild exterior, and a nonsingular bounce. Their later work uses an effective negative-pressure ground state motivated by quantum exclusion to generate the bounce and an expansion phase [8,9].

These models place the child cosmology immediately within a finite collapsing cloud. The three-stage proposal considered here instead places a reciprocal asymptotic region between horizon entry and the child's conformal beginning.

**Contribution:** explicit horizon-scale cosmology and concrete matching lessons.

**Missing piece:** the reciprocal interior and future-boundary crossover.

## 7. Limiting curvature and the de Sitter core

Markov proposed a limiting density, and Frolov, Markov, and Mukhanov applied a limiting-curvature hypothesis to black-hole interiors. They replaced the deep Schwarzschild interior with a de Sitter region across a short spacelike transition layer and investigated continuation into a new universe [10–12].

Regular-black-hole models developed another version of the same geometric idea. Hayward constructed nonsingular dynamical black-hole spacetimes whose central stress-energy behaves like a cosmological constant [13]. Dymnikova-type solutions likewise replace the central singularity with a de Sitter core.

The shared insight is geometric:

\[
\text{Schwarzschild-like exterior}
\longrightarrow
\text{bounded-curvature de Sitter-like interior}.
\tag{4}
\]

Their mechanisms differ from CSBHI. Limiting-curvature models impose a high-curvature bound; regular black holes require a chosen effective stress-energy profile. CSBHI instead obtains de Sitter behavior as the late-time limit of the reciprocal matter–radiation–vacuum history. Its endpoint is approached asymptotically rather than installed at a finite high-curvature surface.

**Contribution:** de Sitter geometry is an established candidate replacement for a black-hole singularity.

**Missing piece:** why the replacement follows from \(a_I=1/r_P\), and how its future becomes a child beginning.

## 8. Black universes: the closest single-spacetime precedent

Bronnikov and collaborators use the term **black universe** for a regular black hole whose horizon opens into an expanding Kantowski–Sachs universe with de Sitter infinity instead of a singularity [14,15]. Their causal chain is remarkably close to the middle portion of Equation (2):

\[
\text{asymptotically flat exterior}
\longrightarrow
\text{event horizon}
\longrightarrow
\text{expanding cosmological region}
\longrightarrow
\text{de Sitter infinity}.
\tag{5}
\]

These are explicit solutions of Einstein's equations, but the four-dimensional examples generally rely on phantom scalar matter with negative kinetic energy. The interior is initially Kantowski–Sachs and only becomes isotropic asymptotically. Braneworld variants can reproduce similar behavior with different effective sources.

This is the closest prior geometry to the claim that Alice can fall through a regular horizon and approach de Sitter infinity rather than a singularity. It does not contain the reciprocal relation, ordinary matter–radiation dilution, or a conformal child universe beginning beyond that infinity.

**Contribution:** an explicit horizon-to-expanding-universe-to-de-Sitter-infinity causal structure.

**Missing piece:** acceptable matter, isotropic reciprocal evolution, and the conformal continuation.

## 9. Einstein–Cartan: a child universe through a finite bounce

Einstein–Cartan theory extends the affine connection by allowing torsion sourced by intrinsic spin [16]. In Popławski's black-hole cosmology, the spin–torsion interaction becomes repulsive at extreme fermion density, prevents the singularity, and produces a nonsingular closed universe after a bounce. Particle production can enlarge and isotropize the child [17,18].

The causal architecture is

\[
\text{collapse}
\longrightarrow
\text{horizon}
\longrightarrow
\text{high-density contraction}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite bounce}
\longrightarrow
\text{expanding child}.
\tag{6}
\]

This supplies precisely what classical Schwarzschild lacks: a physical bridge through the endpoint. It does so by changing the gravitational connection and adding spin-dependent high-density dynamics.

CSBHI's new three-stage reading differs sharply. Alice never reaches \(r_P=0\) in finite proper time, so there is no finite event at which contraction reverses. The child boundary would arise by conformal completion of Alice's infinite future, not by Alice traversing a bounce.

**Contribution:** an explicit parent-black-hole/child-universe mechanism and an arrow of time.

**Missing piece:** it does not support the reciprocal ansatz or an infinite-future crossover.

## 10. Quantum and spacelike transitions

Several programmes replace the singularity with a localized transition.

Haggard–Rovelli and Ashtekar–Olmedo–Singh construct black-to-white-hole or trapped-to-antitrapped transitions motivated by loop quantum gravity [19–21]. Brandenberger, Heisenberg, and Robnik join a black-hole interior to a new cosmology across an S-brane appearing when the Weyl curvature reaches the string scale [22]. False-vacuum baby-universe models use a thin wall, wormhole, and sometimes quantum tunnelling to create an inflating region causally separated from its parent [23–25].

These constructions demonstrate that the global causal diagram can change without placing a material membrane at the ordinary event horizon. They also show the price: a quantum region, new high-curvature physics, exotic wall stress, or tunnelling prescription must do the connecting work.

The reciprocal proposal moves the candidate crossover away from the event horizon and out to an infinite-future boundary. That may avoid a finite high-curvature transition, but it does not eliminate the need for crossover data.

**Contribution:** explicit examples of how a classical singular boundary can be replaced by another causal region.

**Missing piece:** a classical reciprocal and conformal mechanism requiring none of their additional transition physics.

## 11. Penrose: future infinity as another universe's Big Bang

Conformal cyclic cosmology supplies the closest precedent for the second map in Section 1. CCC joins the infinite future of one aeon to the conformally stretched Big Bang of the next across a spacelike crossover surface [26,27]. The physical metrics on either side are related to a regular bridging metric by reciprocal conformal factors.

This supplies the missing conceptual distinction:

\[
\text{infinite future in one physical metric}
\quad\not=\quad
\text{an event reached at finite proper time},
\tag{7}
\]

yet the same conformal boundary can represent a finite cosmological beginning in another physical metric.

CCC is not a black-hole cosmology. Its crossover joins complete cosmic aeons, requires special asymptotic matter conditions, and does not use the Schwarzschild radius or \(a_I=1/r_P\). Nevertheless, it supplies mature mathematical language for exactly the operation the proposed child map now requires.

Penrose's Weyl Curvature Hypothesis adds a boundary condition: the child beginning must have vanishing or extremely small free tidal curvature even while its Ricci curvature and matter density are large [28]. A de Sitter-like reciprocal future has vanishing background Weyl curvature, making it a promising conformal precursor. Whether perturbations remain regular and produce the observed small primordial fluctuations is still open.

**Contribution:** the future-infinity-to-Big-Bang conformal operation and its low-Weyl requirement.

**Missing piece:** a black-hole origin, a reciprocal radial scale, and a derived matter/entropy transfer.

## 12. Boyle–Finn–Turok: continuation across the Big Bang

The CPT-symmetric universe treats the pre- and post-Big-Bang epochs as CPT reflections. The construction analytically continues fields across the bang and uses the symmetry to select a quantum vacuum [29,30].

Its relevance is narrower than CCC's. It establishes that a cosmological singular surface can serve as a symmetry surface relating two descriptions and that the continuation can constrain particle physics. It does not map an infinite future to a new beginning and does not invoke a parent black hole.

**Contribution:** a disciplined example of extending geometry and quantum fields across a cosmological boundary.

**Missing piece:** the parent collapse and reciprocal interior.

## 14. What this changes in the CSBHI research programme

The new decomposition removes one false target. The project need not match a complete child FLRW universe directly onto the parent event horizon. Instead it must solve two smaller problems.

### Problem A: horizon entry

Construct the parent collapse and reciprocal interior so that:

1. the event horizon is regular for Alice;
2. the intrinsic and transverse geometry agrees across the entry surface;
3. parent matter, radiation, and flux satisfy local conservation;
4. the resulting interior is shear-free at the homogeneous background level;
5. its dynamics select, rather than merely assume, \(a_I=1/r_P\).

### Problem B: conformal crossover

Show that the \(r_P\to0\), \(a_I\to\infty\) limit admits a regular conformal completion \(\Sigma\) such that:

1. the reciprocal physical metric has finite asymptotic curvature;
2. Alice reaches \(\Sigma\) only after infinite physical proper time;
3. a child physical metric extends away from \(\Sigma\) with an FLRW hot-Big-Bang history;
4. the Weyl tensor satisfies the required low-Weyl boundary condition;
5. matter, entropy, and perturbation data obey a stated transfer law.

The second problem is not an ordinary junction across a finite material surface. It is a conformal-boundary problem, closer to CCC than to a thin-shell matching.

## 15. Claims this review does not license

The prior literature supports the plausibility of each geometric ingredient. It does not establish their composition. In particular:

- the parent event horizon has not been shown to be the child Big Bang;
- the parent radial limit \(r_P\to\infty\) is not the parent Big-Bang surface;
- a de Sitter endpoint does not automatically admit a hot FLRW continuation;
- a conformal identification does not automatically transfer matter, entropy, or quantum information;
- Einstein–Cartan, limiting-curvature, phantom-matter, and loop-gravity mechanisms do not derive \(a_I=1/r_P\);
- the present CSBHI equations do not yet supply one global metric covering Equation (2).

What the review does license is a sharper conjecture:

> The parent event horizon may begin a reciprocal cosmological history whose regular de Sitter-like future, rather than the horizon itself, serves as the conformal past boundary of a child FLRW universe.

This conjecture aligns three previously separate observations: black-hole interiors can open into expanding cosmological regions; positive-\(\Lambda\) evolution supplies a regular de Sitter-like infinity; and a future conformal infinity can represent another cosmology's Big Bang.

## 16. Conclusion

The proposed geometry is not a rediscovery of one earlier model. It is a specific composition of well-developed ideas.

Schwarzschild and Kruskal provide the horizon and trapped causal region. Oppenheimer–Snyder provides homogeneous FLRW collapse behind one exterior mass. Einstein–Straus and McVittie place the local black hole inside a cosmological parent. Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov and the black-universe solutions replace the singular future with de Sitter-like geometry. Einstein–Cartan and quantum-transition models demonstrate that a child cosmology can follow collapse when additional dynamics resolve the endpoint. Penrose demonstrates that infinite future and a new Big Bang can be two physical representations of one conformal boundary.

CSBHI adds the reciprocal scale relation and proposes that the de Sitter future reached as \(r_P\to0\) is the correct crossover surface. That move explains why earlier attempts to place the whole child universe immediately behind the event horizon felt geometrically strained: they collapsed two distinct maps into one.

The next calculation is no longer “put FLRW inside Schwarzschild.” The literature has explored that problem many times. It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{derive the reciprocal entry map, then construct its conformal future-to-child continuation.}
}
\tag{9}
\]
