# Black-Hole Cosmology: Lineage, Mechanisms, and CSBHI

## Scope

"The universe is inside a black hole" is not one theory. It is a family of proposals that share an image while assigning very different meanings to the horizon, the singularity, the interior geometry, and the birth of cosmic expansion.

This note separates five lines of thought:

1. **containment:** a cosmological region is matched to a black-hole exterior;
2. **regularization:** the singular interior is replaced by a de Sitter core, a bounce, or a limiting-curvature endpoint;
3. **reproduction:** black holes generate new universes and possibly an evolving population of them;
4. **conformal or observer duality:** apparently incompatible descriptions are related by a change of scale or causal standpoint;
5. **quantum-gravity completion:** a specific quantum theory of gravity is used to resolve the classical singularity directly, rather than positing a bound or a new fluid.

CSBHI touches all five lines, but belongs completely to none of them. Its defining relation is

\[
a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)},
\]

where \(r\) is the horizon-normalized radius Bob assigns to Alice's infall and \(a\) is the spatial scale of Alice's proposed interior geometry.

The aim of this note is not only comparison. Several of the names below did the hard work — sometimes in a single page, sometimes across a career — of showing that a black-hole interior could be read as a cosmology at all. CSBHI enters the line of inquiry shaped by that work, and the least it owes them is an accurate account of what each of them actually did.

---

## 1. Who was first?

The answer depends on what counts as the beginning.

### The mathematical precursor: Oppenheimer and Snyder, 1939

Oppenheimer and Snyder gave the canonical exact relativistic model of a homogeneous dust sphere collapsing through its gravitational radius. The exterior is Schwarzschild; the matter-filled interior is a contracting FLRW geometry. [1]

They did not propose that the interior was a new universe. Their result nevertheless established the mathematical precedent used by many later black-hole cosmologies: one spacetime can look like stellar collapse from outside while its material interior is described by a cosmological metric.

### The first published comparison located here: *Nature*, August 1971

An anonymous *Nature* News & Views article, *The Universe Considered as Hole*, observed that a closed universe would be "in essentially a rather large black hole." It described a closed universe that eventually recollapses and perhaps begins another expansion. This was a black-hole analogy for the universe as a whole, not yet a parent/child cosmology, but it predates both Good and Pathria and appears in Pathria's reference list. [2, 4]

### The first nested parent-universe proposal located here: I. J. Good, July 1972

In the one-page *Physics Today* letter *Chinese Universes*, I. J. Good argued that the observable universe was probably a black hole inside a larger universe. He imagined an infinite nesting of black and white holes, "like carved Chinese spheres," and interpreted the Big Bang as the transition of a massive galaxy into a black or white hole within a parent universe. [3]

Good's construction was qualitative and tied to steady-state ideas, continual creation, and a speculative negative-density medium. It supplied a nested-universe picture rather than a junction calculation.

### The first canonical GR construction: R. K. Pathria, December 1972

Pathria's *The Universe as a Black Hole* was received by *Nature* on 19 June 1972 and published on 1 December. He identified a closed, pressureless Friedmann universe at maximum expansion with the interior of a Schwarzschild black hole whose horizon has the same radius. [4]

Good therefore has publication priority over Pathria for the nested parent-universe proposal, while Pathria supplied the first compact mathematical model and became the standard historical citation. The sources establish their publication order; they do not establish a line of influence between the two authors.

Among the sources examined for this note, the fairest summary is:

> Oppenheimer and Snyder supplied the collapse geometry. *Nature* made the universe–black-hole comparison in 1971. Good placed our universe inside a nested parent hierarchy in July 1972. Pathria turned the idea into a recognizable black-hole cosmology calculation that December.

## 2. Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse: cosmology inside a star

The Oppenheimer–Snyder model joins

\[
\text{contracting FLRW dust interior}
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{Schwarzschild vacuum exterior}.
\]

Its central claim is not that a universe is born. It is that homogeneous pressureless matter can cross its Schwarzschild radius and continue collapsing in finite comoving proper time even though the exterior observer sees extreme redshift and asymptotic delay.

This is important to CSBHI for two reasons:

- it is the earliest exact example of cosmological geometry inside a collapsing body;
- it cleanly separates the infaller's proper time from the exterior Schwarzschild time — the same separation that carries the entire weight of GD1's Alice/Bob thought experiment.

Its limitation is equally clear: classical collapse ends at a curvature singularity. It does not provide isotropic expansion after horizon crossing, a reciprocal scale, or a nonsingular future. Everything from Section 3 onward in this note is, in one sense, a list of attempts to fix that one limitation.

## 3. Good and Pathria: two origins of black-hole cosmology

Good and Pathria asked different versions of the same question.

### Good's nested hierarchy

Good proposed that universes and black holes form an infinite hierarchy. A black hole in one universe can be a universe from within, while the containing universe may itself occupy a larger white-hole structure.

**Central claim:** the Big Bang was a black- or white-hole-forming event inside a larger universe.

**Mechanism:** continual creation and a nested steady-state hierarchy.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Good supplied no interior metric, reciprocal relation, or horizon map. His contribution is the parent-child ontology — the picture the rest of this literature spends fifty years trying to make rigorous.

### Pathria's horizon equality

Pathria compared a closed Friedmann universe at maximum expansion with a Schwarzschild black hole and identified the maximum cosmic radius with the event-horizon radius.

**Central claim:** a closed universe can be confined within a Schwarzschild black hole.

**Mechanism:** equality of characteristic radii at the moment of maximum expansion.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Pathria's universe reaches a maximum size and recollapses. CSBHI instead maps decreasing exterior radius to increasing interior scale and approaches a de Sitter future rather than a big crunch.

A later null-shell calculation by Khakshournia found that Pathria's matching hypersurface carries surface pressure. [5] The boundary is not the simple, stress-free coincidence of radii it first appears to be. This is a decisive lesson for GD2: equality of radii does not by itself establish a smooth junction.

## 4. Stuckey: the observable universe as a Schwarzschild interior

W. M. Stuckey's 1994 *American Journal of Physics* paper, *The Observable Universe Inside a Black Hole*, provides a particularly direct comparison with GD1's setup.

Stuckey introduces a Schwarzschild radial coordinate \(R\) for dust-filled Friedmann models. A constant-\(R\) worldline becomes instantaneously null at \(R_n=2GM/c^2\), where \(M\) is the Schwarzschild mass inside that sphere. He then abandons global homogeneity and joins segments of Friedmann dust to segments of Schwarzschild vacuum; in the resulting model, the observable universe may lie inside a black or white hole. [6]

**Central claim:** an observer inside an FLRW dust cosmology can consistently describe their own universe as the interior of a black hole (or white hole), using only the coordinate apparatus already standard for stellar collapse.

**Mechanism:** a Schwarzschild radial coordinate for Friedmann dust, followed by a piecewise construction joining Friedmann and Schwarzschild regions.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Stuckey does not introduce a reciprocal scale relation, an Alice/Bob infall map, or an asymptotic de Sitter endpoint. CSBHI follows one infall history and proposes \(a=1/r\) as the relation between the exterior radius and interior spatial scale.

Stuckey therefore belongs in the direct comparison literature for GD1 and GD2, even though his construction and CSBHI's reciprocal map are distinct.

## 5. False-vacuum bubbles and manufactured child universes

In the 1980s, black-hole cosmology became a problem in vacuum decay and junction dynamics.

Blau, Guendelman, and Guth studied false-vacuum bubbles separated from an exterior region by a thin wall. A sufficiently large inflating interior can become causally separated behind a wormhole or black-hole geometry. Farhi and Guth then showed that, under ordinary classical energy conditions, producing such an inflating universe from nonsingular asymptotically flat initial data encounters an initial-singularity obstruction. Farhi, Guth, and Guven investigated quantum tunnelling as a possible route around that obstruction. [7–9]

**Central claim:** an inflating child universe may branch from a parent spacetime through false-vacuum bubble dynamics.

**Mechanism:** a vacuum-energy bubble, thin-shell junction, wormhole geometry, and possibly quantum tunnelling.

**Difference from CSBHI:** the child universe is nucleated as a new inflating region behind a bubble wall. CSBHI does not introduce a false-vacuum bubble or require a branching event; it follows one infalling history and assigns its reciprocal spatial description.

This literature is especially relevant to the horizon-map problem because it shows how much work hides inside the word "connects": the shell trajectory, energy conditions, global causal diagram, and junction stress tensor all matter, and Farhi–Guth's no-go result is a reminder that some of the most natural-looking constructions turn out to be classically obstructed.

## 6. Markov, and Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov: limiting curvature and a de Sitter core

M. A. Markov proposed a universal upper limit on matter density in 1982 and developed its cosmological consequences in 1984. Frolov, Markov, and Mukhanov later formulated the related limiting-curvature assumption for a black-hole interior. Density and curvature are not interchangeable here: the first is Markov's earlier postulate, while the second is the explicit assumption in the 1989 black-hole model. [10–12]

Frolov, Markov, and Mukhanov then applied this hypothesis specifically to black-hole collapse. They attached the Schwarzschild interior to a de Sitter region across a short spacelike transition layer, replacing the classical curvature blow-up with a bounded, regular core, and asked whether the resulting region could continue into a new universe.

**Central claim:** limiting curvature can replace the Schwarzschild singularity with a regular de Sitter interior.

**Mechanism:** a high-curvature transition from Schwarzschild to de Sitter geometry, imposed as an additional dynamical postulate on top of general relativity.

**Difference from CSBHI:** their de Sitter core is introduced when curvature reaches a postulated fundamental bound. In CSBHI, de Sitter geometry is instead the late-time limit of the matter–radiation–\(\Lambda\) reciprocal interior; no new curvature scale or high-curvature transition layer is introduced.

This is one of the closest precedents for CSBHI's asymptotic endpoint, but the direction is different: Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov regularize the deep interior at high curvature; CSBHI sends Bob's \(r=0\) to Alice's infinite future with finite limiting curvature reached only asymptotically, using nothing beyond standard ΛCDM matter content.

## 7. Smolin: cosmological natural selection

Lee Smolin turned child-universe formation into a theory of why the constants of nature take their observed values. He proposed that black-hole singularities bounce or tunnel into new expanding universes and that dimensionless parameters change slightly during reproduction. Universes that produce more black holes then have more descendants, and — over cosmological-selection time — the parameter space should be found clustered near local optima for black-hole production. [13]

**Central claim:** the parameters of physics may be shaped by Darwinian selection for black-hole production.

**Mechanism:** quantum-gravity resolution of black-hole singularities, reproduction, and small parameter mutations across generations.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Smolin's central result is population-level selection, not the metric experienced inside one black hole. The child-universe bounce is an assumed reproductive mechanism rather than a reciprocal infall-expansion map, and Smolin's proposal is explicitly agnostic about the detailed interior geometry it presupposes.

CSBHI could eventually supply geometric content to the reproduction step, but cosmological natural selection neither predicts nor requires \(a=1/r\); it is a selection argument sitting one level of abstraction above any of the specific interior geometries in this note.

## 8. Easson and Brandenberger: the cosmological payoff

Easson and Brandenberger deliberately worked above the level of any single formation mechanism. They argued that a universe born inside a black hole could naturally alter the horizon, flatness, structure-formation, and information-loss problems normally assigned to the standard Big Bang and inflation. [14]

**Central claim:** black-hole origin scenarios may resolve several cosmological initial-condition problems without a long conventional inflationary phase.

**Mechanism:** intentionally model-independent; the paper identifies consequences shared by a class of scenarios rather than deriving them from one metric.

**Difference from CSBHI:** their work is a programme-level argument rather than a specific geometry. CSBHI proposes the reciprocal geometry whose explanatory consequences can be tested one at a time, which is both its advantage (falsifiability) and its current burden (the horizon map is not yet built).

## 9. 't Hooft and Susskind: complementarity, holography, and the observer split

Two things get conflated under "'t Hooft" in casual treatments of this topic, and both belong here, properly separated and properly credited to their actual authors.

The first is the **holographic principle** itself. 't Hooft's 1993 paper "Dimensional Reduction in Quantum Gravity" argued that Planck-scale degrees of freedom can be described on a two-dimensional lattice, based on unitarity, entropy, and counting. Susskind's 1995 paper "The World as a Hologram" formulated the broader claim that a spatial volume can be encoded on its boundary with roughly one degree of freedom per Planck area. [15, 16]

The second is **black-hole complementarity**, developed with different emphases by 't Hooft and by Susskind, Thorlacius, and Uglum in their 1993 paper "The Stretched Horizon and Black Hole Complementarity." In the latter account, a distant observer uses a stretched-horizon description while an infalling observer crosses the horizon without encountering it as a material surface. The descriptions are complementary because no observer can operationally compare both accounts. [17]

't Hooft later revisited this terrain from a different angle in *Quantum Gravity without Space-time Singularities or Horizons* (2009), combining complementarity with an exact conformal symmetry conjectured to be part of the fundamental description of quantum gravity, under which horizons and singularities might not appear in the enlarged description. [18]

**Central claim:** exterior and infalling descriptions of a black hole are complementary rather than jointly realizable, and the information content of a region scales with its boundary area; 't Hooft's later work adds that an enlarged conformal symmetry might remove horizons and singularities from the fundamental description entirely.

**Mechanism:** the entropy-area relation, complementarity between causally disconnected observers, and (in 't Hooft's 2009 extension) postulated exact scale invariance.

**Difference from CSBHI:** none of this literature constructs an FLRW universe inside a black hole or proposes the radius-scale relation \(a=1/r\). The conceptual parallel is narrower: exterior and infalling descriptions can differ without either being discarded. This comparison does not establish that CSBHI follows historically or mathematically from black-hole complementarity.

Penrose is the closer algebraic cousin because his conformal factors obey a reciprocal hypothesis (Section 11 below). 't Hooft and Susskind are the closer interpretive comparison because their work explicitly distinguishes exterior and infalling descriptions, as CSBHI does through Bob and Alice.

## 10. Gott and Li: the self-creating universe

J. Richard Gott and Li-Xin Li's 1998 paper "Can the Universe Create Itself?" sits adjacent to this literature rather than inside it, for the same reason Penrose's CCC does: it is not a black-hole cosmology, but it shares the family's willingness to let the universe's own future determine its own past.

Gott and Li considered inflationary spacetimes containing closed timelike curves, in which a chronology-violating region removes the need for a first cause. One example has an inflationary universe produce baby universes, one of which is the universe itself. [19]

**Central claim:** under specific general-relativistic and quantum-cosmological conditions, a universe's inflationary beginning could be its own future effect, via a closed timelike curve, with no need for a first cause outside the loop.

**Mechanism:** a chronology-violating region bounded to the future by a Cauchy horizon, with self-consistent vacuum states exhibited in Misner space and multiply connected de Sitter space.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Gott and Li's construction requires closed timelike curves and addresses the problem of a first cause for the universe as a whole. CSBHI takes an already-existing exterior black hole as given, involves no closed timelike curves, and does not attempt to explain why the parent black hole itself exists.

It is included here because it dispenses with an external progenitor altogether. CSBHI asks a different question: it takes Bob's black hole as given and studies the proposed relation between exterior infall and interior expansion.

## 11. Penrose: conformal cyclic cosmology

Penrose's Conformal Cyclic Cosmology is not a black-hole cosmology. It belongs in this comparison because it uses a reciprocal conformal construction to connect apparently opposite cosmic limits.

CCC joins the remote future of one cosmic aeon to the Big Bang of the next. Once rest mass has disappeared and only conformally invariant physics remains, absolute length and time scales lose operational meaning. The exponentially expanded future can then be conformally compressed into the next aeon's beginning. [20]

If \(\hat g\) and \(\check g\) are the physical metrics of consecutive aeons and \(g\) is a bridging conformal metric, CCC writes

\[
\hat g_{ab}=\hat\Omega^2g_{ab},
\qquad
\check g_{ab}=\check\Omega^2g_{ab},
\]

with Penrose's reciprocal hypothesis relating the two conformal factors across the crossover.

**Central claim:** the conformally smooth infinite future of one universe is the conformally stretched Big Bang of the next.

**Mechanism:** conformal completion, loss of massive scales, the Weyl-curvature hypothesis, and a reciprocal relation between consecutive aeons.

### Why Penrose's scaling is not CSBHI's scaling

Penrose rescales complete spacetime metrics on opposite sides of a spacelike crossover between aeons. The conformal factor removes absolute scale while preserving causal structure.

CSBHI instead relates two physical quantities along one black-hole history:

\[
\text{Bob's decreasing infall radius }r(\tau)
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{Alice's increasing spatial scale }a(\tau)=1/r(\tau).
\]

CSBHI does not claim that a scalar Weyl rescaling turns the Schwarzschild metric into FLRW; their Weyl curvature and anisotropy differ, as explained in [Constraints on the Horizon Map](note.html?src=constraints_on_the_horizon_map) and independently of Penrose's construction. It proposes a physical horizon map whose construction remains open. Nor does CSBHI require the evaporation of every black hole, the disappearance of rest mass, or a succession of aeons.

The resemblance is real but limited: both theories use a reciprocal conformal quantity to relate opposite large-scale limits. They apply it to different geometries, different boundaries, and different physical problems.

## 12. Popławski: torsion and the big bounce

Nikodem Popławski develops black-hole cosmology in Einstein–Cartan gravity. Fermion spin sources spacetime torsion. At extreme density, the spin-torsion contribution becomes gravitationally repulsive, prevents a singularity, and produces a nonsingular bounce into a closed expanding universe. Particle production can drive an inflationary phase and increase the child universe's mass and entropy. [21, 22]

**Central claim:** every black hole containing fermionic matter can generate a nonsingular child universe beyond its event horizon.

**Mechanism:** Einstein–Cartan torsion, spin-fluid repulsion, a minimum scale factor, and a bounce.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Popławski modifies the connection (adding torsion to standard GR) and reverses contraction at a finite minimum size. CSBHI uses the standard matter–radiation–\(\Lambda\) background on Alice's side, introduces no torsion, and defers the endpoint asymptotically rather than bouncing through it.

The two programmes agree that an external black-hole description and an internal cosmological description can coexist. They differ on the physics that prevents the classical singularity: torsion here, a curvature bound in Section 6, quantum geometry in Section 13, and an infinite proper-time deferral under \(\Lambda\) in CSBHI.

## 13. Haggard–Rovelli and Ashtekar–Olmedo–Singh: black-to-white-hole transitions

These papers approach the singularity through quantum-gravity effects rather than a classical fluid or a postulated limiting-curvature layer. Their constructions are related to loop quantum gravity, but they do not use one identical mechanism.

**Haggard and Rovelli (2014)** constructed a classical metric satisfying Einstein's equations outside a finite spacetime region in which collapsing matter is taken to undergo a quantum transition from a black hole to a white hole. They argued that quantum effects can accumulate over a long exterior time and affect a small region outside the horizon without violating causality or the semiclassical approximation. The transition is short in the interior time and long in the exterior time. [23]

**Ashtekar, Olmedo, and Singh (2018)** constructed an effective description of macroscopic Kruskal black holes that incorporates quantum-geometry corrections motivated by loop quantum gravity. It covers both interior and asymptotic exterior regions. In the effective spacetime, the classical singularity is replaced by a transition surface joining trapped and anti-trapped regions. [24, 25]

**Central claim:** quantum-geometry effects can replace the classical Schwarzschild singularity with a black-hole-to-white-hole transition.

**Mechanism:** Haggard and Rovelli localize a quantum tunnelling region inside an otherwise classical metric; Ashtekar, Olmedo, and Singh derive an effective metric from a loop-quantized Schwarzschild model and extend it across the classical singularity.

**Difference from CSBHI:** neither construction introduces an isotropic FLRW interior or the reciprocal relation \(a=1/r\). They resolve the endpoint through quantum geometry; CSBHI instead proposes a classical reciprocal interior whose horizon map remains to be constructed.

These models are useful comparisons for GD2 because they make their transition geometry explicit, while CSBHI currently leaves the horizon map open.

## 14. Gaztañaga (and collaborators): a finite FLRW cloud inside its Schwarzschild radius

Enrique Gaztañaga's Black Hole Universe programme is a recent direct comparison. It combines a finite FLRW cloud, a Schwarzschild exterior, and a nonsingular bounce.

The 2022 paper, "How the Big Bang Ends Up Inside a Black Hole" (published in *Universe*), treats a finite FLRW cloud surrounded by vacuum and develops a collapse-and-bounce cosmology inside its Schwarzschild radius. The proposal identifies the black-hole event horizon with the source of apparent cosmic acceleration rather than introducing independent dark energy. [26]

The 2025 paper, "Gravitational Bounce from the Quantum Exclusion Principle," models the collapsing cloud as a perfect fluid whose equation of state evolves from dust toward a uniform ground state with \(P=-\rho\), motivated by quantum exclusion. Within general relativity, that effective equation of state produces a finite-radius bounce and an exponential expansion phase. The authors identify the enclosing Schwarzschild radius with an effective interior cosmological-constant scale, linking the same construction to late-time dark energy. [27]

**Central claim:** the observed universe may be a finite FLRW region formed by collapse and bounce inside its Schwarzschild radius; the bounce supplies an inflationary phase while the horizon supplies an effective dark-energy scale.

**Mechanism:** a bounded FLRW cloud, Schwarzschild exterior, junction conditions, and — in the 2025 extension — a quantum-exclusion-driven bounce rather than a classical crunch.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Gaztañaga begins with a finite FLRW sphere and supplies a collapse-and-bounce history. CSBHI begins with the observer contrast along infall into an already-existing black hole and introduces the reciprocal scale \(a=1/r\) without yet supplying the formation history or horizon map.

The overlap is substantial: parent collapse, horizon-scale \(\Lambda\)-like behavior, and an FLRW interior. The different bounce and scaling mechanisms make this essential comparison literature for GD2 and GD3.

## 16. What CSBHI inherits and what it adds

The historical parallels place CSBHI alongside several durable ideas:

- from Oppenheimer–Snyder, that FLRW geometry naturally appears inside spherical collapse;
- from Good and Pathria, that a universe may be globally associated with a black hole;
- from Stuckey, that Schwarzschild radial coordinates can be introduced in a dust-filled Friedmann model and used in a piecewise black-hole cosmology;
- from the baby-universe literature, that junction conditions and causal structure cannot be skipped, and that some tempting constructions are classically obstructed;
- from Markov and Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov, that de Sitter geometry is a natural regular-interior candidate, and that a fundamental curvature bound is one of the oldest and most literal ways to propose one;
- from Smolin and Popławski, that parent and child cosmologies may form a causal genealogy, by two structurally different mechanisms;
- from 't Hooft and Susskind, that infalling and exterior observers can require complementary descriptions;
- from Gott and Li, that closing a causal loop back on the universe's own origin is a live (if exotic) alternative to positing an external first cause;
- from Penrose, that reciprocal conformal quantities can relate opposite cosmic limits;
- from Haggard–Rovelli and Ashtekar–Olmedo–Singh, that black-to-white-hole transition geometries can be made explicit in quantum-gravity-inspired effective models;
- from Gaztañaga and collaborators, that a finite FLRW universe, a Schwarzschild radius, and the observed cosmological scale may belong to one construction, complete with an explicit formation history.

CSBHI's distinctive move is the reciprocal relation

\[
a(\tau)=\frac1{r(\tau)}.
\]

From this ansatz and Alice's specified spatial geometry, GD1 obtains an isotropically expanding probe congruence, preserves the characteristic matter and radiation powers, maps the Friedmann background into an infall function, defers Bob's \(r=0\) endpoint to infinite Alice proper time, and reaches an exact de Sitter limit.

The historical comparison also sharpens what CSBHI has not yet done. Several developed proposals supply a formation mechanism, an effective singularity-resolution mechanism, or an explicit junction calculation. GD2 must construct the horizon map, determine its junction or higher-dimensional data, and show why the reciprocal relation follows from the completed geometry. Until then, CSBHI is a developed interior proposal rather than a complete parent-child spacetime.
