# Black-Hole Cosmology: Lineage, Mechanisms, and CSBHI

## Scope

"The universe is inside a black hole" is not one theory. It is a family of proposals that share an image while assigning very different meanings to the horizon, the singularity, the interior geometry, and the birth of cosmic expansion.

This note separates four lines of thought:

1. **containment:** a cosmological region is matched to a black-hole exterior;
2. **regularization:** the singular interior is replaced by a de Sitter core, bounce, or child universe;
3. **reproduction:** black holes generate new universes and possibly an evolving population of them;
4. **conformal or observer duality:** apparently incompatible descriptions are related by a change of scale or causal standpoint.

CSBHI touches all four lines, but belongs completely to none of them. Its defining relation is

\[
a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)},
\]

where \(r\) is the horizon-normalized radius Bob assigns to Alice's infall and \(a\) is the spatial scale of Alice's proposed interior geometry.

---

## 1. Who was first?

The answer depends on what counts as the beginning.

### The mathematical precursor: Oppenheimer and Snyder, 1939

Oppenheimer and Snyder gave the first exact relativistic model of a homogeneous dust sphere collapsing through its gravitational radius. The exterior is Schwarzschild; the matter-filled interior is a contracting FLRW geometry.

They did not propose that the interior was a new universe. Their result established the mathematical fact on which later black-hole cosmologies depend: one spacetime can look like stellar collapse from outside while its material interior is described by a cosmological metric.

### The first explicit proposal in print: I. J. Good, July 1972

In the one-page Physics Today letter *Chinese Universes*, I. J. Good argued that the observable universe was probably a black hole inside a larger universe. He imagined an infinite nesting of black and white holes, "like carved Chinese spheres," and interpreted the Big Bang as the transition of a massive galaxy into a black or white hole within a parent universe.

Good's construction was qualitative and tied to steady-state ideas, continual creation, and a speculative negative-density medium. It supplied a vivid nested-universe picture rather than a junction calculation.

### The first canonical GR construction: R. K. Pathria, December 1972

Pathria's *The Universe as a Black Hole* was received by Nature on 19 June 1972 and published on 1 December. He identified a closed, pressureless Friedmann universe at maximum expansion with the interior of a Schwarzschild black hole whose horizon has the same radius.

Good therefore appears to have publication priority for the explicit proposal, while Pathria supplied the first compact mathematical model and became the standard historical citation. The two contributions were effectively contemporaneous and logically independent.

Among the sources examined for this note, the fairest summary is:

> Oppenheimer and Snyder supplied the geometry; Good first published the nested-universe claim; Pathria first turned the claim into a recognizable black-hole cosmology calculation.

## 2. Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse: cosmology inside a star

The Oppenheimer–Snyder model joins

\[
\text{contracting FLRW dust interior}
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{Schwarzschild vacuum exterior}.
\]

Its central claim is not that a universe is born. It is that homogeneous pressureless matter can cross its Schwarzschild radius and continue collapsing in finite comoving proper time even though the exterior observer sees extreme redshift and asymptotic delay.

This is important to CSBHI for two reasons:

- it is the earliest exact example of cosmological geometry inside a collapsing body;
- it cleanly separates the infaller's proper time from the exterior Schwarzschild time.

Its limitation is equally clear: classical collapse ends at a curvature singularity. It does not provide isotropic expansion after horizon crossing, a reciprocal scale, or a nonsingular future.

## 3. Good and Pathria: two origins of black-hole cosmology

Good and Pathria asked different versions of the same question.

### Good's nested hierarchy

Good proposed that universes and black holes form an infinite hierarchy. A black hole in one universe can be a universe from within, while the containing universe may itself occupy a larger white-hole structure.

**Central claim:** the Big Bang was a black- or white-hole-forming event inside a larger universe.

**Mechanism:** continual creation and a nested steady-state hierarchy.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Good supplied no interior metric, reciprocal relation, or horizon map. His contribution is the parent-child ontology.

### Pathria's horizon equality

Pathria compared a closed Friedmann universe at maximum expansion with a Schwarzschild black hole and identified the maximum cosmic radius with the event-horizon radius.

**Central claim:** a closed universe can be confined within a Schwarzschild black hole.

**Mechanism:** equality of characteristic radii at the moment of maximum expansion.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Pathria's universe reaches a maximum size and recollapses. CSBHI instead maps decreasing exterior radius to increasing interior scale and approaches a de Sitter future.

A later null-shell calculation by Khakshournia found that Pathria's matching hypersurface carries surface pressure. This is a decisive lesson for GD2: equality of radii does not by itself establish a smooth junction.

## 4. False-vacuum bubbles and manufactured child universes

In the 1980s, black-hole cosmology became a problem in vacuum decay and junction dynamics.

Blau, Guendelman, and Guth studied false-vacuum bubbles separated from an exterior region by a thin wall. A sufficiently large inflating interior can become causally separated behind a wormhole or black-hole geometry. Farhi and Guth then showed that, under ordinary classical energy conditions, producing such an inflating universe from nonsingular asymptotically flat initial data encounters an initial-singularity obstruction. Farhi, Guth, and Guven investigated quantum tunnelling as a possible route around it.

**Central claim:** an inflating child universe may branch from a parent spacetime through false-vacuum bubble dynamics.

**Mechanism:** a vacuum-energy bubble, thin-shell junction, wormhole geometry, and possibly quantum tunnelling.

**Difference from CSBHI:** the child universe is nucleated as a new inflating region. CSBHI does not introduce a false-vacuum bubble or require a branching event; it follows one infalling history and assigns its reciprocal spatial description.

This literature is especially relevant to the horizon-map problem because it shows how much work hides inside the word "connects": the shell trajectory, energy conditions, global causal diagram, and junction stress tensor all matter.

## 5. Frolov, Markov, and Mukhanov: limiting curvature and a de Sitter core

Frolov, Markov, and Mukhanov assumed that quantum gravity imposes a maximum curvature. They attached the Schwarzschild interior to a de Sitter region across a short spacelike transition layer and explored whether the resulting region could continue into a new universe.

**Central claim:** limiting curvature can replace the Schwarzschild singularity with a regular de Sitter interior.

**Mechanism:** a high-curvature transition from Schwarzschild to de Sitter geometry.

**Difference from CSBHI:** their de Sitter core is inserted when curvature reaches a new fundamental bound. In CSBHI, de Sitter geometry is the late-time limit of the matter–radiation–\(\Lambda\) reciprocal interior.

This is one of the closest precedents for CSBHI's asymptotic endpoint, but the direction is different: Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov regularize the deep interior at high curvature; CSBHI sends Bob's \(r=0\) to Alice's infinite future with finite limiting curvature.

## 6. Smolin: cosmological natural selection

Lee Smolin turned child-universe formation into a theory of why the constants of nature take their observed values. He proposed that black-hole singularities bounce or tunnel into new expanding universes and that dimensionless parameters change slightly during reproduction. Universes that produce more black holes then have more descendants.

**Central claim:** the parameters of physics may be shaped by Darwinian selection for black-hole production.

**Mechanism:** quantum-gravity resolution of black-hole singularities, reproduction, and small parameter mutations.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Smolin's central result is population-level selection, not the metric experienced inside one black hole. The child-universe bounce is an assumed reproductive mechanism rather than a reciprocal infall-expansion map.

CSBHI could eventually supply geometric content to the reproduction step, but cosmological natural selection neither predicts nor requires \(a=1/r\).

## 7. Easson and Brandenberger: the cosmological payoff

Easson and Brandenberger deliberately worked above the level of any single formation mechanism. They argued that a universe born inside a black hole could naturally alter the horizon, flatness, structure-formation, and information-loss problems normally assigned to the standard Big Bang and inflation.

**Central claim:** black-hole origin scenarios may resolve several cosmological initial-condition problems without a long conventional inflationary phase.

**Mechanism:** intentionally model-independent; the paper identifies consequences shared by a class of scenarios.

**Difference from CSBHI:** their work is a programme-level argument rather than a specific geometry. CSBHI proposes the reciprocal geometry whose explanatory consequences can be tested one at a time.

## 8. Penrose: conformal cyclic cosmology

Penrose's Conformal Cyclic Cosmology is not a black-hole cosmology. It belongs in this comparison because it uses a reciprocal conformal construction to connect apparently opposite cosmic limits.

CCC joins the remote future of one cosmic aeon to the Big Bang of the next. Once rest mass has disappeared and only conformally invariant physics remains, absolute length and time scales lose operational meaning. The exponentially expanded future can then be conformally compressed into the next aeon's beginning.

If \(\hat g\) and \(\check g\) are the physical metrics of consecutive aeons and \(g\) is a bridging conformal metric, CCC writes

\[
\hat g_{ab}=\hat\Omega^2g_{ab},
\qquad
\check g_{ab}=\check\Omega^2g_{ab},
\]

with Penrose's reciprocal hypothesis relating the two conformal factors across the crossover.

**Central claim:** the conformally smooth infinite future of one universe is the conformally stretched Big Bang of the next.

**Mechanism:** conformal completion, loss of massive scales, the Weyl-curvature hypothesis, and a reciprocal relation between consecutive aeons.

### Why Penrose's scaling is not CSBHI's scaling

Penrose rescales complete spacetime metrics on opposite sides of a spacelike crossover between aeons. The conformal factor removes absolute scale while preserving causal structure.

CSBHI instead relates two physical quantities along one black-hole history:

\[
\text{Bob's decreasing infall radius }r(\tau)
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{Alice's increasing spatial scale }a(\tau)=1/r(\tau).
\]

CSBHI does not claim that a scalar Weyl rescaling turns the Schwarzschild metric into FLRW; their Weyl curvature and anisotropy differ. It proposes a physical horizon map whose construction remains open. Nor does CSBHI require the evaporation of every black hole, the disappearance of rest mass, or a succession of aeons.

The resemblance is real but limited: both theories discover that an infinite large-scale limit can be represented through a reciprocal conformal quantity. They apply that insight to different geometries, different boundaries, and different physical problems.

## 9. 't Hooft: complementarity and conformal observer descriptions

Gerard 't Hooft is also adjacent to black-hole cosmology rather than a direct member of it. In *Quantum Gravity without Space-time Singularities or Horizons*, he combines black-hole complementarity with exact scale invariance. An outside observer and an infalling observer give descriptions that appear fundamentally different; 't Hooft argues that the difference can be expressed through conformal transformations within an enlarged symmetry of quantum gravity.

**Central claim:** exact local scale symmetry and black-hole complementarity may remove horizons and singularities from the fundamental description while preserving causality and locality.

**Mechanism:** conformal symmetry, observer complementarity, and quantum information.

**Difference from CSBHI:** 't Hooft does not construct an FLRW universe inside a black hole or propose the radius-scale relation \(a=1/r\). His work is nevertheless the closest conceptual precedent for CSBHI's Alice/Bob logic: exterior and infalling accounts can be radically different without either being dismissed as meaningless.

Penrose is the closer algebraic cousin because his conformal factors obey a reciprocal hypothesis. 't Hooft is the closer interpretive cousin because his conformal map is explicitly tied to the disagreement between exterior and infalling observers.

## 10. Popławski: torsion and the big bounce

Nikodem Popławski develops black-hole cosmology in Einstein–Cartan gravity. Fermion spin sources spacetime torsion. At extreme density, the spin-torsion contribution becomes gravitationally repulsive, prevents a singularity, and produces a nonsingular bounce into a closed expanding universe. Particle production can drive an inflationary phase and increase the child universe's mass and entropy.

**Central claim:** every black hole containing fermionic matter can generate a nonsingular child universe beyond its event horizon.

**Mechanism:** Einstein–Cartan torsion, spin-fluid repulsion, a minimum scale factor, and a bounce.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Popławski modifies the connection and reverses contraction at finite minimum size. CSBHI uses the standard matter–radiation–\(\Lambda\) background on Alice's side, introduces no torsion, and defers the endpoint asymptotically rather than bouncing through it.

The two programmes agree that an external black-hole description and an internal cosmological description can coexist. They disagree on the physics that prevents the classical singularity.

## 11. Gaztañaga: a finite FLRW cloud inside its Schwarzschild radius

Enrique Gaztañaga's Black Hole Universe programme is the closest recent direct comparison. It treats a finite FLRW cloud surrounded by vacuum, calculates its mass and junction, and asks whether its radius lies within its Schwarzschild radius. The proposal identifies the Schwarzschild radius with the late-time horizon scale and argues that this boundary can reproduce apparent cosmic acceleration without independent dark energy. Related work describes the Big Bang as a bounce following the collapse of a parent cloud.

**Central claim:** the observed universe is a finite FLRW region inside its own Schwarzschild radius, with the black-hole horizon supplying the effective cosmological scale.

**Mechanism:** a bounded FLRW cloud, Schwarzschild exterior, junction conditions, and in the formation picture a collapse-and-bounce history.

**Difference from CSBHI:** Gaztañaga begins with a finite FLRW sphere and tests whether it is a black hole. CSBHI begins with the observer contrast along infall and introduces the reciprocal scale \(a=1/r\). Gaztañaga attributes expansion/static duality to a frame transformation; CSBHI treats the Schwarzschild-to-FLRW horizon map as a still-unconstructed change of geometry.

The overlap is substantial: finite causal extent, parent collapse, horizon-scale \(\Lambda\), and an FLRW interior. This makes the Black Hole Universe papers essential comparison literature for GD2.

## 13. What CSBHI inherits and what it adds

CSBHI inherits several durable insights:

- from Oppenheimer–Snyder, that FLRW geometry naturally appears inside spherical collapse;
- from Good and Pathria, that a universe may be globally associated with a black hole;
- from the baby-universe literature, that junction conditions and causal structure cannot be skipped;
- from Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov, that de Sitter geometry is a natural regular interior candidate;
- from Smolin and Popławski, that parent and child cosmologies may form a causal genealogy;
- from Penrose, that reciprocal conformal quantities can relate opposite cosmic limits;
- from 't Hooft, that infalling and exterior observers may require physically different descriptions;
- from Gaztañaga, that a finite FLRW universe, a Schwarzschild radius, and the observed cosmological scale may belong to one construction.

Its distinctive move is narrower and more explicit:

\[
a(\tau)=\frac1{r(\tau)}.
\]

From this ansatz and Alice's specified spatial geometry, GD1 obtains an isotropically expanding probe congruence, preserves the characteristic matter and radiation powers, maps the Friedmann background into an infall function, defers Bob's \(r=0\) endpoint to infinite Alice proper time, and reaches an exact de Sitter limit.

The historical comparison also sharpens what CSBHI has not yet done. GD2 must construct the horizon map, determine its junction or higher-dimensional data, and show why the reciprocal relation follows from the completed geometry. Until then, CSBHI is a developed interior proposal rather than a complete parent-child spacetime.
