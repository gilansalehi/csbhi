# The Weyl Curvature Hypothesis as a Fixed Point

*The low-Weyl Big Bang is not a fine-tuned initial condition but a consequence of how black holes form. A universe born from the smooth collapse of a broad overdensity inherits a Weyl-free beginning; and because smooth beginnings are what grow the smooth overdensities that collapse into the next universe, low initial Weyl is a fixed point of the succession rather than a first-moment selection. Read this way, the entropy arrow follows without destroying information.*

---

## Introduction

Penrose's Weyl Curvature Hypothesis is the observation that the Big Bang was gravitationally special: the Weyl tensor was near zero there while the Ricci part was large. Matter was hot and dense, but the gravitational field was maximally un-excited — smooth, homogeneous, low in gravitational entropy. The future is the reverse: matter clumps, and Weyl grows without bound toward black-hole singularities. The arrow of time is the growth of Weyl, and the near-zero Weyl of the Big Bang is the low-entropy past that lets the arrow exist at all.

The difficulty is the specialness. Among initial conditions, low-Weyl states are extraordinarily rare — Penrose's estimate is one part in \(10^{10^{123}}\). The hypothesis states the condition; it does not say why the condition holds. Inflation does not close the gap, on Penrose's own reading, because inflationary smoothing presupposes an already-smooth patch to work on: it propagates the special condition rather than producing it.

Recursive black-hole cosmology supplies the missing ingredient — a reason the low-Weyl condition is generic rather than tuned. A child universe is born from the smooth collapse of a broad parent overdensity, so its low-Weyl start is a property of how it formed, inherited along the lineage rather than selected at a first moment.

## 1. A smooth beginning from smooth collapse

The mechanism is a single fact about collapse. The Weyl tensor of a homogeneous dust interior vanishes identically: Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse has an exactly FLRW interior, \(\Psi_2\equiv0\), even as its density diverges. A broad, low-amplitude overdensity — a compensated curvature peak, isolated from the surrounding expansion in the Einstein–Straus manner — collapses to an interior that is smooth on large scales. The child born from that collapse inherits low Weyl at its beginning, carrying the overdensity's residual small-scale structure as its primordial seed. This is the Weyl Curvature Hypothesis condition, produced by the collapse rather than imposed on it.

The birth is at the parent's finite collapse epoch — the moment its black-hole region forms — not at any future infinity. Cosmic no-hair plays a supporting part: Wald's theorem, for homogeneous Bianchi models with \(\Lambda>0\), ensures the expanding background between overdensities dilutes toward de Sitter, which isolates the collapsing region so the child forms as a clean patch. It is not itself the source of the child's low Weyl — that is the collapse smoothness — but it keeps the neighborhood from contaminating the newborn.

## 2. Low Weyl is a fixed point

The step that turns a mechanism into an explanation is that the same smoothness recurs. Small perturbations are broad, shallow overdensities; broad shallow overdensities collapse to low-Weyl interiors; low-Weyl interiors are smooth children that in turn grow only small perturbations. Low initial Weyl and a small primordial amplitude are not two conditions but one, and that one condition reproduces itself down the lineage.

This is the sense in which the hypothesis stops being a selection. A tuned initial condition is a number chosen once, against odds; a fixed point is a condition a process returns to because producing it is what the process does. That the succession has such a fixed point — that the parent-to-child map converges on the low-Weyl, small-amplitude condition rather than quietly relocating the tuning into the map itself — is the load-bearing conjecture here, and it is not yet proved. What can be said now is that the two ends the fixed point must join, a smooth birth and a small seed, are the same quantity, so there is one condition to converge on rather than two.

## 3. The arrow, and the entropy, without information loss

Penrose's arrow is the growth of Weyl, and the hardest part of his construction is the reset: how the next aeon begins at low entropy when the previous one ends at enormous entropy. His answer is that information is destroyed at black-hole singularities, removing entropy from the phase space — the element of his picture most often rejected, since it breaks unitarity.

The recursive reading offers a reset that destroys nothing, by keeping two entropies apart. One is how excited the gravitational field currently is — the Weyl, or occupied, entropy — which is low at every birth because each child forms from smooth collapse. The other is the ceiling: the largest entropy the universe can eventually reach, its horizon budget. That ceiling is set by the parent. With the idealized matching \(\Lambda=3/R_S^2\), the child's de Sitter horizon has the same radius as the parent's Schwarzschild horizon, and the two horizon entropies coincide exactly,

\[
S_{\rm dS}(\text{child}) = \frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\,\ell_p^2} = \frac{\pi R_S^2}{\ell_p^2} = S_{\rm BH}(\text{parent}).
\]

The parent's horizon entropy is not erased; it becomes the child's available phase space. Each newborn universe therefore sits far below its own ceiling — low occupied entropy, high available entropy — which is itself a thermodynamic arrow, while globally no entropy is destroyed and no information is lost. The reset Penrose reaches by discarding information is reached here by handing it forward.

Two caveats travel with this. The horizon identity holds at the idealized matching \(\Lambda=3/R_S^2\); the junction of transverse curvature across the crossover is not yet settled. And "the parent's horizon entropy becomes the child's phase space" is a reading of the two coincident areas, not a derived transport of microstates — the interpretation that has to be justified, not a theorem already in hand.

## 4. What this removes from Penrose's construction

Read this way, three commitments Conformal Cyclic Cosmology is pressed on become unnecessary.

| Conformal Cyclic Cosmology requires | The recursive interior replaces it with |
|---|---|
| all black holes evaporate before the boundary | black holes *are* the crossover cells — no evaporation required |
| information destroyed at singularities | information passes into the child — sequestered, not erased |
| rest mass decays to zero for conformal invariance | massive matter falls behind horizons, into children — no free mass at the boundary |
| a reciprocal factor \(\Omega\to1/\Omega\) posited at the boundary | \(a=1/r\), a geometric relation between infall radius and interior scale — a candidate physical origin for the same inversion |

The last line is the one to state carefully. \(a=1/r\) is CSBHI's ansatz, at the same epistemic standing as Penrose's \(\Omega\to1/\Omega\): posited, not derived. The difference is in kind. His inversion is imposed at an abstract conformal boundary; \(a=1/r\) is a relation between two physical scales — the infalling areal radius and the interior scale factor — constructed from the infall geometry in [Geometric Duality I](gd1.html). It is a candidate physical origin for the crossover, to be tested, not a theorem.

## 5. What remains open

The framework is answerable, and it answers through a calculation it has not yet performed. Its observational content — that our large-scale smoothness is the smoothness of the overdensity we collapsed from, and our primordial fluctuations its residual small-scale structure — turns on transferring the collapsing overdensity's residual Weyl into the child's curvature perturbation. A local Weyl scalar is not the curvature perturbation \(\zeta\) without a normalization across the collapse surface, and until that transfer is carried out on a concrete collapse benchmark and tested against the observed amplitude \(\sim10^{-5}\) and tilt \(n_s\simeq0.965\), the low-Weyl claim and the seed amplitude remain one unperformed calculation. That number, not the narrative, is what will decide whether this is a result.

Three further assumptions are load-bearing, and are best stated as such. The succession needs a "smooth-or-collapse" dichotomy for regions — weaker than the full inhomogeneous no-hair conjecture, but an assumption rather than a theorem. The entropy exchange of §3 needs a phase-space statement behind the coincidence of areas. And the fixed point of §2 needs its convergence shown rather than assumed. None of these is closed; each is a definite next target rather than a vague worry.

## 6. Result

> Read through recursive black-hole cosmology, the Weyl Curvature Hypothesis is a consequence of how black holes form: a child is born at its parent's finite collapse epoch, from a broad smooth overdensity whose homogeneous collapse gives a Weyl-free interior (\(\Psi_2\equiv0\)), so its beginning is smooth without fine-tuning. Because a smoothly-born universe is what grows the smooth overdensities that collapse into the next, low initial Weyl and a small primordial amplitude are a single fixed-point condition of the succession. The entropy arrow follows without destroying information: the parent's horizon entropy becomes the child's available phase space, so each universe begins far below its ceiling while nothing is erased. The reading removes Conformal Cyclic Cosmology's contested moves — no mandatory evaporation, no information loss, no imposed mass-decay — and offers \(a=1/r\) as a candidate physical origin for the reciprocal crossover. What it does not yet have is the number: the low-Weyl beginning and the seed amplitude reduce to one calculation, the Weyl-to-\(\zeta\) transfer across the collapse surface, and that is what will decide it.
