*A living index of open questions for CSBHI*

**Author:** Codex (OpenAI).

**Updated:** 2026-08-09.

## Purpose

A part of the theory is mathematically legible when its objects are defined, its assumptions are visible, and its equations determine what happens next. An unanswered question is not automatically illegible. Illegibility begins where the theory still lacks the geometric object, dynamical law, boundary condition, or observable needed to make a calculation unique.

CSBHI now has a legible conditional interior. Given

\[
a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)},
\]

Alice's spatially flat isotropic metric, Einstein's equations, and conserved matter, radiation, and vacuum components produce a definite expansion history and its reciprocal infall law. The open problem is no longer a direct Schwarzschild–FLRW junction. CSBHI does not paste two independently chosen four-metrics across a zero-width surface. It must derive one collapse-to-child correspondence that contains GD I's reciprocal geometry.

## I. The parent-to-child geometry

### Q1. What selects the reciprocal correspondence?

**Known:** Once the parent–child exchange is required to be horizon-fixing, involutive, and scale-free within the power-law class, the nontrivial exponent is fixed: (a=1/r).

**Missing:** A derivation of those three properties from collapse geometry. The theorem fixes the reciprocal power; it does not prove that nature must realize the exchange.

**Closure test:** Derive the reciprocal relation from a regular parent-collapse solution or a deeper variational principle, rather than inserting it as a boundary rule.

### Q2. What is the collapse-cohort event map?

**Known:** Repeating the GD I experiment at successive parent epochs separates the construction into three pieces:

1. a candidate null map from the parent event-horizon history into a child causal history;
2. an enclosed-mass shell map that preserves homogeneous spherical ordering; and
3. the reciprocal evolution of every cohort after crossing.

The exact boundary reduction leaves one synchronization lapse between parent collapse time and child proper time. A compensated (\Lambda\)LTB benchmark reproduces the formation and de Sitter endpoint powers but rejects the simplest unit-lapse clock in the intermediate era.

**Missing:** The nontrivial lapse selected by the matter-filled collapse, together with the conformal normalization of the mapped angular geometry.

**Closure test:** Integrate the parent event horizon and optical transfer through a realistic collapse, derive the lapse without fitting the child expansion history, and recover one monotone causal map across the full formation history.

### Q3. Does the map extend to a regular global spacetime?

**Known:** GD I is regular along Alice's reciprocal worldline and approaches finite de Sitter curvature at infinite proper time. Homogeneous shell ordering can map an entire finite spherical family with one conformal factor.

**Missing:** A four-metric—or a demonstrably necessary higher-dimensional completion—covering neighboring timelike and null curves while preserving the reciprocal interior, causal one-wayness, and a smooth crossing.

**Closure test:** Construct the metric, calculate its curvature invariants and characteristic surfaces, rule out shell crossings and impulsive forces, and establish geodesic completeness or identify the remaining boundary precisely.

### Q4. How does parent inhomogeneity become the child background?

**Known:** In spherical symmetry the Weyl scalar is controlled by the density contrast between local and enclosed-average density. Homogeneous collapse has zero background Weyl, while departures from the common shell profile carry perturbative information.

**Missing:** The transfer law taking realistic parent density, velocity, and Weyl perturbations into child scalar, vector, and tensor modes.

**Closure test:** Derive the gauge-invariant perturbation matching and calculate the primordial spectrum, CMB transfer functions, matter power spectrum, lensing, and gravitational-wave signatures.

## II. Completing the cosmology

### Q5. What fixes the absolute reciprocal normalization?

**Known:** Standard cosmology measures scale-factor ratios. CSBHI makes (r=a=1) at horizon crossing a physical candidate anchor.

**Missing:** The relation between that crossing, the hot beginning, and today's normalization.

**Closure test:** Obtain (a_0), the corresponding parent data, and the parent-mass/child-(\Lambda) relation from the collapse map without choosing a thermal or horizon landmark afterward.

### Q6. Where do the child fluids and thermal history come from?

**Known:** Once matter, radiation, and vacuum components are specified, their equations of state and dilution laws follow from conservation.

**Missing:** Their production, initial abundances, entropy, baryon asymmetry, and near-thermal radiation state.

**Closure test:** Recover inflation or an alternative causal mechanism, reheating, nucleosynthesis, neutrino decoupling, recombination, and the acoustic scale with one consistent parameter set.

### Q7. Does geometry determine the observed vacuum scale?

**Known:** The observed (\Lambda) is naturally expressed as a de Sitter horizon curvature, and the Planck-to-cosmic hierarchy becomes a ratio of geometric lengths.

**Missing:** Why quantum vacuum contributions do not shift that curvature and whether the parent collapse fixes its value.

**Closure test:** Derive the effective vacuum term and show how radiative corrections preserve, cancel, or fail to gravitate against it.

### Q8. Is the construction stable beyond spherical symmetry?

**Known:** The developed background calculations use spherical symmetry and nonrotating parents.

**Missing:** Rotation, charge, accretion, mergers, nonspherical collapse, and nonlinear stability.

**Closure test:** Extend the construction to Kerr-like parents and perturb it without restoring a curvature singularity, Cauchy horizon, or mass-inflation instability.

## III. Predictions and later consequences

### Q9. Does the galaxy acceleration scale evolve?

**Known:** A scale-free self-dual mixing law selects the square-root form

\[
g_{\rm mix}=\kappa\sqrt{g_N A}.
\]

Identifying (A) with the contemporaneous horizon acceleration gives the coefficient-independent prediction

\[
\frac{g_\dagger(z)}{g_\dagger(0)}=\frac{H(z)}{H_0},
\]

which rises with redshift where a fixed scale remains flat. The same H-linked functional law has prior MOND precedent; CSBHI supplies an independent reciprocal-horizon motivation. Existing intermediate-redshift evidence is compatible with the rise but does not decide it.

**Missing:** A dynamical reason galaxies couple to the apparent-horizon scale rather than the asymptotic de Sitter scale, plus the normalization and relativistic completion.

**Closure test:** Derive the weak-field response from the completed geometry and test the preregistered high-redshift baryonic Tully–Fisher offset, rotation curves, lensing, and clusters.

### Q10. What observation uniquely distinguishes CSBHI?

**Known:** The homogeneous background deliberately reproduces much of the successful matter–radiation–(\Lambda) history. The evolving (g_\dagger) relation is the first stated discriminator.

**Missing:** Independent signatures in perturbations, horizon-scale correlations, black-hole/cosmology relations, or gravitational waves that cannot be absorbed by initial conditions or conventional feedback models.

**Closure test:** State quantitative predictions before fitting the relevant data.

### Q11. What becomes of gravitational entropy and information?

**Known:** The idealized parent black-hole and child de Sitter horizons share an area-entropy identity when their radii are identified. The low-Weyl child background supplies the correct direction for Penrose's gravitational arrow.

**Missing:** The microscopic information map, the entropy carried by perturbations, and compatibility with unitary black-hole evolution.

**Closure test:** Derive the entropy and information transfer from the same degrees of freedom that generate the parent-to-child geometry.
