# Geometric Duality II: The Horizon Triple Identity

## Abstract

GD1 proposes the reciprocal relation \(a=1/r\) between Bob's horizon-normalized infall coordinate and Alice's spatial scale. Within Alice's geometry, this relation maps the complete matter-radiation-\(\Lambda\) Friedmann background into the infall function, defers Bob's \(r=0\) endpoint to infinite Alice proper time, and approaches an exact de Sitter interior. The de Sitter limit admits the standard 5D Minkowski embedding and, in Alice's areal radius \(\bar R=aR\), the exact PG flow \(F_{\rm dS}=H_\Lambda\bar R\).

GD2 addresses the remaining load-bearing problem: construct the horizon map connecting Bob's exterior geometry to Alice's isotropic interior. It tests whether the de Sitter curvature scale \(L\), the causal horizon \(\mathcal H\), and the resulting matching history \(\Sigma\) are different presentations of one throat geometry.

---

## 1. Purpose of GD2

GD2 is the geometric completion paper. Its theorem target is

\[
L \leftrightarrow \mathcal H \leftrightarrow \Sigma.
\]

The symbols name different kinds of objects:

- \(L\): an invariant curvature scale;
- \(\mathcal H\): a null causal boundary;
- \(\Sigma\): the candidate matching structure or history produced by the completed map.

The paper must determine whether these distinctions arise from one common construction without treating them as literally interchangeable.

---

GD2 should preserve GD1's notation:

## 3. Results inherited from GD1

- \(H_{\rm A}=\dot a/a=F(r)/r\).
- The probe congruence expands isotropically with \(\Theta=3H_{\rm A}\), zero shear, and zero vorticity.
- Matter and radiation dilute as \(a^{-3}\) and \(a^{-4}\).
- The matter-radiation-\(\Lambda\) Friedmann history maps into the infall function.
- With \(\Lambda>0\), Bob's \(r=0\) lies at infinite Alice proper time with finite limiting curvature.
- The pure-\(\Lambda\) limit is the standard de Sitter flat patch.
- In Alice's areal radius, that de Sitter patch has PG flow \(F_{\rm dS}=H_\Lambda\bar R\).
- In pure de Sitter, the curvature and causal-horizon scales satisfy \(L=c/H_\Lambda\).

### Proposed identification

- Bob's radial history supplies Alice's inverse spatial scale through \(a=1/r\).
- Alice physically experiences the conformally flat interior geometry specified in GD1.

### Not yet established

- A matching surface with solved stress-energy and stability conditions.
- The triple identity beyond the pure de Sitter equality of scales.

## 4. The three-track construction

GD2 should synthesize three tracks without conflating them.

### Causal track

Alice crosses the horizon in finite proper time and experiences no local singularity there. Bob's exterior description is horizon-limited. This observer asymmetry motivates the search for two descriptions of one global history.

### Intrinsic geometric track

Alice's proposed interior is spatially flat FLRW. In the pure de Sitter limit, defining her areal radius

\[
\bar R=a(\tau)R
\]

gives

\[
ds^2
=-d\tau^2
+\left(d\bar R-H_\Lambda\bar R\,d\tau\right)^2
+\bar R^2d\Omega^2.
\]

This is an exact rewriting of the intrinsic de Sitter geometry.

### Embedding track

The same pure de Sitter geometry is the hyperboloid

\[
-X_0^2+X_1^2+X_2^2+X_3^2+X_4^2=L^2
\]

in 5D Minkowski space. Its flat expansion is a Lorentz boost in the embedding plane. This supplies a standard geometric origin for the asymptotic PG flow, but not yet for the full dust-radiation history or the parent-child horizon map.

## 5. The horizon-map problem

GD2 must construct

\[
(\mathcal M_B,g_B,r)
\xrightarrow{\;\mathcal D_{\rm GD}\;}
(\mathcal M_A,g_A,\tau,a).
\]

An ordinary coordinate transformation or scalar conformal rescaling cannot complete the map: the vacuum Schwarzschild interior has nonzero Weyl curvature and anisotropic spatial evolution, whereas the FLRW interior is conformally flat and isotropic.

The principal construction routes are:

1. a null junction in horizon-regular coordinates;
2. a timelike transition layer away from the parent black-hole event horizon;
3. two 4D projections or slicings of a common higher-dimensional throat.

The calculation must determine the junction data rather than assume a stress-free match.

---

## 6. Curvature scale, causal horizon, and matching history

### Curvature scale \(L\)

\[
L=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\Lambda}}.
\]

This is the invariant curvature radius of the de Sitter limit.

### Causal horizon \(\mathcal H\)

In pure de Sitter, every geodesic observer sees a horizon of radius

\[
R_{\mathcal H}=\frac{c}{H_\Lambda}=L.
\]

The equality is a consequence of maximal symmetry; it does not identify a null surface with a curvature scalar.

### Matching history \(\Sigma\)

\(\Sigma\) denotes whatever geometric history the horizon-map construction produces. If it is timelike, it cannot literally be the null horizon. If both structures occur, GD2 must derive how they track one distinguished scale.

---

## 7. The triple-identity hypothesis

### Weak statement

The pure de Sitter curvature radius and observer-horizon radius coincide. A completed GD construction may select its matching data using this same distinguished scale.

### Strong statement

> Curvature, causal access, and the matching history are different presentations of one throat geometry.

The weak statement is established only for \(L=R_{\mathcal H}\) in pure de Sitter. The strong statement is the result GD2 must earn.

---

## 8. Success criteria

GD2 succeeds only if it provides:

1. a common geometric construction or valid junction;
2. the induced metric and appropriate junction data;
3. the stress-energy content of any transition structure;
4. a derivation, rather than placement, of the distinguished scale;
5. a clear relation between the null horizon and any timelike history;
6. a controlled limit recovering the exact de Sitter embedding and PG flow;
7. a perturbative or stability programme precise enough to continue after the background solution.

---

## 9. Handoff to GD3

If the horizon map establishes one throat scale with geometric, causal, and dynamical faces, GD3 can ask how local baryonic curvature couples to that global scale. The target weak-field relation is

\[
g_{\rm mix}\sim\sqrt{g_NcH}.
\]

GD2 must supply the geometry that makes the two inputs belong to one system; GD3 must supply the dynamics that mix them.

---

## 10. Conclusion

GD1 constructs the reciprocal interior and identifies its exact de Sitter endpoint. GD2 must now construct the missing bridge across the horizon. The Horizon Triple Identity is not a relabeling of three known objects. It is the proposal that a successful horizon map will derive all three from one throat geometry.
