# Horizon Thermodynamics and the GD Interpretation

## Abstract

This future paper will connect Geometric Duality to horizon thermodynamics, emergent-gravity arguments, and the galaxy-scale clues identified by Milgrom, McGaugh, Verlinde, Padmanabhan, and others. The purpose is not to frame these approaches as competitors to GD. The purpose is to ask whether they are partial recognitions of one throat-centered geometry.

The guiding thesis is:

> MOND phenomenology, emergent gravity, de Sitter horizon thermodynamics, and black-hole/cosmology duality may be different projections of one underlying horizon-centered spacetime structure.

---

## 1. Function of the Paper

This paper is not part of the immediate GD trilogy unless the program expands beyond GD3.

Its role would be synthetic:

- connect GD to horizon entropy,
- explain why de Sitter horizons matter for local dynamics,
- relate GD to emergent-gravity and holographic arguments,
- and clarify how thermodynamic gravity might be the coarse-grained face of throat geometry.

It should be written after GD2 and GD3 have established the geometric and galaxy-dynamical sides of the project.

---

## 2. Core Thesis

GD should not be presented as anti-MOND, anti-Verlinde, or anti-Padmanabhan.

A stronger framing is:

> GD supplies the geometric unification layer explaining why several independent approaches kept finding the same horizon-scale clues.

In this view:

- Milgrom identified the acceleration-scale phenomenology;
- McGaugh mapped the empirical galaxy law;
- Verlinde emphasized the de Sitter entropy/horizon connection;
- Padmanabhan emphasized holographic/equipartition control of cosmic dynamics;
- GD proposes the underlying throat geometry tying these clues together.

---

The paper should organize the landscape around recurring clues:

| Clue | Source / tradition | GD interpretation |

---

## 4. Relation to Verlinde

Verlinde-style emergent gravity ties galaxy-scale anomalies to the de Sitter horizon and its entropy structure.

GD should treat this as a clue, not a rival.

The GD interpretation is:

> the horizon matters thermodynamically because it is also a real geometric throat or matching structure.

In this framing, entropy arguments are not discarded. They become macroscopic shadows of a deeper geometric junction.

---

## 5. Relation to Padmanabhan

Padmanabhan's work emphasizes horizon degrees of freedom, holographic equipartition, and the emergence of cosmic space.

GD can reinterpret these ideas through the proposed horizon map:

- \(\Sigma\) is the candidate history-bearing structure that GD2 must derive;
- \(\mathcal H\) is the causal/informational boundary;
- \(L\) is the curvature scale;
- thermodynamic quantities may describe the coarse-grained accounting of the completed throat geometry.

If GD2 constructs that map, the thermodynamic and geometric languages may become complementary descriptions of the same throat.

---

## 6. Relation to Milgrom and McGaugh

Milgrom's key clue is the acceleration scale:

\[
a_0\sim cH.
\]

McGaugh and others sharpened the empirical side through the radial acceleration relation and the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation.

GD3 attempts to explain why the scale enters:

\[
g_{\rm mix}\sim\sqrt{g_NcH}.
\]

This future thermodynamic paper would then ask why the same cosmic scale appears in both entropy/horizon arguments and galaxy dynamics.

The GD answer should be:

> local matter and global horizon curvature are coupled because both live inside the same throat-centered geometry.

---

## 7. Thermodynamic Face of the Triple Identity

GD2 tests the triple identity:

\[
L \leftrightarrow \mathcal H \leftrightarrow \Sigma.
\]

If the geometric identity is established, a thermodynamic synthesis would add a fourth face:

\[
L \leftrightarrow \mathcal H \leftrightarrow \Sigma \leftrightarrow S_H,
\]

where \(S_H\) denotes horizon entropy or the coarse-grained thermodynamic accounting of the horizon/throat structure.

This should be treated carefully. The claim is not that all four objects are literally identical. The claim is that they may be different languages for the same underlying regime.

---

## 8. Questions the Paper Should Answer

1. Why does the de Sitter horizon appear in galaxy-scale arguments?
2. Why does \(a_0\sim cH\) appear in MOND phenomenology?
3. Why do thermodynamic derivations of gravity so often involve horizons?
4. What does the completed horizon map add that previous thermodynamic approaches lacked?
5. Can horizon entropy be reinterpreted as the coarse-grained face of GD throat geometry?
6. Does GD recover the successes of emergent-gravity reasoning without relying on entropy as the fundamental cause?

---

## 9. What This Paper Should Not Do

This paper should not try to replace GD2 or GD3.

It should not:

- prove the triple identity from scratch,
- derive the square-root galaxy law from scratch,
- claim Verlinde or Padmanabhan were simply wrong,
- reduce geometry to thermodynamics,
- or reduce thermodynamics to metaphor.

Its job is reconciliation and synthesis.

---

## 10. Provisional Research Statement

A mature version of the paper could state:

> We propose that several apparently distinct lines of thought in modern gravitational physics — MOND phenomenology, radial acceleration scaling, de Sitter horizon thermodynamics, emergent gravity, and black-hole/cosmology duality — may be partial manifestations of a single geometric regime. The proposed bridge is a throat-centered spacetime whose PG flow and de Sitter embedding are exact in the symmetric limit, and whose complete horizon map would connect local baryonic gravity to cosmological curvature.

---

## 11. Conclusion

This paper would turn the GD program outward toward the broader literature.

Rather than positioning GD as one more alternative to MOND, emergent gravity, or horizon thermodynamics, it should position GD as the geometric synthesis that explains why all of those programs were repeatedly drawn to the same clues:

- horizons,
- entropy,
- de Sitter space,
- \(a_0\sim cH\),
- and the deep relation between local gravity and cosmic scale.
