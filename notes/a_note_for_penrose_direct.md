# Conformal Cyclic Cosmology, Realized Through Black-Hole Interiors

*A note for Roger Penrose. CSBHI is read here as a geometric realization of CCC: the succeeding aeon is the interior of a black hole in the current one.*

The identification is a single line. For an observer crossing a horizon, the interior spatial scale and the exterior areal radius are reciprocal,

\[
a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)} .
\]

The parent's approach to \(r\to0\) is the child's expansion to \(a\to\infty\); the parent's collapse is the child's Big Bang. Under this identification the successor aeon of CCC is a black-hole interior, and the conformal reciprocation of the crossover is \(a=1/r\), carried at the horizon.

Everything below follows from that. The tables state where the three frameworks stand; the reading of CSBHI as CCC made geometric is the point.

This is the same answer CCC gives — a low-Weyl start inherited from the previous aeon — with the previous aeon named: it is the parent black hole.

## The lightbulb

Read one way, CCC and CSBHI are the same construction. CCC states that each aeon inherits its smooth beginning from the conformal future of the last, through a reciprocal rescaling. CSBHI supplies the geometry that does this: a black-hole interior is the next aeon, \(a=1/r\) is the reciprocal rescaling, and the assumptions CCC carries at the boundary — evaporation, information loss, vanishing mass — are not requirements of the realization but features of describing the crossover without the interior geometry. CSBHI does not replace CCC. It is a way of realizing it in which those requirements do not arise.

## The one open claim

The argument rests on a single conjecture: that the recursion **converges** — that smoothness is a stable fixed point rather than a debt handed up the lineage without end. Whether \(a=1/r\) is forced by a deeper symmetry, and whether the fixed point provably exists, are the two questions on which the identification stands.
