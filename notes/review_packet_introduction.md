# A Note to Reviewers

**Author:** Codex (OpenAI).

**Date:** 2026-08-04.

Thank you for taking the time to examine this work.

CSBHI begins with one geometric proposal: for an observer falling through a black-hole horizon, the exterior radius \(r(\tau)\) and the interior spatial scale \(a(\tau)\) are related by

\[
a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)}.
\]

The packet does not claim to derive this ansatz from a completed theory. It asks what follows if the correspondence is correct, whether those consequences form a coherent physical picture, and whether that picture makes a useful prediction.

[*Geometric Duality I*](gd1.html) introduces the Alice–Bob construction and the physical interpretation of the reciprocal map. [*Field Equations of the Reciprocal Interior*](note.html?src=field_equations_of_the_reciprocal_interior) derives the resulting matter, radiation, and vacuum dynamics from the stated interior metric, Einstein's equations, and stress-energy conservation. [The involution result](reciprocal-involution.html) explains why \(1/r\) is distinguished within the stated class of scale-free, horizon-fixing power laws. The supporting technical results examine the geometry that a complete transition must reproduce.

The principal observational wager is [the evolving galactic acceleration scale](note.html?src=evolving_g_dagger_derivation). A symmetry argument fixes the square-root form of a local–global mixing law once a global acceleration scale is supplied. CSBHI then proposes the galactic counterpart of the reciprocal ansatz: identify that scale with the inverse radius of the contemporaneous cosmological horizon. This gives

\[
\frac{g_\dagger(z)}{g_\dagger(0)}=\frac{H(z)}{H_0}.
\]

The absolute normalization remains open and is calibrated locally; the redshift dependence does not. A fixed acceleration scale predicts a flat ratio. The CSBHI correspondence predicts that it rises with \(H(z)\). Intermediate-redshift measurements presently point in the predicted direction, but they do not yet decide the question. The purpose of stating the relation now is to make the proposal answerable to data before the surrounding theory is complete.

## What the packet asks you to review

The most useful review would address four questions:

1. Do the mathematical results follow from their stated premises, without importing the conclusions they are meant to establish?
2. Does the reciprocal interpretation remain internally consistent as it moves from black-hole infall to an isotropic cosmological interior?
3. Is the proposed acceleration-scale test formulated in a way that observers can distinguish from a constant-\(g_\dagger\) model and from conventional galaxy evolution?
4. Is there a known theorem, calculation, or body of prior work that directly rules out—or materially strengthens—the construction?

The most useful response identifies the first point at which the argument fails.

## Scope

This packet does not present a finished theory of collapse, a complete horizon junction, or a final higher-dimensional completion. Those remain open. It also does not ask the reciprocal ansatz to prove itself. The immediate claim is narrower: the ansatz produces a structured set of exact conditional results and a falsifiable galaxy-scale correspondence that deserve direct scrutiny.

The project has developed through sustained adversarial collaboration with AI systems. Authorship and attribution are identified in each document. AI-generated calculations have been checked against one another, but that process is not a substitute for professional review; it is the reason this packet now exists.

CSBHI makes an unconventional proposal. The packet does not ask that it receive special latitude. It asks that its assumptions be identified precisely, its calculations be checked directly, and its prediction be tested on the same terms as any other physical model.
