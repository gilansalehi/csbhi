# CSBHI Mini-Program — Level 5 Setup

## Event-horizon creases, rotation, and the child birth geometry

**Working note — September 10, 2026. Source: upstream project analysis. Independent review and revisions: Codex (OpenAI).**

---

## Abstract

Levels 0–4 treated spherical collapse. Exact symmetry compresses the past endpoints of the forming black-hole event horizon to one central vertex. Generic rotating or aspherical collapse need not retain that pointlike structure. Different horizon generators can acquire past endpoints at different spacetime events, producing an extended endpoint and crease set.

A crease is a nonsmooth structure of the event horizon, not a density enhancement in the collapsing matter. Smooth matter perturbations alter the metric; curvature alters the null-generator congruence; competing null arrival paths then generate caustics, crossover points, and creases in the horizon wavefront.

This distinction removes two false inferences. An extended crease does not imply multiple black holes or multiple child universes, and its statistics need not reproduce the child's scalar perturbation field. The Level-3 and Level-4 transfer of smooth density, pressure, flux, anisotropic stress, and Weyl structure remains the better candidate for primordial scalar perturbations. The crease instead records the causal topology of horizon formation.

The conformal question is sharp. A smooth positive Weyl rescaling preserves causal curves and unparametrized null geodesics. Combined with a smooth one-to-one spacetime map, it also preserves the multiplicity and nondifferentiability responsible for a crease. An ordinary conformal map therefore carries a parent crease into a child crease; it cannot erase one.

Result IV leaves the exact birth limit and absolute conformal normalization unresolved. If the required completion becomes singular or degenerate on the endpoint set, the ordinary preservation argument no longer decides the outcome. Level 5 must determine whether the generic local two-sheet crease maps into one acceptable child birth geometry without divergent stress, disconnected origins, or an unacceptable preferred direction.

Level 5 is therefore scoped but not passed.

## 1. Three different structures

The rotating-formation problem contains three objects that must remain separate:

1. the matter distribution and its density, pressure, velocity, and angular momentum;
2. the event horizon as a null wavefront generated by null geodesics;
3. the image of that wavefront under the proposed parent-to-child conformal correspondence.

A feature of one object is not automatically a feature of another. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\text{crease}\ne\text{density enhancement}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Matter influences the crease only through the causal chain

\[
\boxed{
T_{ab}
\longrightarrow
g_{ab}
\longrightarrow
\text{null-generator propagation}
\longrightarrow
\text{horizon endpoint structure}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

The stellar density may remain smooth where the event horizon is not differentiable.

## 2. Endpoint, crease, corner, and caustic sets

Let \(\mathcal H_P\) be the parent black hole's future event horizon. In four spacetime dimensions it is a three-dimensional achronal boundary generated by null geodesics. It is a smooth null hypersurface only where those generators fit together differentiably.

Let \(\mathcal E_P\subset\mathcal H_P\) be the set of past endpoints of its null generators. For \(p\in\mathcal E_P\), define the multiplicity

\[
m(p)=
\text{number of distinct horizon generators emanating from }p.
\tag{3}
\]

Then define

\[
\mathcal D_P
=\{p\in\mathcal E_P:m(p)=1\},
\tag{4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_P
=\{p\in\mathcal E_P:m(p)>1\}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

The set \(\mathcal C_P\) is the crease set. The remainder \(\mathcal D_P\) contains single-generator endpoints, including generic caustic boundary points. A normal crease point is a noncaustic endpoint where exactly two generators enter the horizon. A normal corner has three entering generators.

Under the global assumptions used in the standard endpoint-set construction, \(\mathcal E_P\) is arcwise connected and acausal. For a stable generic four-dimensional horizon that is smooth at late time,

\[
\mathcal C_P=\operatorname{int}(\mathcal E_P),
\qquad
\mathcal E_P=\overline{\mathcal C_P},
\tag{6}
\]

with the boundary supplied by multiplicity-one endpoints. Normal crease pieces form two-dimensional spacelike submanifolds; normal corners are one-dimensional, and generic caustic sets have dimension at most one.

These qualifications matter. “Crease set” is not a synonym for the entire endpoint set.

## 3. One horizon, not many births

In spherical collapse, symmetry forces the relevant generators to share one central past endpoint. Generic asphericity spreads the joining events over an extended endpoint set.

That does not imply multiple disconnected event horizons:

\[
\boxed{
\text{many generator endpoints}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{many black holes or many child universes}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

A crease point is the past endpoint of particular generators within one event horizon, not the past endpoint of an entire independent light cone.

Nor should the spherical vertex be identified uncritically with “the Big Bang.” In an FLRW model, the Big-Bang limit is a spacelike limiting boundary rather than one event at one spatial position. The current GD2 construction has established only an outgoing child birth cone in the OS formation patch; its stronger status as a particle horizon or complete lower causal boundary remains open.

There is also a dimensional obstruction to the naive identification

\[
\mathcal C_P=\text{child initial hypersurface}.
\tag{8}
\]

A normal crease submanifold is two-dimensional and spacelike in four-dimensional spacetime, whereas a cosmological initial spatial hypersurface would be three-dimensional. The conformal event map must determine the role of \(\mathcal C_P\); dimensional analogy cannot.

## 4. How a crease forms

Take a smooth late-time cross-section of the event horizon and trace its null generators backward through the collapse. Let \(k^a\) be a generator tangent, \(e_A^a\) with \(A=1,2\) a screen basis transverse to the ray, and \(\xi^A\) the separation between neighboring generators.

The null geodesic-deviation equation is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d^2\xi^A}{d\lambda^2}
=-\mathcal T^A{}_B\xi^B,
}
\tag{9}
\]

where, up to the sign convention for the Riemann tensor,

\[
\mathcal T_{AB}
=R_{acbd}e_A^ak^ce_B^bk^d.
\tag{10}
\]

Its trace is Ricci focusing from matter,

\[
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal T
=R_{ab}k^ak^b,
\tag{11}
\]

while its trace-free part is the screen projection of Weyl curvature.

In spherical symmetry the optical history is angularly degenerate. Asphericity breaks that degeneracy, so distinct generators need not acquire the same past endpoint. Neighboring generators can terminate at caustics; non-neighboring generators can meet at crossover points. At a normal crease, two smooth null sheets meet transversely and the event horizon is not differentiable.

Rotation must not be represented solely as an (O(\chi^2)) quadrupole. In a slow-rotation expansion,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{AB}
=T_0\delta_{AB}
+\chi\,\mathcal T^{(1)}_{AB}
+\chi^2\mathcal T^{(2)}_{AB}
+\cdots .
}
\tag{12}
\]

The (O(\chi)) sector contains frame-dragging and odd-parity or magnetic-Weyl information. Centrifugal shape deformation and the leading mass-quadrupole correction enter at (O(\chi^2)). Which terms control the crease depends on the null congruence and collapse history.

## 5. Fermat potential and the Maxwell set

The variational description is better suited to the global crease than a local Jacobi map alone.

Let (x) label a generator on a late smooth horizon cross-section and let (u) label a candidate earlier location in the collapse region. A generalized Fermat potential

\[
\mathscr F(x,u)
\tag{13}
\]

encodes the relevant null-arrival ordering. Normally, one generator supplies the global minimum at (u). If two distinct generators satisfy

\[
\mathscr F(x_1,u)
=\mathscr F(x_2,u)
=\min_x\mathscr F(x,u),
\qquad
x_1\ne x_2,
\tag{14}
\]

then (u) belongs to the Maxwell set (B_{\rm Maxwell}(\mathscr F)). Under the projection back to spacetime,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_P
=\psi\!\left(B_{\rm Maxwell}(\mathscr F)\right).
}
\tag{15}
\]

Crease formation is therefore a degeneracy of competing null arrival paths. The ordinary two-sheet crease is the stable \((A_1,A_1)\) Maxwell structure. Higher multiplicities produce corners, while caustics live on the boundary of the generic crease set.

## 6. Gaussian perturbations do not require a Gaussian crease

Suppose the stellar perturbation is modeled by an approximately Gaussian random field \(\delta\rho(\mathbf x)\). Linearized gravity can carry approximate Gaussianity into first-order metric and Weyl perturbations:

\[
\delta\rho
\longrightarrow
\delta g_{ab}
\longrightarrow
\delta C_{abcd}.
\tag{16}
\]

The crease is produced by the nonlinear operation

\[
\mathscr F
\longrightarrow
B_{\rm Maxwell}(\mathscr F),
\tag{17}
\]

which extracts locations where distinct global minima become degenerate. There is no reason for this geometrical skeleton to inherit Gaussian field statistics. Indeed, without specifying an ensemble of collapse geometries, “the probability distribution of a crease” is not defined.

This does not conflict with nearly Gaussian primordial perturbations. Cosmological Gaussianity concerns fields such as \(\zeta(\mathbf x)\), not the later geometry of peaks, ridges, walls, or caustic sets derived from those fields.

Accordingly,

\[
\boxed{
\text{crease geometry}
\ne
\text{primordial scalar perturbation field}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

The smooth Level-3 and Level-4 parent perturbations remain the natural candidate source for the child's scalar perturbations.

## 7. Rotation supplies coherent angular structure

A rotating parent selects an axis. Slow rotation produces coherent low-multipole structure: frame dragging at first order in angular velocity and centrifugal or quadrupolar deformation at second order. Axisymmetric numerical collapse likewise produces organized, equation-of-state-dependent dynamics rather than a statistically isotropic spectrum.

A literal dominant identification

\[
\text{rotational crease}
\longrightarrow
\text{primordial density spectrum}
\tag{19}
\]

would therefore risk an excessive preferred direction. Level 5 need not make that identification.

The crease should instead be treated as the causal topology of horizon formation. Smooth stellar fluctuations can still supply the almost-FLRW scalar structure. Rotation asks whether the child also inherits vector, tensor, vorticity, or coherent anisotropic modes and whether those modes remain acceptably small or decay.

## 8. What a conformal map can and cannot remove

Suppose a parent formation region and child region are related by a diffeomorphism (F) and a Weyl factor. Written on the parent region,

\[
\boxed{
F^*g_C=\Gamma^2g_P.
}
\tag{20}
\]

If

\[
0<\Gamma<\infty
\tag{21}
\]

and (F) and \(\Gamma\) are smooth and nondegenerate across the region, then the causal cones and unparametrized null geodesics are preserved. A smooth bijection also preserves the local multiplicity of generator endpoints and whether the horizon image is differentiable.

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{
F(\mathcal C_P)=\mathcal C_C
}
\tag{22}
\]

for the corresponding null boundary, provided the map extends smoothly across the crease. A regular conformal rescaling transports a crease; it does not smooth one away.

Result IV does not yet establish a regular nonzero \(\Gamma\) at the exact horizon-birth limit. Its local formation map determines relative scaling away from the vertex while leaving the absolute calibration and exact completion open. If \(\Gamma\) vanishes, diverges, becomes nondifferentiable, or if (F) ceases to be a diffeomorphism on the endpoint set, Equation (22) no longer settles the child geometry.

That exceptional behavior cannot be assumed merely because it would be useful. It is the calculation Level 5 must perform.

## 9. The Level-5 program

Level 5 naturally divides into two coupled tests.

### 5A. Smooth slow-rotation transfer

Perturb the spherical formation geometry in the dimensionless spin parameter \(\chi\). Track

\[
\text{frame dragging},
\quad
\text{magnetic Weyl curvature},
\quad
\text{vorticity},
\quad
\text{shear},
\quad
\text{quadrupolar deformation}
\tag{23}
\]

through the conformal event map. Determine which child vector and tensor modes appear at (O(\chi)) and which scalar or shape corrections appear at (O(\chi^2)).

### 5B. Crease completion

Take the generic local normal crease, where two smooth null sheets meet along a two-dimensional spacelike submanifold, and apply the actual GD birth completion. Determine whether the child image is

\[
\boxed{
\text{one connected and physically admissible birth geometry}
}
\tag{24}
\]

or whether it contains divergent transformed stress, multiple disconnected child domains, or a persistent nonsmooth preferred-direction signal.

The first local calculation should use the stable \((A_1,A_1)\) Maxwell germ. More complicated generic structures can then be assembled from the classified crease, corner, and caustic pieces.

## Conclusion

The event-horizon crease is a property of the global null boundary, not a material overdensity. It records where multiple generators acquire a common past endpoint. Generic four-dimensional horizons contain two-dimensional normal crease pieces, one-dimensional corners, and lower-dimensional caustics rather than one structurally stable spherical vertex.

This does not produce many universes. It replaces the spherical point endpoint with an extended causal skeleton inside one connected horizon.

Nor should the crease be asked to reproduce the primordial density spectrum. Smooth density, pressure, dissipative, and Weyl perturbations already provide that channel. Rotation and the crease present a different question: whether the causal topology and preferred angular structure of a realistic horizon can pass through the GD birth map without spoiling the child geometry.

A regular Weyl rescaling cannot solve the problem because it preserves null paths and transports the crease. The only possible change must arise from the unresolved behavior of the parent-to-child map at the exact endpoint set.

The next calculation is therefore local and decisive:

\[
\boxed{
\text{generic two-sheet parent crease}
\xrightarrow{\ \text{GD birth completion}\ }
\text{child null-boundary geometry}.
}
\]

That calculation begins Level 5. It does not yet complete it.
