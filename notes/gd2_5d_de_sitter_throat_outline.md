# Geometric Duality II: The 5D de Sitter Throat

*Working outline for a future primary-author paper*

## Abstract

GD1 proposes the reciprocal relation \(a=1/r\) between Bob's horizon-normalized infall coordinate and Alice's spatial scale. Within Alice's specified interior, that relation carries the matter–radiation–\(\Lambda\) expansion history into the infall picture, defers the \(r=0\) endpoint to infinite Alice proper time, and approaches an exact de Sitter geometry.

GD2 asks where that reciprocal interior comes from. Its subject is the 5D de Sitter throat: a higher-dimensional geometric structure whose four-dimensional descriptions may include Bob's black-hole exterior, Alice's expanding interior, and the transition between them. The paper must keep the curvature scale, causal horizons, apparent horizons, and matching history distinct. Its goal is not to identify those objects, but to derive their relationships from one geometry.

---

## 1. Purpose

GD2 is the geometric construction paper. GD1 specifies the reciprocal interior and derives its principal consequences. GD2 must explain how that interior belongs to a complete spacetime.

The central question is:

> Can a 5D de Sitter throat generate Bob's exterior description, Alice's reciprocal interior, and the junction data connecting them as different projections or slices of one geometry?

A successful construction would turn the reciprocal ansatz from a proposed dictionary into a feature of a global model.

## 3. Results inherited from GD1

Within Alice's specified geometry:

- \(H_A=\dot a/a=F(r)/r\);
- the reciprocal probe congruence expands isotropically;
- matter and radiation dilute as \(a^{-3}\) and \(a^{-4}\);
- the matter–radiation–\(\Lambda\) Friedmann history maps into the infall function;
- positive \(\Lambda\) places Bob's \(r=0\) endpoint at infinite Alice proper time with finite limiting curvature;
- the vacuum-dominated limit is the standard de Sitter flat patch.

These results determine the child-side geometry. They do not construct the parent–child map.

## 4. The standard 5D embedding

Four-dimensional de Sitter spacetime is the hyperboloid

\[
-X_0^2+X_1^2+X_2^2+X_3^2+X_4^2=L^2
\]

embedded in five-dimensional Minkowski space. Its curvature radius is

\[
L=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\Lambda}}=\frac{c}{H_\Lambda}.
\]

The flat FLRW slicing appears as a Lorentz boost in the embedding plane. In Alice's areal radius \(\bar R=aR\), the same de Sitter geometry takes the Painlevé–Gullstrand form

\[
ds^2=-d\tau^2+
\left(d\bar R-H_\Lambda\bar R\,d\tau\right)^2
+\bar R^2d\Omega^2.
\]

This equivalence is exact in the vacuum-dominated limit. GD2 must determine whether the embedding can be extended into the full throat geometry rather than used only to describe its asymptotic child-side endpoint.

## 5. Distinct geometric structures

The construction contains several objects whose radii can coincide in symmetric limits without becoming the same object:

- \(L\): the invariant de Sitter curvature scale;
- \(R_A\): Alice's apparent-horizon radius;
- \(B(\tau)\): Alice's cosmological event-horizon radius;
- the parent black-hole event horizon;
- \(\Sigma\): the matching history or finite transition region.

In exact de Sitter space, several associated radii equal \(c/H_\Lambda\). At finite matter or radiation density they separate. GD2 must derive their relationships and causal roles without replacing those distinctions with a single “horizon” label.

---

## 6. The throat construction

The working geometric possibilities are:

1. two four-dimensional slices or projections of one five-dimensional throat;
2. a finite transition region interpolating between parent Weyl curvature and child conformal flatness;
3. a null limit of that transition at the parent black-hole horizon;
4. a dynamical matching history whose parent-side area becomes Alice's cosmological event-horizon history.

The throat must explain why the reciprocal coordinate is selected, how isotropy emerges on Alice's side, and where the transverse stress or distributed curvature resides.

## 7. Junction constraints inherited from the technical sequence

*Junction Conditions for the Reciprocal Interior* supplies acceptance tests for the construction:

- spherical areal radius must agree across the matching structure;
- a common cosmological constant cancels from the local timelike Kottler mass condition;
- dust admits a clean local timelike match, while radiation leaves a controlled pressure residual;
- the simplest stationary null match fixes equal Schwarzschild and apparent-horizon radii but leaves a transverse-curvature jump;
- that jump equals the child-side work density projected from volume to area;
- Alice's event-horizon history obeys \(H_A=(c+\dot B)/B\);
- a completed parent geometry must derive that history rather than reconstruct it from observations.

These are boundary conditions for GD2, not substitutes for its 5D geometry.

## 8. Required derivations

GD2 should aim to provide:

1. an explicit five-dimensional metric or embedding construction;
2. the four-dimensional parent and child geometries induced by the relevant slices;
3. the map between Bob's exterior variables and Alice's interior variables;
4. the causal character and finite width, if any, of the throat;
5. the induced and transverse junction data;
6. the stress-energy or higher-dimensional curvature supporting the transition;
7. the origin of the reciprocal relation \(a=1/r\);
8. the controlled de Sitter limit and its exact PG flow;
9. a stability and perturbation programme for later papers.

---

## 9. What would count as success

The paper succeeds if one geometric construction produces the reciprocal child interior and satisfies the junction constraints without identifying distinct horizons by notation or assumption.

The strongest result would derive both:

\[
a(\tau)=\frac1{r(\tau)}
\]

and

\[
H_A=\frac{c+\dot B}{B}
\]

from the same parent–child throat geometry. That would connect the reciprocal scale, local expansion, and global causal boundary without importing the observed expansion history as input.

---

## 10. Handoff

GD2 should end with the throat geometry itself, not a catalogue of later promises. Once the construction is secure, later work can ask how perturbations cross it, how horizon thermodynamics arises from it, and whether its curvature couples local baryonic gravity to the global acceleration scale.
