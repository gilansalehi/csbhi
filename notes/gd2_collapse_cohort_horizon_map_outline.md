## Abstract

GD I follows one freely falling Alice and proposes \(a=1/r\). GD II repeats the experiment throughout the formation history of the parent black hole. Comparing collapse-day Alice with present-day Alice turns the unknown junction into an operational cohort map.

The crossings form a null history. In child conformal coordinates its minimal spherical image is \(R=\eta=\Phi(v_H)\), the particle-horizon history of one FLRW causal patch. Homogeneous collapse supplies an invariant shell label through enclosed mass fraction, and one conformal factor maps the full shell family into child comoving shells. The reciprocal ansatz evolves each cohort after crossing. The radial horizon map is thereby reduced to one clock law, \(\Phi(v_H)\).

GD II's central calculation is to derive that law from a dynamical parent collapse while preserving GD I's isotropic reciprocal interior. Success would remove the point singularity from the completed spacetime, fix the child causal slicing, and determine whether four dimensions suffice or a scale/throat dimension is required.

## 1. Begin with GD I

Reintroduce Alice, Bob, the probe cloud, and \(a=1/r\). State what GD I establishes and what remains open. The only new move is repeating the experiment at different parent epochs.

## 2. Collapse-day and present-day experiments

Define the cohort family \(\gamma_{v_H}(\tau)\). Treat Precursor Alice as the limiting early cohort and Present Alice as a later cohort. Separate the timelike Alice branch from the null photon tangent to the event horizon.

## 3. The causal wedge

Draw the radial conformal diagram and derive

\[
R=\eta=\Phi(v_H).
\]

Identify this child surface as a particle-horizon history. Keep the Hubble radius, apparent horizon, cosmological event horizon, and CMB surface distinct.

## 4. Every collapse shell

Label shells by enclosed mass fraction \(\mu\). In homogeneous spherical collapse, derive

\[
R_P(\mu)=B_*\mu^{1/3},
\qquad
R_C(\mu)=R_b\mu^{1/3}.
\]

Show that one conformal factor maps all shells. Interpret deviations from the common profile as the Weyl/density-contrast data the geometry must relax.

## 5. Evolution after crossing

For every cohort use

\[
\frac{a(T)}{a(T_i)}
=
\frac1{r_i(\tau)}.
\]

Recover the probe expansion, dilution laws, Friedmann history, and de Sitter future inherited from GD I and RI I.

## 6. Derive the clock

Show why \(r=1\) along the horizon leaves \(\Phi'(v_H)\) undetermined. Use a dynamical collapse embedded in the parent FLRW background to compare:

- Bob's station proper time;
- an advanced horizon coordinate;
- parent FLRW cosmic time;
- horizon flux and area;
- the optical transfer derivative.

The calculation must select one clock rather than fit one afterward.

## 7. Curvature and singularity replacement

Construct the four-metric over the cohort family and verify:

- no locally detectable impulse at the horizon;
- finite curvature as \(r\to0\);
- no shell crossing;
- controlled Weyl-to-Ricci relaxation;
- geodesic completeness or a characterized boundary.

Exact Schwarzschild's singularity is not a coordinate artifact. The completed CSBHI metric must replace the vacuum interior before that endpoint forms.

## 8. Test the need for a fifth dimension

After the four-dimensional cohort construction is explicit, determine whether its clock and transverse stress can arise in four dimensions. If not, promote reciprocal scale to the geometric degree of freedom developed in the scale-field and dilaton notes.

The 5D throat then becomes the answer to a demonstrated residual rather than the opening premise.

## 9. Calibration and tests

Use the derived \(\Phi\) to calculate:

- child age assigned to a parent crossing epoch;
- particle-horizon history;
- optical redshift from Bob to Alice;
- mapping of parent accretion into child epochs;
- whether \(R_p\), \(R_H\), and \(H_0\) follow consistently.

## 10. Success criterion

GD II succeeds if one collapse geometry produces:

1. the null cohort map \(R=\eta=\Phi(v_H)\);
2. the homogeneous shell correspondence;
3. GD I evolution along every cohort;
4. the physical normalization of \(\Phi\);
5. a regular global causal diagram without a point singularity.

Perturbations, rotation, and the galaxy law follow only after this background map is secure.
