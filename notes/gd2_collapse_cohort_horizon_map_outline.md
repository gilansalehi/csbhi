## Abstract

GD I follows one freely falling Alice and proposes \(a=1/r\). GD II repeats the experiment throughout the formation history of the parent black hole. Comparing collapse-day Alice with present-day Alice turns the unknown junction into an operational cohort map.

The crossings form a null history. In child conformal coordinates its minimal spherical image is \(\chi_C=\eta_C=\Phi(v_H)\), an outgoing birth cone whose stronger particle-horizon interpretation remains to be tested. Homogeneous collapse supplies a Lagrangian cohort label, and one conformal factor maps the full shell family into child comoving shells. The map is not isometric: it does not equate parent and child areal radii, masses, or marginal surfaces. The reciprocal ansatz evolves each cohort after crossing. The radial horizon map is thereby reduced to one clock law, \(\Phi(v_H)\).

GD II's central calculation is to derive that law from a dynamical parent collapse while preserving GD I's isotropic reciprocal interior. Success would remove the point singularity from the completed spacetime, fix the child causal slicing, and determine whether four dimensions suffice or a scale/throat dimension is required.

## 1. Begin with GD I

Reintroduce Alice, Bob, the probe cloud, and \(a=1/r\). State what GD I establishes and what remains open. The only new move is repeating the experiment at different parent epochs.

## 2. Collapse-day and present-day experiments

Define the cohort family \(\gamma_{v_H}(\tau)\). Treat Precursor Alice as the limiting early cohort and Present Alice as a later cohort. Separate the timelike Alice branch from the null photon tangent to the event horizon.

## 3. The causal wedge

Draw the radial conformal diagram and derive

\[
\chi_C=\eta_C=\Phi(v_H).
\]

Identify the image first as an outgoing child birth cone. Then test the stronger interpretation as a particle-horizon history by determining whether its vertex is the lower causal boundary of the child patch. Keep the Hubble radius, apparent horizon, cosmological event horizon, and CMB surface distinct.

## 4. Every collapse cohort

Label cohorts by a Lagrangian coordinate or conserved material charge. For OS dust, enclosed mass fraction \(\mu\) supplies the convenient special case

\[
R_P(\mu)=B_*\mu^{1/3},
\qquad
R_C(\mu)=R_b\mu^{1/3}.
\]

Show that one conformal factor maps all cohorts. Interpret deviations from the common profile as the Weyl/density-contrast data the geometry must carry into child perturbations.

The shared \(\mu^{1/3}\) profile is a dust-specific normalized shell-label correspondence, not an equality \(R_P=R_C\). With pressure, compression work changes the Misner–Sharp mass of a comoving shell, so the general label must be Lagrangian or current-based. In physical units the construction requires \(R_C=\Gamma R_P\), with the transformed Misner–Sharp mass including gradient terms; marginality is not assumed to transfer.

## 5. Evolution after crossing

For every cohort use

\[
\frac{a(T)}{a(T_i)}
=
\frac1{r_i(\tau)}.
\]

Recover the probe expansion, dilution laws, Friedmann history, and de Sitter future inherited from GD I and RI I.

Show why \(r=1\) along the horizon leaves \(\Phi'(v_H)\) undetermined in general. Use Result IV's exact Oppenheimer–Snyder map as the homogeneous analytic benchmark. [*The Tolman VII Birth-Vertex Amplitude Benchmark*](note.html?src=tolman_vii_birth_vertex_amplitude_benchmark) measures the central tidal coefficient and shows why the outer star requires nonlinear evolution. The [*Level 3 Progress Note*](note.html?src=level_3_general_spherical_perfect_fluid_completion) supplies the exact event-horizon, mass, Weyl, and Alice-tilt transport equations for a perfect fluid. The [*Level 4 Progress Note*](note.html?src=level_4_dissipative_spherical_collapse) extends the same protection to regular heat flux, radiation transport, and pressure anisotropy. The [*Level 5 Setup*](note.html?src=level_5_event_horizon_creases_and_rotation_test) defines the new nonspherical obstruction: a regular conformal map preserves the generic event-horizon crease. The [*Level 5 Conditional Result and Campaign Summary*](note.html?src=level_5_rotation_and_campaign_summary) shows that slow rotation creates no first-order scalar obstruction and that a local crease-nucleation perestroika has controlled intrinsic metric distortion, while leaving the absolute birth completion and its full crease image open. A collapse embedded in the parent FLRW background must compare:

- Bob's station proper time;
- an advanced horizon coordinate;
- parent FLRW cosmic time;
- horizon flux and area;
- the optical transfer derivative.

The calculation must select one clock rather than fit one afterward.

## 7. Curvature and singularity replacement

Construct the four-metric over the cohort family and verify:

- no locally detectable impulse at the horizon;
- finite curvature as \(r\to0\);
- no shell crossing;
- controlled Weyl-to-Ricci relaxation;
- geodesic completeness or a characterized boundary.

Exact Schwarzschild's singularity is not a coordinate artifact. The completed CSBHI metric must replace the vacuum interior before that endpoint forms.

## 8. Test the need for a fifth dimension

After the four-dimensional cohort construction is explicit, determine whether its clock and transverse stress can arise in four dimensions. If not, promote reciprocal scale to the geometric degree of freedom developed in the scale-field and dilaton notes.

The 5D throat then becomes the answer to a demonstrated residual rather than the opening premise.

## 9. Calibration and tests

Use the derived \(\Phi\) to calculate:

- child age assigned to a parent crossing epoch;
- outgoing birth-cone history and the status of its proposed particle-horizon interpretation;
- optical redshift from Bob to Alice;
- mapping of parent accretion into child epochs;
- whether \(R_p\), \(R_H\), and \(H_0\) follow consistently.

## 10. Success criterion

GD II succeeds if one collapse geometry produces:

1. the null cohort map \(\chi_C=\eta_C=\Phi(v_H)\);
2. the homogeneous cohort correspondence, with a physically conserved label;
3. GD I evolution along every cohort;
4. the physical normalization of \(\Phi\);
5. a regular global causal diagram without a point singularity.

Perturbations, rotation, and the galaxy law follow only after this background map is secure.
