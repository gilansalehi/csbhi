## Abstract

The Oppenheimer–Snyder interior is a closed FLRW dust geometry and is therefore conformally flat. Its growing black-hole event horizon is an outgoing radial null surface. The standard conformal map from the Einstein cylinder to Minkowski space sends that generator exactly to

\[
T_M=R_M=\frac12\tan q.
\]

This is the strongest part of the proposed formation map: the pointlike event-horizon vertex and its complete null history become the outgoing light cone used in the GD2 cohort construction. Interpreting that cone as the child's birth boundary—and specifically as a particle-horizon history—still requires the CSBHI parent–child identification, a choice of child FLRW conformal factor, and a statement that the vertex is the lower causal boundary of the child patch.

For the \(\beta=0\) conformal completion used in the original calculation, the overlapping-cohort condition gives a short accelerated formation phase. In the weak-compactness limit its effective equation of state approaches \(w=-2/3\), while its scale factor grows by \(9/4\), or \(0.811\) e-fold. The null map does not select \(\beta\), so the effective equation of state is completion-dependent. In every case the sweep is too short to account for the large parent–child hierarchy, whose absolute calibration remains undetermined.

The conformal Misner–Sharp transformation can be evaluated exactly on the event horizon. It does reproduce the child FLRW mass, but this agreement is an identity rather than an independent test. More specifically,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{M_C}{\Gamma M_P}
=
\cos^2q\,
\cos^2\!\left(\frac{3q_0-q}{2}\right)
}.
\]

The gradient contribution is negative in this benchmark: it reduces the naïve term \(\Gamma M_P\). The large absolute mass scale therefore resides in the still-open conformal calibration, not in a positive mass generated by the gradient terms.

The next decisive problem is to supply a dynamical law or junction principle that fixes the conformal normalization and extends the null correspondence to the timelike cohort congruence.

## 1. Oppenheimer–Snyder setup

Use geometrized units \(c=G=1\). The homogeneous dust interior is

\[
ds_P^2
=
S^2(\eta_P)
\left(
-d\eta_P^2+dq^2+\sin^2q\,d\Omega^2
\right).
\tag{1}
\]

Write the collapsing dust solution as

\[
S(\eta_P)
=
\frac{A_m}{2}(1+\cos\eta_P).
\tag{2}
\]

Let the stellar surface be \(q=q_0\). It crosses its Schwarzschild radius when

\[
\eta_f=\pi-2q_0.
\tag{3}
\]

The outgoing event-horizon generator reaches the surface at that event and begins at the center at

\[
\eta_0=\eta_f-q_0=\pi-3q_0.
\tag{4}
\]

For this vertex to lie after the conventional OS initial slice at maximum expansion, one must have

\[
\boxed{q_0<\frac{\pi}{3}}.
\]

Using \(R_{\rm init}=A_m\sin q_0\) and \(2M=A_m\sin^3q_0\), the same condition is

\[
\frac{2M}{R_{\rm init}}=\sin^2q_0<\frac34.
\]

For \(q_0\ge\pi/3\), the formal vertex lies before the selected initial slice. The conformal identity remains valid on its domain, but this OS interval no longer describes horizon formation from initially horizon-free data.

With

\[
\bar\eta=\eta_P-\eta_0,
\tag{5}

the event horizon inside the dust is

\[
\boxed{\bar\eta=q},

0\le q\le q_0.

Along it,

=

## 2. What the multi-cohort condition proves

For a general spherical collapse, let

\[
r_q(t)
=
\frac{R(t,q)}{R[t_H(q),q]}.
\tag{9}
\]

Suppose one parent synchronous time \(t\) maps to one child cosmic proper time \(\tau_C(t)\), independent of shell label. Applying the reciprocal evolution to every crossed cohort gives

\[
\frac{a_C[\tau_C(t)]}{a_C[\tau_H(q)]}
=
\frac1{r_q(t)}.
\tag{10}
\]

Differentiation at fixed \(q\) yields

\[
H_C(\tau_C)\frac{d\tau_C}{dt}
=
-\partial_t\ln R(t,q).
\tag{11}
\]

The left side is shell independent, and hence

\[
\partial_q\partial_t\ln R=0,
\tag{12}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{R(t,q)=S(t)F(q)}.
\tag{13}
\]

Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse satisfies this condition exactly, with

\[
R=S(\eta_P)\sin q.
\tag{14}
\]

Equation (13) is a useful result, but its logical scope matters. It is necessary only after adopting the shell-independent synchronization \(\tau_C=\tau_C(t)\). A more general map could have \(\tau_C=\tau_C(t,q)\). Nor does areal-radius separability alone prove that a general spherical metric is FLRW. Exact FLRW additionally requires the appropriate lapse, radial metric, constant-curvature spatial slices, homogeneous density, and shear-free comoving congruence.

The covariant generalization of the test is therefore not separability alone. It is whether the mapped timelike congruence has FLRW kinematics and constraints:

\[
\sigma_{ab}=0,
\qquad
\omega_{ab}=0,
\qquad
D_a\Theta=0,
\tag{15}
\]

together with homogeneous matter and constant spatial curvature. Oppenheimer–Snyder passes because its interior is already an exact closed FLRW solution.

## 3. Exact conformal null map

Define null coordinates on the Einstein cylinder and Minkowski patch by

\[
U
=
\tan\frac{\bar\eta-q}{2},
\qquad
V
=
\tan\frac{\bar\eta+q}{2},
\tag{16}
\]

\[
T_M=\frac{V+U}{2},
\qquad
R_M=\frac{V-U}{2}.
\tag{17}
\]

The metrics obey

\[
-dT_M^2+dR_M^2+R_M^2d\Omega^2
=
\frac{
-d\bar\eta^2+dq^2+\sin^2q\,d\Omega^2
}{(\cos\bar\eta+\cos q)^2}.
\tag{18}
\]

On the parent event horizon, \(\bar\eta=q\), so

\[
U=0,
\qquad
V=\tan q.
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T_M=R_M=\frac12\tan q
}.
\tag{20}
\]

This is an exact geometric result. The conformal image of the OS event-horizon generator is the outgoing Minkowski light cone from the origin.

The associated normalized map is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Phi(q)}{\Phi(q_0)}
=
\frac{\tan q}{\tan q_0}
}.
\tag{21}
\]

For \(q_0\ll1\), this canonical tangent-coordinate representative shares the leading-order homogeneous shell map

\[
\frac{\Phi(q)}{\Phi(q_0)}
\simeq
\frac{q}{q_0}
\simeq
\mu^{1/3}.
\tag{22}
\]

The distinction between result and interpretation is precise:

- **[E]** the OS event horizon maps to an outgoing Minkowski null cone;
- **[C]** GD identifies that null cone with an outgoing child birth cone and proposes its particle-horizon interpretation;
- **[O]** a dynamical law must select the physical member of the residual conformal family and show that neighboring timelike parent cohorts become the child comoving congruence.

Conformal transformations preserve null curves as unparameterized null geodesics. They do not preserve timelike geodesics in the same way. The null result therefore does not complete the multi-Alice map by itself.

## 4. The local four-dimensional Weyl factor

Let

\[
D(\bar\eta,q)
=
\cos\bar\eta+\cos q.
\tag{23}
\]

Equation (18) puts the parent metric in the form

\[
ds_P^2
=
S^2(\eta_P)D^2ds_M^2.
\tag{24}
\]

Choose a spatially flat child metric on the same Minkowski patch,

\[
ds_C^2=\mathcal A^2(T_M)ds_M^2.
\tag{25}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
g_C=\Gamma^2g_P,
\qquad
\Gamma(\eta_P,q)
=
\frac{\mathcal A(T_M)}{S(\eta_P)D(\bar\eta,q)}
}.
\tag{26}
\]

Here \(T_M\) is the dimensionless Minkowski conformal coordinate defined in Equation (17), and \(\mathcal A\) includes the constant conversion to the chosen physical child conformal coordinate. Equation (26) is a mathematically exact local Weyl relation once \(\mathcal A(T_M)\) has been supplied. It does not derive the child scale factor: every spatially flat FLRW metric can be written in this form.

The overlapping-cohort condition in the synchronous OS slicing gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_H(q)S_H(q)=K
}.
\tag{27}
\]

Along the event horizon, \(D=2\cos q\), and hence

\[
\Gamma_H(q)
=
\frac{K}{2S_H(q)^2\cos q}.
\tag{28}
\]

The relative evolution is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Gamma_H(q)}{\Gamma_H(0)}
=
\left[\frac{S_H(0)}{S_H(q)}\right]^2
\frac1{\cos q}

\tag{29}
\]

At the surface,

\[
\frac{S_H(0)}{S_H(q_0)}

\boxed{

\tag{31}
\]

An overall rescaling of the Minkowski coordinates changes the displayed normalization of \(\mathcal A\) and must be carried through Equation (26). The invariant physical question is what fixes the complete identification map and therefore the absolute ratio between the two physical metrics. Until a junction condition, field equation, or boundary datum fixes it, \(K\) is an open calibration—not a derived hierarchy.

## 5. Formation-era expansion

\boxed{

If this mapped scale factor is interpreted through the spatially flat Einstein–FLRW equations, its effective equation of state is

The weak-compactness OS sweep therefore gives a short accelerated formation phase, not a long inflationary epoch. The exact endpoint value is

The initial endpoint is

For the displayed \(\eta_C=L\tan q\) completion, direct differentiation shows \(w'(q)>0\) throughout \(0\le q\le q_0<\pi/3\). This is opposite the evolution of a positive radiation–matter–vacuum mixture as its scale factor grows. Equation (36) is its \(\beta=0\) member. This residual freedom is better described as an unfixed bulk conformal completion than automatically as gauge: a coordinate transformation of one already-fixed FLRW metric cannot change its physical equation of state. A dynamical or boundary principle must select the child congruence and the allowed \(\beta\).

In particular, the endpoint remains accelerating only for \(q_0<\pi/6\). The statement that the entire formation sweep is accelerated should therefore remain tied to the weak-compactness regime.

This history therefore creates a completion problem rather than a derived birth equation of state. A complete construction must select the conformal frame and either recover the GD1 matter content smoothly or identify the formation stress that separates the two regimes.

## 6. Exact Misner–Sharp mass audit

For a spherical metric \(g_{ab}\) with areal radius \(\mathcal R\), a Weyl transformation

\[
\widetilde g_{ab}=\Gamma^2g_{ab},
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathcal R}=\Gamma\mathcal R
\tag{40}
\]

changes the Misner–Sharp mass according to

\[
\widetilde M
=
\Gamma M
-\mathcal R^2\nabla^a\Gamma\nabla_a\mathcal R
-\frac{\mathcal R^3}{2\Gamma}
\nabla^a\Gamma\nabla_a\Gamma.
\tag{41}
\]

This follows directly by inserting \(\widetilde{\mathcal R}=\Gamma\mathcal R\) into the defining relation

\[
1-\frac{2M}{\mathcal R}
=
\nabla^a\mathcal R\nabla_a\mathcal R.
\tag{42}
\]

For the flat child metric, the Misner–Sharp mass is

\[
M_C
=
\frac12H_C^2\mathcal R_C^3
=
\frac{\mathcal A_{T_M}^2R_M^3}{2\mathcal A},
\tag{43}
\]

where \(\mathcal A_{T_M}=d\mathcal A/dT_M\). Along the mapped event horizon,

\[
R_M=T_M=\frac12\tan q,
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal A_{T_M}}{\mathcal A}=2\cos^2q\,\cot y.
\tag{44}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
M_C(q)
=
\frac{\mathcal A_H(q)}4
\cos q\,\sin^3q\,\cot^2y
}.
\tag{45}
\]

The naïvely scaled parent term is

\[
\Gamma_HM_P
=
\frac{\mathcal A_H(q)\sin^3q}
{4\sin^2y\cos q}.
\tag{46}
\]

Dividing gives the exact result

\[
\boxed{
\frac{M_C}{\Gamma_HM_P}
=
\cos^2q\cos^2y
}.
\tag{47}
\]

Consequently the combined gradient contribution in Equation (41) is

\[
\boxed{
M_\nabla
\equiv
M_C-\Gamma_HM_P
=
-\Gamma_HM_P
\left(1-\cos^2q\cos^2y\right)
\le0
}.
\tag{48}
\]

The transformation law passes exactly, but it could not have failed: Equation (41) is an identity between the Misner–Sharp definitions of two Weyl-related spherical metrics. Its value is diagnostic. In this OS formation patch:

1. the gradient terms do not supply a positive reservoir of child mass;
2. they reduce the \(\Gamma M_P\) term;
3. the absolute child mass remains proportional to the unfixed conformal normalization through \(\mathcal A_H\) and \(K\); and
4. the weak-compactness correction is small, since

\[
\frac{M_\nabla}{\Gamma_HM_P}
=
-q^2-y^2+O(q_0^4).
\tag{49}
\]

The calculation therefore corrects the proposed interpretation. Weyl-gradient terms contribute to the transformed quasi-local mass, but they do not by themselves generate the large positive cosmological mass scale in this benchmark.

The split into \(\Gamma M_P\) and \(M_\nabla\) is a transformation-law decomposition, not a division into separately conserved material energies. The negative sign in Equation (48) therefore does not by itself imply a negative local energy density.

The same result rules out an isometric or marginality-preserving reading of the Weyl map. Since \(R_C=\Gamma_HR_P\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{2M_C}{R_C}
=
\frac{2M_P}{R_P}\cos^2q\cos^2y
}.
\]

At the stellar surface \(q=q_0\), where the parent event and trapping horizons meet, the child compactness is \(\cos^4q_0\) times the parent compactness. The gradient terms that correct \(M_C\neq\Gamma M_P\) also prevent generic marginality transfer. Equal-radius or equal-mass matching belongs to a different, isometric surrogate and cannot be combined with this formation map.
