Abstract

The Universe is a Black Hole.

We present a geometric duality between late‐time cosmic acceleration and black‐hole infall by embedding a flat FLRW patch in a Schwarzschild–de Sitter exterior and expressing the interior in Painlevé–Gullstrand form.

In the exterior chart, with coordinate time \( t \), the areal radius evolves as \begin{equation} \frac{dr}{dt} \;=\; -\,F(r), \quad F(r) \;=\; r\,\sqrt{\frac{8\pi G}{3}\,\rho}, \end{equation} where \( \rho = \rho_{m} r^{3}\ + \rho_{\Lambda} \) is the energy density contained in \( r \).

In the interior FLRW chart, with proper time \( \tau \), the matching shell follows \begin{equation} r(\tau) \;=\; a(\tau) \;\propto\; e^{+H\,\tau}, \quad H \;=\;\sqrt{\frac{8\pi G}{3}\,\rho}. \end{equation}

A perspective‐shift thought experiment compares these two exponentials under the sign flip \( \tau \to -\tau \), showing that cosmic inflation is mathematically equivalent to inertial infall.

This construction suggests dark energy may emerge naturally from the interior geometry of a cosmic horizon, rather than requiring an explicit fluid with negative pressure. The geometric model estimates \begin{equation} \Lambda \approx 1.6\times10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}, \end{equation} within 50% of the conventionally inferred value, and we anticipate that re‐analysing data within a geometric framework will drive the predicted and observed Λ into even closer agreement.

You Are Here

In Euclidean geometry, Pythagoras tells us that the distance \(ds\) between two points in a Cartesian coordinate grid \((x,y)\) is given by

\begin{equation} ds^{2} = dx^{2} + dy^{2} \end{equation} [1]

This immediately generalizes to three (and higher) dimensions:

\begin{equation} ds^{2} = dx^{2} + dy^{2} + dz^{2} + \dots \end{equation}

This positive-definite quadratic form of the Euclidean metric is invariant under rotations and translations, providing a universal measure of distance and forming the geometric backbone of Newtonian physics.

In 1905, Einstein unified space and time in his theory of Special Relativity, replacing the simple metric with the spacetime interval:

\begin{equation} ds^{2} = -\,dt^{2} + dx^{2} + dy^{2} + dz^{2} \end{equation} [2]

or, for purely radial separations,

\begin{equation} ds^{2} = -\,dt^{2} + dr^{2}. \end{equation}

Minkowski (1908) interpreted this geometrically to show that events lie on a four-dimensional manifold with Lorentzian signature \((-,+,+,+)\), and that \(ds^{2}\) plays exactly the role of a metric distance [3]. Crucially, the negative sign on the temporal component distinguishes timelike \((ds^{2} < 0)\) from spacelike \((ds^{2}> 0)\) separations, enforcing the invariant light-cone structure that governs causality and underpinning the hallmark relativistic effects of time dilation and length contraction.

Einstein’s theory of General Relativity (1915) promotes the metric \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\) to a dynamical field. Free-falling particles follow geodesics determined by the Christoffel symbols (first derivatives of \(g\)), while the manifold’s curvature—encoded in the Riemann and Ricci tensors (second derivatives of \(g\))—comes from energy and momentum. This relationship is described in Einstein's field equations (with \( c = G = 1 \)):

\begin{equation} G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\,\mathcal R\,g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\,T_{\mu\nu} \end{equation} [4]

In 1916 Schwarzschild found the first unique spherically symmetric vacuum solution of these field equations, describing a Black Hole:

\begin{equation} ds^{2} = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)\,dt^{2} + \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)^{-1}\,dr^{2} + r^{2}\,d\Omega^{2}. \end{equation} [5]

Adopting conventional units where \(2M=1\) locates the Black Hole's Event Horizon at \(r=1\). If we only consider radial motion, the \(d\Omega\) term goes to 0, leaving just

\begin{equation} ds^{2} = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{r}\Bigr)\,dt^{2} + \Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{r}\Bigr)^{-1}\,dr^{2} \end{equation}

Above the Event Horizon (\( r > 1 \)), the factor \((1 - 1/r)\) is positive, so \(t\) remains a timelike coordinate and \(r\) remains spacelike, in accordance with our usual notion of time and space, but below the Event Horizon (\( r < 1 \)), the factor \((1 - 1/r)\) becomes negative, flipping the signs in front of \(dt^{2}\) and \(dr^{2}\) and thus exchanging the roles of \(t\) and \(r\): the radial direction becomes timelike and the timelike direction becomes spacelike.

But what does it mean for space and time to exchange roles at the horizon?

ΛCDM Model: Predictions & Limitations

But first: what does it mean for the coordinate roles to be fixed? In standard cosmology we enforce a global comoving time parameter \(t\) that remains timelike everywhere, so space and time never swap roles. Let \(R\) be the comoving radial coordinate in FLRW space. This gives the spatially flat FLRW line element:

\begin{equation} ds^{2} = -\,dt^{2} + a(t)^{2}\bigl(dR^{2} + R^{2}\,d\Omega^{2}\bigr). \end{equation}

The Hubble expansion rate is \(H\equiv\dot a/a\). Its dynamics follow from the Friedmann equations [6]:

\begin{equation} H^{2} = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\bigl(\rho_{m} + \rho_{r} + \rho_{\Lambda}\bigr), \quad \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\bigl(\rho_{m} + 2\rho_{r} - 2\rho_{\Lambda}\bigr), \end{equation}

Here \(\rho_m\), \(\rho_r\), and \(\rho_\Lambda\) are the matter, radiation, and vacuum-energy densities, with \(\rho_m\propto a^{-3}\), \(\rho_r\propto a^{-4}\), and \(\rho_\Lambda=\Lambda/(8\pi G)\).

ΛCDM excels at matching:

  • Type Ia supernovae indicating late‐time acceleration [9],
  • Baryon Acoustic Oscillation scales [10],
  • Cosmic Microwave Background anisotropies [11],
  • Hubble expansion rate (\(H_{0}\approx67\text{–}73\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\)) [11, 12].

Yet ΛCDM faces deep conceptual and observational challenges:

  • Cosmological constant problem: Quantum‐field estimates of \(\rho_{\Lambda}\) exceed observations by 50+ orders of magnitude.
  • Fine-tuning: Why is \(\rho_{\Lambda}\) nonzero yet so extraordinarily small?
  • Coincidence problem: Why do we happen to live at the epoch where \(\Omega_{m}\approx\Omega_{\Lambda}\)?
  • No direct detection: Dark energy interacts only via gravity, with no nongravitational signature.
  • Hubble Tension: A persistent discrepancy between local distance‐ladder and CMB‐inferred values of \(H_{0}\) [11, 12].
  • Inevitable singularities: Hawking–Penrose theorems guarantee classical GR produces spacetime singularities under reasonable conditions [13].

By fixing the comoving slicing, ΛCDM sidesteps the coordinate‐role swap seen at a black‐hole horizon. Is it possible that ΛCDM is overlooking something of great significance?

A Leap of Faith

To conceptualize the coordinate switch, let us consider physics’ two most famous astronauts, Alice and Bob, in just the scenario Schwarzschild describes. Their space station is in deep space, where the spacetime curvature is negligible, observing a supermassive black hole (SMBH) from a super‐safe distance. Let \(r\) denote the Schwarzschild areal radius Bob assigns to Alice. For now, let us say they are hovering far above the horizon, at \(r\gg 1\) (in conventional units where \(r=2M=1\)) with thrusters, so that Bob's clock tracks the idealized FLRW comoving time parameter with negligible error. On the station they experience inertial motion, and by the Equivalence Principle [14] they measure space to be flat in all directions. Alice departs in the G‐Odyssic, a spacecraft built to survive the journey to the black hole interior, and maneuvers until the SMBH’s gravity pulls her in.

Initially, Alice and Bob are very close (both at \(r\gg 1\)), and their relative velocity is small. Alice sees Bob and the station receding in her rearview cameras, eventually shrinking into a single point of light in the sky behind her, while she lets gravity pull the G‐Odyssic straight down. Meanwhile, Bob sees Alice begin to drift inward and fall toward the SMBH, accelerating as she does. He plots their positions on a large piece of graph paper, with the SMBH drawn as a unit circle (horizon at \(r=1\), singularity at \(r=0\)), which looks like this:

Bob knows two things from General Relativity [18]:

  • In his own coordinate time \(t\), he will never see Alice cross the horizon at \(r=1\), nor receive any signal from her once she does. As \(t\to\infty\), her light and radio pulses become infinitely redshifted and fade away.
  • In Alice’s proper time \(\tau\), she will reach \( r=1 \) after a finite interval and then continue inward in free fall.

Because Alice's motion remains inertial during the entire journey, she registers no forces. In her local frame, her clock ticks normally, and she measures space as flat. But what she experiences and what Bob sees are starkly different.

As Alice free‐falls toward the horizon, her radial coordinate \(r(\tau)\) decreases, and her speed relative to Bob increases. The black hole grows larger and larger in her viewport. She jettisons a cloud of miniature probes that follow her in free fall on parallel worldlines to collect data. In the unit‐circle diagram, Alice slowly approaches the event horizon located at \(r=1\)—see Figure 2.

At the precise moment Alice’s coordinate reaches \(r=1\), Bob’s reception interval between successive radio pulses stretches to infinity—he sees her signal smear across the event horizon. To him, Alice asymptotically approaches \(r=1\) as \(t\to+\infty\). Yet in Alice’s local frame, crossing \(r=1\) is unremarkable: she registers no sudden forces, space appears locally flat, and her proper‐time \(\tau\) continues smoothly, as required by the Equivalence Principle. In the unit‐circle diagram, she simply passes through the perimeter—see Figure 3.

As Alice moves past \(r=1\), Bob’s station vanishes from her instruments entirely— his radio pulses have redshifted to zero. In fact, all light from above the horizon shifts from red to black. Yet aboard the freely-falling G-Odyssic, nothing changes: her local spacetime remains perfectly flat (Minkowskian), just as the Equivalence Principle demands.

Below the horizon, Alice is in uncharted territory. According to GR, in pure‐vacuum Schwarzschild, Alice’s proper‐time to reach the central singularity at \(r=0\) is finite:

\begin{equation} \tau_{\text{vac}} = \int_{1}^{0} \frac{-\,dr}{\sqrt{1/r}} = \tfrac{2}{3} \end{equation}

Inside the horizon, Alice's proper time increases toward the future while the Schwarzschild radius decreases. Thus \(d\tau/dr<0\), and the oriented element \(-dr/F(r)\) measures positive elapsed proper time.

But this pure-vacuum picture doesn’t account for the coordinate switch, and leads to an inevitable singularity at \(r=0\). Is it possible to conceive the Black Hole interior in such a way that Alice doesn’t crash into the singularity? And if she doesn’t hit it at \( r=0 \), where is it? Fortunately, we already have the mental tools to intuitively grasp the interior geometry of the Black Hole. All we need is a little perspective—see Figure 6.

Just as a driver will never reach the road's vanishing point, where parallel lines appear to intersect, so too will Alice never reach the singularity, where all geodesics appear to converge.

A pure-vacuum interior, as Schwarzschild envisioned, forces a real crash at \(r=0\) in finite \( \tau \). This is where we depart from the standard story: to preserve Alice’s inertial experience all the way down, we replace the vacuum with a dust + Λ Painlevé-Gullstrand interior metric, characterized by the infall function \(F(r)\). In that geometry, the proper‐time \( \tau_{\rm int} \) to \(r=0\) becomes

\begin{equation} \tau_{\rm int} = \int_{1}^{0} \frac{-\,dr}{F(r)}, \quad F(r) = r \sqrt{\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho}. \end{equation} [15, 16]

Because \(F(r)\sim r\sqrt{\rho_{\Lambda}}\) near \(r=0\), \(\tau_{\rm int}\to\infty\), Alice never reaches a singularity in finite proper time—her journey remains inertial, and her local space stays flat at every moment, as required by the Equivalence Principle. The coordinate switch means the black hole singularity is not a point in space, but a point in time located at future infinity.

Furthermore, the specific geometry of this novel perspective has some very surprising qualities.

Infall Becomes Expansion

Let \(r(\tau)\) be Alice's position on Bob's radial diagram, and define her positive inward flow by \[ F(r) \equiv -\frac{dr}{d\tau}. \] The infall function introduced above follows from the inverse-radius geometry and the dust + Λ energy constraint. Geometric Duality sets \[ a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)}, \] so \[ H_{\rm GD} = \frac{\dot a}{a} = -\frac{\dot r}{r} = \frac{F(r)}{r}, \qquad F(r)=rH_{\rm GD}. \] For conserved dust plus Λ, this gives \[ \boxed{ F(r) = r\sqrt{ \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_{m0}r^3+\rho_\Lambda\right) } }. \] Appendix A provides the full derivation.

The inverse-radius ansatz

Return to the cloud of probes falling alongside Alice. At the event horizon, \(r=a=1\). Let \(\ell_H\) be Alice's initial separation from a neighboring probe. As Alice falls, the scale factor stretches this separation equally in every spatial direction: \[ \boxed{ d\ell_\alpha^2 = a(\tau)^2d\ell_H^2, \qquad \ell_\alpha(\tau) = a(\tau)\ell_H = \frac{\ell_H}{r(\tau)} }. \] Thus, as Alice moves toward \(r=0\) on Bob's diagram, the distance she measures between the parallel worldlines grows without bound.

Differentiating the probe separation gives \[ \boxed{ H_{\rm GD} \equiv \frac{\dot\ell_\alpha}{\ell_\alpha} = \frac{\dot a}{a} = -\frac{\dot r}{r} = \frac{F(r)}{r} }. \] The same inward flow that carries Alice toward \(r=0\) drives the outward expansion of her probe cloud.

The probe cloud

Because the reciprocal factor acts equally in every spatial direction, a spherical cloud of probes remains spherical while its volume grows as \[ V_\alpha(\tau)=a(\tau)^3V_H. \] Its expansion, shear, and rotation are therefore \[ \boxed{ \Theta = \frac{\dot V_\alpha}{V_\alpha} =3H_{\rm GD}, \qquad \sigma_{\mu\nu}=0, \qquad \omega_{\mu\nu}=0 }. \] This is where GD departs from the standard Schwarzschild interior. Schwarzschild tides stretch a freely falling cloud in one direction while compressing it in the other two; the inverse-radius cloud expands isotropically.

From probes to dust

The probes are test particles, but the same collection of non-interacting, freely falling particles becomes pressureless dust once its mass density is included. Conservation of matter requires \[ \rho_m(\tau)V_\alpha(\tau) = \rho_{m0}V_H, \] where \(\rho_{m0}\) is the density at \(a=1\). The expanding probe volume therefore gives \[ \boxed{ \rho_m(\tau)=\rho_{m0}a(\tau)^{-3} }. \] Matter dilution has not been imported from cosmology; it follows from the inverse-radius volume and conservation of the dust cloud.

The resulting expansion

Substituting the dust density into the energy constraint gives \[ \frac{F(r)}{r} = \sqrt{ \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_m+\rho_\Lambda\right) }. \] Since \(H_{\rm GD}=F(r)/r\), Alice's expansion obeys \[ \boxed{ H_{\rm GD}^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left( \rho_{m0}a^{-3}+\rho_\Lambda \right) }. \] Differentiating and using \(\dot\rho_m=-3H_{\rm GD}\rho_m\) gives \[ \boxed{ \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_m-2\rho_\Lambda\right) }. \] The cloud expands at every stage. Its expansion initially decelerates while matter dominates, then accelerates when \(2\rho_\Lambda>\rho_m\).

The geometry that emerges

We can now identify the geometry without assuming it at the start. Center a spatial coordinate system on Alice and use \((R,\theta,\phi)\) to label the probes at the horizon. These labels are distinct from Bob's black-hole-centered coordinate \(r\). Applying the derived scale factor throughout Alice's interior gives \[ \boxed{ ds^2 = -d\tau^2 +a(\tau)^2 \left(dR^2+R^2d\Omega^2\right) }. \] This is the spatially flat FLRW metric, and the equation for \(H_{\rm GD}\) above is its dust plus Λ Friedmann equation. We did not begin by assuming that Alice observes FLRW expansion. It appears here as the geometry of the inverse-scaled probe cloud.

Geometric Duality: The Inverse-Radius Interior

Section 4 shows that the inverse-radius ansatz maps the proposed inward flow onto Alice's FLRW expansion. We can now use that ansatz to write Alice's interior geometry directly in terms of \(r_\beta\).

Alice’s interior metric

Substituting \(a=1/r_\beta\) into the spatially flat FLRW metric gives \[ ds^2 = -d\tau^2 +\frac{1}{r_\beta(\tau)^2} \left(dR^2+R^2d\Omega^2\right). \] Since \(dr_\beta/d\tau=-Hr_\beta\), the same geometry may be written with \(r_\beta\) as its time coordinate: \[ \boxed{ ds^2 = \frac{1}{r_\beta^2} \left[ -\frac{dr_\beta^2}{H(r_\beta)^2} +dR^2+R^2d\Omega^2 \right] }. \] In this chart, \(r_\beta=1\) marks the horizon and \(r_\beta\to0\) marks Alice's future endpoint. Bob's radial label has become a time coordinate in Alice's description.

The probe experiment

Return to the probe cloud Alice released during her descent. Let \(\delta R\) be the fixed comoving separation between Alice and one of the probes. The distance Alice measures to it is \[ \ell_\alpha(\tau) = a(\tau)\lvert\delta R\rvert = \frac{\lvert\delta R\rvert}{r_\beta(\tau)}. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{ \frac{\dot\ell_\alpha}{\ell_\alpha}=H }, \qquad \frac{\ddot\ell_\alpha}{\ell_\alpha} = \frac{\ddot a}{a}. \] As Bob's radial coordinate assigned to Alice decreases, the distance Alice measures to the probe increases. Because the same scale factor multiplies every spatial direction, a cloud of such probes expands isotropically around her.

The comoving four-velocity is \(U^\mu=(1,0,0,0)\) in \((\tau,R,\theta,\phi)\) coordinates. It is normalized and geodesic. Its kinematical invariants are \[ \boxed{ \Theta=\nabla_\mu U^\mu=3H, \qquad \sigma_{\mu\nu}=0, \qquad \omega_{\mu\nu}=0 }. \] The expansion is the same in all three directions, with no shear or rotation. This is the operational content of the GD role reassignment: \(r_\beta\) measures Alice's progress through time, while \(1/r_\beta\) scales her space.

Dust + Λ evolution

For the late-time matter content considered here, \[ H(r_\beta)^2 = H_0^2\left(\Omega_m r_\beta^3+\Omega_\Lambda\right) = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_{m0}r_\beta^3+\rho_\Lambda\right). \] Probe separations obey \[ \frac{\ddot\ell_\alpha}{\ell_\alpha} = H_0^2 \left( \Omega_\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}\Omega_m r_\beta^3 \right). \] Their distances grow whenever \(H>0\), decelerating during matter domination and accelerating after the Λ term becomes dominant. In the pure-Λ limit, \[ r_\beta=e^{-H_\Lambda(\tau-\tau_H)}, \qquad a=e^{+H_\Lambda(\tau-\tau_H)}. \] These exponentials are reciprocal descriptions along the same future-directed Alice evolution, not solutions connected by \(\tau\to-\tau\).

Singularity Deferral and the Horizon Map

Infinite Alice proper time

The inverse-radius relation gives \[ \frac{du}{d\tau}=-uH_A(u). \] The proper time elapsed while \(u\) decreases from \(u_0\) to \(u\) is therefore \[ \boxed{ \tau(u)-\tau(u_0) = \int_u^{u_0}\frac{du'}{u'H_A(u')} }. \] For dust + Λ, \[ H_A(u)^2 = H_0^2\left(\Omega_m u^3+\Omega_\Lambda\right). \] As \(u\to0\), \(H_A(u)\to H_\Lambda=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}\), so \[ \tau(u)-\tau(u_0) \sim \frac{1}{H_\Lambda}\ln\frac{u_0}{u} \longrightarrow\infty. \] The endpoint that Bob labels \(\rho_B=0\) is therefore never reached at finite Alice proper time within the GD interior metric.

Finite limiting curvature

Infinite travel time alone would not exclude a curvature divergence. For spatially flat FLRW, the Ricci and Kretschmann scalars are \[ \mathcal R = 6\left(\dot H_A+2H_A^2\right), \qquad K = 12 \left[ \left(\frac{\ddot a_A}{a_A}\right)^2+H_A^4 \right]. \] With dust + Λ these become \[ \mathcal R = 3H_0^2\Omega_m u^3 +12H_0^2\Omega_\Lambda \longrightarrow4\Lambda, \] and \[ K\longrightarrow\frac{8}{3}\Lambda^2 \qquad (u\to0). \] Hence \(u=0\) is a regular future conformal boundary of this Alice-frame geometry, not a curvature singularity. This is the precise mathematical content of singularity deferral in GD.

What remains to be derived

The preceding result follows exactly from the inverse-radius map and Alice metric: probe expansion, infinite future proper time, and finite late-time curvature. What remains is the smooth map from a Schwarzschild or Kottler exterior to that interior at the event horizon.

This distinction matters. A vacuum Schwarzschild interior is anisotropic and has nonzero Weyl curvature, whereas FLRW is isotropic and conformally flat. No coordinate rotation or regular scalar conformal rescaling can turn one into the other. Moreover, an event horizon is null, so the timelike Israel–Darmois shell argument previously used here does not establish matching at the horizon [17, 18].

A complete GD construction must therefore supply additional geometric structure. Viable directions include:

  1. a null-horizon matching calculation in horizon-regular coordinates;
  2. a finite timelike transition layer away from the horizon, including any required surface stress;
  3. a higher-dimensional throat for which the exterior and Alice interior arise as distinct projections or slicings.

Appendix C records this junction problem explicitly. GD1 defines and tests the Alice interior geometry; the stress-free embedding of that geometry inside Schwarzschild–de Sitter remains to be constructed.

Observables

Since the interior metric is exactly flat ΛCDM, it reproduces all standard expansion–history tests: \[ H(a)^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\bigl(\rho_{m}\,a^{-3}+\rho_\Lambda\bigr), \quad H(a)\equiv\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{dt}. \] At \(a=1\), \[ H_0 = \sqrt{\frac{8\pi G}{3}\bigl(\rho_{m}+\rho_\Lambda\bigr)} \approx 67\text{–}73\;\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}, \] in agreement with local and CMB‐inferred values. The cosmic age \[ T_0 = \int_0^1\frac{da}{a\,H(a)} \approx 13.8\;\mathrm{Gyr}, \] and the luminosity and angular‐diameter distances \[ D_L(z) = (1+z)\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}, \quad D_A(z) = \frac{1}{1+z}\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}, \] exactly match supernova, BAO, and CMB distance measures.

Discussion

Conceptual Interpretation

Accepting this Geometric Duality for Black Hole Interiors does a lot more than provide asymptotic continuity for Alice's worldline. It has serious implications for our understanding of space, time, and the universe.

In the standard ΛCDM paradigm, the cosmological constant is introduced as an extra term in Einstein’s equations purely to fit the observed late-time acceleration [33]. Although it provides an excellent phenomenological match to supernova, CMB and large-scale-structure data, Λ remains an ad-hoc parameter without deeper geometric origin [24].

Geometric Duality turns this model inside-out. Bob’s inward radial coordinate becomes Alice’s interior time variable, while its reciprocal becomes the scale of her spatial geometry. Filled with pressureless dust plus a constant energy density, this interior follows the flat ΛCDM Friedmann evolution, and the key observables—Hubble rate, cosmic age, and distance–redshift relations—follow from the same geometry.

This shift from treating Λ as an empirical adjustment to interpreting it geometrically yields several immediate conceptual benefits:

  • Singularity resolution: The proper-time to the center diverges, so what would have been a curvature singularity becomes a “future endpoint” at \(\tau\to\infty\), preserving inertial continuity.
  • Natural scale setting: Normalizing the horizon at \(u=1\) places Alice’s scale factor at \(a_A=1\) when she crosses it.
  • Coincidence reframed: If the completed horizon map fixes matter–vacuum equality at that scale, then \(\Omega_m\approx\Omega_\Lambda\) becomes a geometric feature rather than a coincidence.
  • Geometric origin of acceleration: The inverse-radius geometry connects Alice’s expansion to Bob’s infall, including the transition from matter-dominated deceleration to Λ-driven acceleration.
  • Unified framework: The same inverse scaling that governs free-fall inside a black-hole interior governs the universe’s expansion, unifying two seemingly disparate regimes.

By interpreting dark energy through the geometry of a cosmic horizon rather than as a mysterious fluid, Geometric Duality reframes the cosmological-constant problem and suggests new avenues for understanding singularities and horizon thermodynamics. The remaining task is to derive the horizon map that connects Alice’s interior to Bob’s exterior.

Clarifications

A few technical clarifications should be stated explicitly.

  • Horizon thermodynamics: The familiar black-hole area law is derived for stationary vacuum horizons. The horizon structure used here is more general; deriving a complete entropy or flux law is beyond the scope of this paper.
  • Time orientation: Alice and Bob both evolve toward their respective futures. The opposite behavior of \(u\) and \(a_A\) follows from \(a_A=1/u\), not from a sign flip \(\tau\to-\tau\).
  • Distinct radii: \(\rho_B\) is Bob’s exterior areal-radius label; \(\mathcal R_A=a_A\chi\) is an areal distance in Alice’s FLRW geometry. GD relates \(\rho_B/\rho_H\) to \(1/a_A\); it does not identify the two areal radii.
  • Horizon status: Normalizing \(u=1\) and \(a_A=1\) at Alice’s crossing is a boundary convention, not a completed junction. The event horizon is null, and its map to the Alice geometry remains to be derived.

Limitations

We have made a number of assumptions to keep the model restricted to the simplest classical regime.

  • Symmetry: Alice’s interior is assumed homogeneous and isotropic. Whether a completed GD map produces that symmetry, and how perturbations modify it, remains open.
  • Matter content: The interior source is taken to be dust + Λ. Radiation, pressure, and other early-universe components are set aside, and may alter the detailed form of the flow outside the late-time regime studied here.
  • Horizon map: No null matching, transition layer, or higher-dimensional projection has yet been shown to connect the exterior black-hole geometry to the Alice interior.
  • Parameters and observables: GD1 does not derive \(H_0\), \(\Omega_m\), or \(\Omega_\Lambda\) independently. Its background observational agreement follows after those parameters and the dust + Λ expansion history are supplied.
  • Perturbative and semiclassical effects: Only the homogeneous background is studied. A full treatment must include perturbations and possible semiclassical effects near the horizon or at high curvature.

Future Directions

We highlight three promising avenues for extending and testing Geometric Duality:

  • Completing the Geometry: Appendix E points toward a deeper geometric backbone, but only in outline. The next step is to construct \(\mathcal D_{\rm GD}\) through a null junction, a physical transition layer, or a higher-dimensional throat, and determine rather than assume its stress-energy content.
  • Horizon, Scale, and Age: The horizon-scale consistency estimate suggests a possible geometric relation between the cosmic horizon, the Hubble radius, and the age of the universe. Making that relation explicit may help clarify whether these quantities are independent inputs or different expressions of one underlying structure.
  • Dark Matter: A natural continuation of this work is to ask whether the geometric reinterpretation proposed here for Dark Energy can be extended to the remaining mass discrepancy attributed to Dark Matter, particularly in galaxy rotation curves.

Conclusion

By interpreting our observable universe as the interior of a cosmic-scale Schwarzschild horizon, we have constructed a concrete model of geometric duality. Its central relation is

\begin{equation} u=\frac{\rho_B}{\rho_H}, \qquad a_A=\frac{1}{u}, \end{equation}

which gives \begin{equation} \dot u=-H_Au \end{equation} and, within Alice’s dust + Λ interior, \begin{equation} H_A^{2} = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \bigl(\rho_{m0}u^3+\rho_{\Lambda}\bigr). \end{equation} This is the flat ΛCDM Friedmann evolution written in the inverse-radius variable. It reproduces the standard Hubble, age, and distance–redshift relations.

Appendix A: From Friedmann Expansion to the Infall Function

Let \(r(\tau)\) be the radius Bob assigns to Alice's position, parameterized by Alice's proper time. Since \(r\) decreases as Alice falls, define the positive inward speed Bob assigns to Alice's trajectory as \[ F(r)\equiv-\frac{dr}{d\tau}. \] Geometric Duality introduces the inverse-radius ansatz \[ a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)}. \] Differentiating with respect to \(\tau\) gives \[ \frac{\dot a}{a} = -\frac{\dot r}{r} = \frac{F(r)}{r}. \] Section 4 identifies \(a\) as the scale of Alice's probe cloud, so its fractional expansion rate is \[ H_{\rm GD} \equiv\frac{\dot a}{a} =\frac{F(r)}{r}. \] This is the kinematic content of the ansatz: Alice's inward speed in Bob's radial description fixes the fractional expansion rate of her probe cloud. The dynamics determine how that rate changes with time.

Recall from Section 2 that a universe containing matter, radiation, and vacuum energy obeys the Friedmann acceleration equation [6]: \[ \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_m+2\rho_r-2\rho_\Lambda\right). \] Conservation fixes how each component changes as the universe expands. Let \(\rho_{m0}\) and \(\rho_{r0}\) denote the matter and radiation densities at \(a=1\): \[ \rho_m=\rho_{m0}a^{-3}, \qquad \rho_r=\rho_{r0}a^{-4}, \qquad \rho_\Lambda=\text{constant}. \] Under \(a=1/r\), the same densities become \[ \rho_m=\rho_{m0}r^3, \qquad \rho_r=\rho_{r0}r^4, \qquad \rho_\Lambda=\text{constant}. \]

We can now express the acceleration equation in terms of Alice's inward speed. Since \(H_{\rm GD}=F/r\) and \(\dot r=-F\), \[ \frac{\ddot a}{a} = \dot H_{\rm GD}+H_{\rm GD}^2 = -\frac{F(r)F'(r)}{r} +\frac{2F(r)^2}{r^2}. \] Substitution into the Friedmann acceleration equation gives \[ -\frac{F(r)F'(r)}{r} +\frac{2F(r)^2}{r^2} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_{m0}r^3+2\rho_{r0}r^4-2\rho_\Lambda\right). \] Solving this differential equation yields \[ F(r)^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left( \rho_{m0}r^5 +\rho_{r0}r^6 +\rho_\Lambda r^2 \right) +Cr^4, \] where \(C\) is the integration constant. Dividing by \(r^2\) shows what it represents: \[ H_{\rm GD}^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left( \rho_{m0}r^3 +\rho_{r0}r^4 +\rho_\Lambda \right) +Cr^2. \] The final term is the usual spatial-curvature term written with \(a=1/r\), with \(C=-k\). For the spatially flat geometry used in Section 2, \(C=0\). More generally, taking the positive root for inward motion gives \[ \boxed{ F(r) = r\sqrt{ \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left( \rho_{m0}r^3 +\rho_{r0}r^4 +\rho_\Lambda \right) +Cr^2 } }. \]

The late-time model used in Section 3 neglects radiation and takes \(C=0\), reducing the complete expression to \[ F(r) = r\sqrt{ \frac{8\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_{m0}r^3+\rho_\Lambda\right) }. \] As \(r\to0\), \(F(r)\sim H_\Lambda r\). The proper-time integral \(\int_0^1dr/F(r)\) therefore diverges, placing \(r=0\) at Alice's future infinity.

These are familiar Friedmann dynamics written in an unfamiliar variable. The result introduced by GD is the reciprocal ansatz itself: \(a=1/r\). Once that relation is adopted, the established expansion history maps exactly onto the infall function. Appendix B examines how much physical structure survives that map.

Appendix B: What the Reciprocal Map Preserves

The Friedmann equations and their matter, radiation, curvature, and vacuum-energy terms are established results. The central result proposed by GD is the reciprocal ansatz \[ a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)}. \] Appendix A shows that this single relation carries the familiar Friedmann dynamics into Alice's infall function. The significance of the correspondence lies in how much structure survives the map.

Four components, one map

A fluid with equation of state \(p_i=w_i\rho_i\) dilutes according to \[ \rho_i(a)=\rho_{i0}a^{-3(1+w_i)}. \] The reciprocal ansatz sends each power of \(a\) to the corresponding power of \(r\): \[ \rho_i(r)=\rho_{i0}r^{3(1+w_i)}. \] Since \(F=rH_{\rm GD}\), every contribution to \(H_{\rm GD}^2\) gains the same additional factor of \(r^2\) in \(F^2\): \[ \begin{aligned} \text{radiation:}\quad &a^{-4}\longmapsto r^4\longmapsto r^6, \\ \text{matter:}\quad &a^{-3}\longmapsto r^3\longmapsto r^5, \\ \text{curvature:}\quad &a^{-2}\longmapsto r^2\longmapsto r^4, \\ \text{vacuum energy:}\quad &a^0\longmapsto r^0\longmapsto r^2. \end{aligned} \] The map requires no separate rule for any component.

Curvature occupies a special place in this sequence. It affects \(H_{\rm GD}^2\), but its effective equation of state \(w=-1/3\) makes \(\rho_k+3p_k=0\), so it does not appear in the acceleration equation. It returns as the integration constant \(C=-k\) when that equation is solved. Setting \(C=0\) selects the spatially flat model used in Section 2; retaining it carries an open or closed FLRW geometry into the infall picture.

Cosmic history in a shrinking radius

As Bob's \(r\) decreases, Alice's \(a\) grows. Radiation therefore dilutes fastest, followed by matter, while vacuum energy remains constant. The expansion decelerates while \[ \rho_m+2\rho_r>2\rho_\Lambda \] and accelerates after the inequality reverses. The reciprocal map preserves the relative scaling of the components, any resulting sequence of dominance, and the condition separating deceleration from acceleration.

In the final vacuum-dominated limit, \[ H_{\rm GD}\longrightarrow H_\Lambda, \qquad F(r)\longrightarrow H_\Lambda r. \] Alice's matter and radiation densities approach zero as her spatial scale grows without bound. In Bob's radial description, the same asymptotic future becomes an inward trajectory that approaches \(r=0\) forever without arriving in finite proper time.

What the correspondence contributes

The reciprocal map does more than reverse a radial coordinate. It preserves the organization of Friedmann evolution: every component retains its characteristic scaling, the relative order of the cosmic eras is unchanged, spatial curvature remains an independent degree of freedom, and the asymptotic future becomes a nonterminating approach to the black-hole origin.

This correspondence supports GD by showing that the inverse-radius ansatz has greater explanatory reach than singularity deferral alone. One geometric relation organizes cosmic dilution, the transition from deceleration to acceleration, spatial curvature, heat death, and Alice's nonterminating proper-time trajectory. Constructing the complete map between Bob's exterior geometry and Alice's interior remains a separate task. Appendix C states that problem precisely.

Appendix C: Status of the Horizon Map

The map to be constructed

The results in Sections 5–6 define a relation between two geometries: \[ \left(\mathcal M_B,g_B,\rho_B\right) \xrightarrow{\;\mathcal D_{\rm GD}\;} \left(\mathcal M_A,g_A,u,a_A\right), \qquad u=\frac{\rho_B}{\rho_H}, \quad a_A=\frac1u. \] The inverse-radius relation specifies the desired correspondence along Alice’s history. It does not by itself give the junction data needed to derive \(g_A\) from \(g_B\).

Why ordinary conformal matching is insufficient

A vacuum Schwarzschild interior is a Kantowski–Sachs geometry: its distinguished radial direction and its two angular directions evolve differently. It also has nonzero Weyl curvature, \[ C_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} C^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = \frac{48G^2M^2}{\rho_B^6}, \] whereas every FLRW spacetime is conformally flat. Because a regular conformal transformation preserves whether the Weyl tensor vanishes, no change of coordinates plus scalar conformal factor can convert the vacuum Schwarzschild interior into Alice’s isotropic FLRW geometry. The GD operation must contain additional geometric structure [17, 18].

Why the previous timelike-shell argument is not a horizon proof

The black-hole event horizon is a null hypersurface. By contrast, \(u=\mathrm{constant}\) surfaces in Alice’s FLRW metric are spacelike spatial slices. Setting both labels equal to one does not make those hypersurfaces geometrically identical.

The Israel–Darmois calculation for a timelike shell therefore cannot establish a smooth, stress-free match at the horizon. A null match requires horizon-regular coordinates and the null-shell junction formalism, including continuity of the degenerate intrinsic geometry and the appropriate transverse-curvature data [36]. Those data have not yet been supplied for the GD map.

Open construction routes

  1. Null boundary map: formulate both descriptions in horizon-regular coordinates and solve the null junction conditions, allowing the calculation to determine whether a surface stress tensor is required.
  2. Timelike transition layer: place a finite layer away from the null horizon and use the ordinary Israel–Darmois formalism there, retaining rather than assuming away any resulting surface stress.
  3. Higher-dimensional throat: derive the exterior and Alice interior as different four-dimensional projections or slicings of a common higher-dimensional geometry. Appendix E records the exact de Sitter asymptotic structure that motivates this route.

Consequently, this paper claims exact results within the Alice interior but leaves \(\mathcal D_{\rm GD}\) as an open problem. No stress-free Schwarzschild–FLRW horizon matching is assumed in the calculations.

Appendix D: Horizon-Scale Consistency Estimate for \(\Lambda\)

This appendix records a dimensional consistency check, not an independent derivation of \(\Lambda\). It inserts the measured Hubble and age scales into the de Sitter relation \(\Lambda=3/L_\Lambda^2\). Because \(H_0^{-1}\) and the cosmic age are observationally related, the two estimates below are not statistically independent.

From the Horizon Radius

Approximating the late-time de Sitter curvature radius by the present Hubble radius gives \[ R_H = \frac{c}{H_0}, \quad H_0 = 70\;\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}} \approx 2.27\times10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}, \] giving \[ R_H \approx \frac{3.0\times10^8\;\mathrm{m/s}}{2.27\times10^{-18}\;\mathrm{s^{-1}}} \approx 1.32\times10^{26}\;\mathrm{m}. \] Assigning \(L_\Lambda\approx R_H\) then gives \[ \Lambda = \frac{3}{R_H^2} \;\approx\; \frac{3}{(1.32\times10^{26}\,\mathrm{m})^2} \approx 1.7\times10^{-52}\;\mathrm{m^{-2}}. \]

From the Universe’s Age

A second dimensional estimate uses the measured cosmic age \(T_0\approx13.8\;\mathrm{Gyr}=4.35\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{s}\) as a proxy for \(L_\Lambda/c\): \[ \Lambda = \frac{3}{c^2\,T_0^2} = \frac{3}{(3.0\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s})^2\,(4.35\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{s})^2} \approx 1.6\times10^{-52}\;\mathrm{m^{-2}}. \] Their agreement within \(\sim10\%\) reflects the observed order-unity relation \(H_0T_0\sim1\). It motivates a common horizon-scale interpretation, but does not validate the GD map or predict \(\Lambda\) without observational input.

Corresponding Vacuum‐Energy Density

Converting \(\Lambda\) to an equivalent vacuum‐energy density via \(\rho_{\Lambda} = \Lambda\,c^2/(8\pi G)\) yields \[ \rho_{\Lambda} \approx \frac{1.7\times10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}\times(3.0\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s})^2} {8\pi\times6.67\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3/kg\,s^2}} \approx 9\times10^{-27}\;\mathrm{kg/m^3}. \]

A quantitative comparison must distinguish \(H_0\) from \(H_\Lambda=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}\) and include the matter contribution in \(T_0=\int_0^1da/[aH(a)]\). Such a calculation uses fitted cosmological parameters and remains a consistency test until GD supplies an independent rule fixing the curvature scale.

Appendix E: Connection to the 5D de Sitter Embedding

A useful exact check on the Alice geometry is that its late-time dust + Λ limit is de Sitter spacetime, which may be realized as a 4-dimensional hyperboloid embedded in 5-dimensional Minkowski space. Concretely:

De Sitter asymptotics: For dust + Λ in a flat FLRW patch, as \(a\to\infty\) (or \(t\gg H_{0}^{-1}\)), the matter term redshifts away and \begin{equation} H^{2}(t)\;\to\;\frac{\Lambda}{3},\quad a(t)\sim e^{\sqrt{\Lambda/3}\,t}. \end{equation} The metric becomes \begin{equation} ds^{2}\;\approx\;-\,dt^{2}+e^{2\sqrt{\Lambda/3}\,t}\,d\vec x^{\,2}, \end{equation} the standard flat‐slicing of de Sitter space.

In the pure-Λ GD variables, \[ u=e^{-H_\Lambda(\tau-\tau_H)}, \qquad H_\Lambda=\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{3}}. \] Defining conformal time \(\eta=-u/H_\Lambda\) gives \[ ds_A^2 = \frac{1}{H_\Lambda^2\eta^2} \left(-d\eta^2+d\vec\chi^{\,2}\right), \] exactly the flat de Sitter patch. Thus the inverse-radius Alice metric has a standard geometric realization in its asymptotic pure-Λ limit.

5D hyperboloid: That de Sitter geometry is isometric to the hyperboloid \begin{equation} -X_{0}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{3}^{2}+X_{4}^{2} = \frac{3}{\Lambda}, \end{equation} living in 5D Minkowski space with signature \((-++++\)). The flat FLRW coordinates above cover one patch of this hyperboloid.

What the embedding establishes: The five-dimensional hyperboloid realizes the Alice metric exactly in the pure-Λ limit and identifies \(L_\Lambda=\sqrt{3/\Lambda}\) as its curvature radius. It does not by itself embed a Schwarzschild exterior, identify the black-hole horizon with a particular hyperboloid section, or derive the dust contribution. Those are additional requirements on the higher-dimensional version of \(\mathcal D_{\rm GD}\).

The embedding is therefore supporting structure rather than a completed junction. Its value is that one end of the GD construction—the asymptotic Alice geometry—already has an exact and familiar higher-dimensional realization.
