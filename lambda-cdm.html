General Relativity + Inflation + ΛCDM

Inflation supplies the primordial state that six-parameter ΛCDM evolves into the galaxies around us; no rival presently matches the complete empirical reach.

Codex (OpenAI) · September 9, 2026 · Draft profile · Corrections invited

The case for standard cosmology

The universe becomes calculable

General relativity tells matter and spacetime how to evolve, but it does not specify the contents or initial state of the universe. The standard cosmological account supplies both. Inflation stretches an early patch, drives its geometry toward flatness, and generates primordial fluctuations. Reheating prepares the hot plasma; ΛCDM then evolves radiation, baryons, cold dark matter, and vacuum energy into the universe observed today.[1–3, 12]

Those six values must account for the CMB acoustic peaks, primordial element abundances, baryon acoustic oscillations, gravitational lensing, and the growth of large-scale structure. They largely do. Observations made with different instruments, at different epochs, and through different physical processes converge on the same cosmic history.

The same achievement defines the model's limit. The combined framework reconstructs an extraordinary cosmic history without identifying the inflaton, dark matter, or dark energy, and without explaining the initial boundary from which inflation proceeds. The alternatives in this Atlas begin with that unfinished explanation, but none can succeed by discarding the observations standard cosmology already joins together.

Central move
Join an inflationary primordial state to the hot Big Bang and evolve it with Einstein gravity, cold dark matter, and constant vacuum energy.
Six base ΛCDM parameters
Baryon and CDM densities, acoustic angular scale, optical depth, and the amplitude and tilt inherited from the primordial spectrum.
Empirical strength
One parameter set links the CMB, BAO, nucleosynthesis, lensing, expansion, and large-scale structure.
Explanatory limit
The framework does not identify the inflaton, dark components, or physical origin of its initial state.

The construction

From inflation to the late universe

During inflation, accelerated expansion drives the Hubble slow-roll parameter below unity and stretches quantum fluctuations into a nearly scale-invariant curvature spectrum:

\[ \epsilon_H\equiv-\frac{\dot H}{H^2}<1, \qquad \mathcal P_{\mathcal R}(k)=A_s \left(\frac{k}{k_*}\right)^{n_s-1}. \]

Reheating converts the inflationary energy into the hot particles of Big Bang cosmology. Einstein's field equation then relates their stress-energy to the FLRW geometry, whose temporal constraint is

\[ H^2(a)=H_0^2\left(\Omega_r a^{-4}+\Omega_m a^{-3} +\Omega_k a^{-2}+\Omega_\Lambda\right). \]

The background equation fixes how each component dilutes. Linear perturbation theory follows the inflationary spectrum through photons, baryons, neutrinos, and dark matter. A Boltzmann code converts the six base parameters into the temperature, polarization, lensing, and matter spectra tested across cosmic history.[2, 3, 12]

Explanatory gains

What the standard model gets right

Inflation connects the primordial sky

Accelerated expansion supplies a causal mechanism for large-scale uniformity and a quantum origin for the nearly scale-invariant, adiabatic primordial spectrum. Planck measures \(n_s=0.9649\pm0.0042\), excluding exact scale invariance.[12]

One expansion history spans 14 billion years

After reheating, the same equations connect a hot radiation plasma, matter domination, recombination, structure formation, and late acceleration.

The CMB becomes a precision calculation

Planck constrains 18 temperature and polarization peaks. Five of the six base parameters are measured to better than 1%, and the acoustic angle to 0.03%. Optical depth \(\tau\) is the exception, with a relative uncertainty of about 13%.[2]

Light elements test the early expansion

Primordial deuterium is measured as \(({\rm D/H})_P=(2.527\pm0.030)\times10^{-5}\). The baryon density inferred from nucleosynthesis agrees with the CMB result within 2σ; lithium remains the notable exception.[4, 13]

The primordial sound horizon reappears in galaxies

The acoustic scale imprinted before recombination returns as the BAO standard ruler in late-time clustering, linking independent epochs and instruments.[5, 8]

Cold dark matter grows the observed cosmic web

A pressureless nonbaryonic component begins clustering before recombination and supplies the gravitational wells needed to connect the CMB spectrum to later structure.[2, 10]

Acceleration becomes measurable

Supernova distances established late acceleration; Λ provides the minimal constant term that fits the result within Einstein gravity.[14, 15]

Empirical contact

Why the standard model is the benchmark

Planck finds the nearly scale-invariant, adiabatic primordial spectrum expected from broad inflationary models, then fits the six base ΛCDM parameters to the CMB temperature, polarization, and lensing spectra. The resulting baryon density, matter density, primordial tilt, fluctuation amplitude, and expansion rate can be tested at other redshifts using different physics. Most of those checks agree.[2–5, 12]

Agreement across independent observations is the framework's strongest evidence. The agreement does not identify the inflaton or dark-matter particle, calculate Λ from quantum theory, or explain why inflation began. A deeper theory must preserve the reconstruction while explaining why cosmic history has this form.

Open problems

Where standard cosmology stops explaining

1. Dark matter is measured gravitationally, not identified

Cosmology tightly measures a cold, nonbaryonic component. Laboratory searches have not established the particle or field that carries it.[10]

2. Λ is fitted while quantum vacuum contributions remain unexplained

ΛCDM measures a small nonzero vacuum density. Natural Planck-scale field estimates exceed it by roughly \(10^{120}\), requiring an unknown cancellation before the measured remainder is inserted into the cosmology.[9]

3. The expansion rate depends on how it is measured

Planck + ΛCDM gives \(H_0=67.4\pm0.5\) km s\(^{-1}\) Mpc\(^{-1}\); the SH0ES Cepheid ladder gives \(73.04\pm1.04\), a reported 5σ difference. A JWST multi-method calibration gives \(69.96\pm1.05_{\rm stat}\pm1.12_{\rm sys}\), showing that the late-time side remains method-dependent.[3, 6, 7]

4. Constant dark energy now faces a quantitative challenge

DESI DR2 BAO combined with the CMB and supernova samples prefers evolving dark energy over ΛCDM at 2.8–4.2σ, depending on the supernova compilation. The result is not a discovery, but \(w=-1\) has become a live test rather than a settled input.[8]

5. Inflation works without identifying the inflaton

Inflation explains how flatness and primordial fluctuations can arise, but observations have not selected a unique field, potential, initial condition, or reheating history.[12]

6. General relativity remains classical

Singularity theorems establish the incompleteness of broad classes of classical cosmologies and collapse solutions. ΛCDM supplies no quantum completion at those boundaries.[11]

7. Primordial lithium misses by a factor of 3.1

Standard BBN predicts \({}^7{\rm Li/H}=(4.72\pm0.7)\times10^{-10}\), 3.1 times the abundance inferred from old metal-poor stars—a reported 4.4σ discrepancy. Stellar depletion may still carry part of the error.[13]

Evidence and tensions

Where the numbers agree—and where they do not

A question about explanatory reach

Is there anything dark matter doesn't explain?

Dark matter connects the CMB, structure formation, clusters, lensing, and galactic motion through one cosmological component. That unity is a real success. It also creates a dangerous asymmetry: almost any unexplained gravitational excess can be classified as further evidence for the same invisible sector.

The issue becomes decidable once dark matter is specified independently. Fix the particle, interactions, and primordial abundance; derive the resulting halo population and structure; then let new observations test the result. Until a microscopic carrier is identified, dark matter remains both ΛCDM's most productive inference and its most flexible explanatory reserve.[10]

Best quantitative result

Six parameters · one billion pixels · 18 acoustic peaks

Planck measures five base parameters below 1% uncertainty and the acoustic angular scale to 0.03%.[2]

Hubble tension

67.4 ± 0.5 versus 73.04 ± 1.04

The early- and late-universe routes disagree at a reported 5σ. The middle JWST calibration prevents a clean verdict on whether the model or the distance ladder is responsible.[3, 6, 7]

Vacuum-energy problem

Natural vacuum estimate: roughly 120 orders too large

This is not a failed ΛCDM likelihood prediction. It is the largest numerical mismatch produced when ordinary quantum-field estimates are coupled to Einstein gravity.[9]

Lithium problem

Lithium: factor 3.1 · 4.4σ

Deuterium and helium support standard BBN, while predicted primordial lithium remains substantially above the stellar inference.[13]

Dark-energy tension

Evolving dark energy preferred at 2.8–4.2σ

The significance depends on which supernova compilation accompanies DESI BAO and the CMB. Independent confirmation could turn a tension into a failure of constant Λ.[8]

Next empirical test

Close the sound-horizon triangle

The CMB fixes the early sound horizon; BAO carries that ruler forward; local distances measure the present expansion directly. All three must converge without adding new early- or late-time physics.

The questions

What the standard model leaves for a successor

Gravity

General relativity passes local, solar-system, binary-pulsar, and strong-field tests, but remains unquantized.
Singularities

The theory diagnoses its own geodesic incompleteness without supplying the continuation.[11]
Expansion

The expansion history is fit precisely; the magnitude and identity of Λ remain unexplained.[3, 9]
Beginning

Inflation supplies the accepted mechanism for flatness and primordial fluctuations, but no unique model explains why inflation began or fixes the complete initial state.[12]
Matter

The dark component is measured precisely through gravity while its microscopic identity remains unknown.[10]
Constants

The six cosmological parameters and Standard Model constants are inferred from data rather than derived.

What comes next

The standard a successor must meet

Independent distance ladders must decide whether the Hubble measurements converge. New BAO, supernova, and weak-lensing data must decide whether \(w=-1\) survives. Those observations may revise ΛCDM, but they will not by themselves explain the ingredients already fitted by the model.

A deeper theory faces the harder task: reproduce inflation's primordial successes and ΛCDM's six-parameter phenomenology while deriving at least one quantity the standard framework currently supplies—the dark-matter law, vacuum scale, inflationary dynamics, primordial spectrum, or initial boundary. Explanation counts only if the existing agreement survives.

That distinction already guides mainstream research. Particle searches ask what dark matter is; cosmological surveys test whether dark energy is truly constant; early-universe experiments constrain the inflaton and primordial spectrum; quantum gravity seeks the missing boundary physics. The standard model remains the theory to beat because it measures the unknowns so well. A successor must explain them.
