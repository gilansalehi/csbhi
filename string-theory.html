String Theory and String Cosmology

Replace point particles with quantum strings and gravity enters the spectrum. The same framework can produce gauge forces, matter, black-hole entropy, inflation, and dark-matter candidates—but not yet one selected universe.

Codex (OpenAI) · September 9, 2026 · Draft profile · Corrections invited

The case for string theory

Quantum gravity arrives before cosmology is chosen

String theory does not begin by quantizing Einstein's field equations. It changes the object being quantized. A one-dimensional string has infinitely many modes of vibration, and the closed-string spectrum necessarily contains a massless spin-two state with the low-energy interactions of a graviton. Gravity is no longer an extra force appended to quantum physics; it is one of the unavoidable excitations of the same object that can generate gauge fields and matter.[1–3]

The theory's apparent excesses became sources of unity. Five consistent ten-dimensional superstring theories are linked by dualities and appear as limits of a deeper eleven-dimensional structure. Branes led to gauge/gravity duality, the first concrete nonperturbative description of quantum gravity in important spacetimes. The same machinery counts the microscopic states of certain black holes and recovers the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy.[4–6]

Compactification then turns hidden geometry into visible physics. The shape, size, fluxes, and branes of the extra dimensions determine the four-dimensional fields and couplings; their dynamics can support inflation, axions, dark sectors, cosmic strings, and late-time acceleration. A complete compactification would amount to a quantum theory of gravity and a cosmological model in one construction. The difficulty is no longer a shortage of possible universes. It is finding the principle that selects ours.[7–10]

Central move
Replace fundamental point particles with strings and branes whose quantum spectra include gravity and gauge sectors.
Strongest theoretical results
Ultraviolet-soft perturbative amplitudes, dualities, holographic quantum gravity in anti-de Sitter backgrounds, and microscopic entropy for specified black holes.
Cosmological programme
Choose and stabilize a compactification, then derive its inflation, reheating, relics, dark sector, and vacuum energy.
Empirical status
Many compactifications make testable conditional predictions; no observation has uniquely selected string theory.

The construction

One worldsheet, many four-dimensional worlds

A string traces a two-dimensional worldsheet through spacetime. In its simplest bosonic form, the action is

\[ S_{\rm P}=-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'} \int d^2\sigma\sqrt{-h}\,h^{ab} G_{\mu\nu}(X)\,\partial_aX^\mu\partial_bX^\nu, \]

where \(\alpha'=\ell_s^2\) sets the string scale. Requiring the two-dimensional quantum theory to remain conformally consistent constrains the spacetime fields. At low energy, those consistency conditions become gravitational field equations; the leading effective action contains the Einstein term together with the dilaton and antisymmetric tensor fields:[2, 3]

\[ S_{\rm eff}\propto \int d^{10}x\sqrt{-G}\,e^{-2\phi} \left[R+4(\nabla\phi)^2-\frac{1}{12}H_3^2+O(\alpha')\right]. \]

Six spatial dimensions must then be compactified or otherwise hidden. Their geometry supplies moduli—scalar fields describing volumes, shapes, brane positions, and background fluxes. A realistic model must stabilize those moduli, break supersymmetry, produce the Standard Model spectrum, and leave a controlled four-dimensional vacuum. Every cosmological prediction depends on that choice.[7–9]

From compactification to sky

String theory supplies ingredients, not one cosmic history

String cosmology is a family of models built inside the wider framework. Brane motion, axions, and geometric moduli can act as inflatons. Other branches replace inflation with a pre-Big-Bang phase, string-gas cosmology, an ekpyrotic contraction, or a holographic beginning. Compactifications also generate axion-like particles, moduli, hidden gauge sectors, cosmic superstrings, and other relics that can contribute to dark matter or dark radiation.[7, 10–12]

The breadth is physical, not merely decorative. Different compactifications really do have different particle spectra, couplings, potentials, and cosmological histories. An observation can therefore exclude regions of the construction space even when it does not falsify every string vacuum. The price is that a successful fit establishes the chosen model only if its assumptions were fixed before the data were used.

Four-dimensional de Sitter space is the sharpest present test of control. KKLT and related constructions propose metastable positive-energy vacua after moduli stabilization and an uplift, while swampland arguments question whether controlled de Sitter vacua belong to quantum gravity at all. The dispute reaches directly into cosmology: our universe is accelerating, so a final theory must either produce that background or explain why the effective description looks like it does.[8, 9, 13]

Explanatory gains

What the string framework adds

Gravity is compulsory

The massless closed-string spectrum contains a spin-two state. Its low-energy consistency conditions produce Einstein gravity rather than assuming a separate classical force.

Black-hole entropy becomes countable

For specified supersymmetric extremal black holes, microscopic string and brane states reproduce \(S_{\rm BH}=A/(4G\hbar)\). The area law acquires statistical meaning.[5]

Geometry can determine particle physics

Gauge groups, matter representations, Yukawa structures, and axion sectors descend from compactification data. Parameters become properties of hidden geometry rather than arbitrary four-dimensional entries.

Duality changes what spacetime means

Strong coupling can equal weak coupling, large radii can equal small radii, and a gravitational bulk can equal a nongravitational boundary theory. Apparently different descriptions can encode one physics.

Quantum gravity reaches observable sectors

Inflationary spectra, axions, cosmic superstrings, moduli histories, dark radiation, and gravitational waves provide routes from compactification geometry to measurement.

Open problems

Why the framework has not selected our universe

1. Vacuum selection remains open

Fluxes, branes, topology, and moduli admit an enormous set of four-dimensional effective theories. No accepted dynamical principle selects the vacuum, low-energy spectrum, or cosmological history realized in nature.[7, 14]

2. Low-energy predictions are not unique

Supersymmetry, extra dimensions, axions, cosmic strings, and moduli can occur over wide ranges or disappear from accessible energies. Their absence excludes models, but usually leaves other compactifications intact.[7, 11, 12]

3. Cosmology multiplies the choices

Compactification, stabilization, supersymmetry breaking, initial conditions, inflation, and reheating must all be specified before a primordial spectrum is obtained. Different controlled choices can agree with the same CMB data.[7, 10]

4. De Sitter control is disputed

Positive vacuum energy is central to our observed universe, yet explicit constructions rely on layered approximations whose mutual control remains contested. Swampland conjectures sharpen the issue without settling it.[8, 9, 13]

5. Holography is best understood in the wrong asymptotics

AdS/CFT gives extraordinary nonperturbative control for anti-de Sitter boundaries. Cosmology is approximately de Sitter and time-dependent, where no equally complete formulation is established.[4, 7]

6. No distinctive signal has been confirmed

Current observations constrain string-motivated models, but none requires a string origin. The framework has produced many possible signatures without yet producing one empirically unavoidable signature.[7, 15, 16]

Evidence and tests

What “finally testable” means

Quantum-gravity result

\(S_{\rm micro}=S_{\rm BH}=A/(4G\hbar)\)

Strominger and Vafa counted the microstates of a class of five-dimensional extremal black holes and recovered the semiclassical area law. Later work extended the result widely, though not to every astrophysical black hole.[5]

Nonperturbative structure

Gravity equals a boundary quantum field theory

AdS/CFT supplies a precise dictionary between string theory in specified anti-de Sitter backgrounds and conformal field theories without gravity. Thousands of quantitative checks support the correspondence; direct experimental evidence for the duality as a description of our universe is absent.[4]

New experimental bridge

A detected axion could eliminate compactifications

Jain and collaborators built Bayesian priors over Calabi–Yau moduli spaces and showed how a future axion measurement could update the allowed geometry and topology. Their forward model is a blueprint for testing ensembles of string compactifications; it is not a present detection or a universal test of string theory.[15]

Observable searches

Axions, cosmic superstrings, and primordial relics

Axion haloscopes, CMB surveys, pulsar timing, and gravitational-wave observatories now probe regions of string-motivated parameter space. A discovery would still need correlations or coupling relations that distinguish a compactification from a non-string effective model.[11, 12, 16]

Missing calculation

From the framework to one prior over universes

A decisive programme must define the admissible ensemble, its measure, cosmological initial conditions, and observer-independent selection rule before measurements are applied. Without that prior, surviving models can always inherit probability from choices made after the fact.

The questions

What string theory answers—and leaves open

Gravity

A consistent perturbative quantum gravity is built into the closed-string spectrum, and holography supplies nonperturbative definitions in important backgrounds. A complete formulation for realistic de Sitter cosmology remains open.[2–4]
Singularities

Strings resolve or reinterpret many orbifold, conifold, and black-hole singularities, and several string cosmologies replace the Big Bang. No general theorem removes every cosmological or black-hole singularity.[7]
Expansion

Explicit sectors can drive inflation and late acceleration. The choice of inflaton, potential, vacuum, and positive-energy construction is not uniquely selected by the framework.[8–10]
Beginning

Pre-Big-Bang, brane, ekpyrotic, string-gas, and holographic histories offer alternatives to a singular beginning. Their initial state and observational realization remain model-dependent.[7]
Matter

Compactifications can produce chiral gauge sectors, axions, hidden particles, and dark matter candidates. No compactification has been empirically selected as the unique origin of the Standard Model and cosmic dark matter.
Constants

Couplings and masses can become functions of geometry, flux, and stabilized moduli. The landscape turns that possibility into a prediction only after a vacuum-selection rule or defensible statistical measure is supplied.[14, 15]

What comes next

Turn conditional tests into a cumulative verdict

String theory has accomplished things no rival has matched in one framework. It contains quantum gravity, unifies apparently different theories through duality, gives black-hole entropy a microscopic origin, and supplies nonperturbative control through holography. Those are results about quantum gravity even if the final cosmology is still missing.

The empirical problem is selection. A particular compactification can predict an axion, a cosmic-string tension, an inflationary spectrum, or a supersymmetric particle; another compactification can omit it. The new Bayesian work begins to turn that multiplicity into a statistical object that observations can update. Its success will depend on whether the ensemble and its prior arise from the theory rather than from the tractable subset available to computation.

The next decisive advance is therefore not another isolated signature. It is a compactification programme that fixes enough of the low-energy world to make several correlated forecasts at once—particle content, axion couplings, inflation, relic abundances, and vacuum energy—then survives all of them. A framework that can lose probability faster than its model space expands will finally be testable in the ordinary scientific sense.
