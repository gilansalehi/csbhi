The point of contact

Professor Mannheim, your recent conversation with Curt Jaimungal returned repeatedly to an idea that also sits at the center of this project: galactic dynamics cannot be separated from the global universe. Your conformal-gravity programme makes that statement dynamical. The Robertson–Walker background and its inhomogeneities appear in the rest frame of a galaxy as universal linear and quadratic contributions to its rotation curve.

Conformal Scaling of Black Hole Interiors reaches the same local–global interface through a different geometric proposal. Its starting point is the reciprocal relation \(a=1/r\), connecting black-hole infall to interior cosmological expansion. Applied recursively at galactic scale, the proposal identifies a galaxy's global acceleration scale with the inverse radius of its cosmological horizon, \(A=c^2/R_{\rm hor}\). A separate symmetry argument then selects the deep-regime square-root form \(g_{\rm mix}=\kappa\sqrt{g_NA}\).

If the relevant boundary is the contemporaneous apparent horizon, \(R_{\rm hor}=c/H(z)\), the unknown coefficient cancels from the redshift ratio: \[ \boxed{\frac{g_\dagger(z)}{g_\dagger(0)}=\frac{H(z)}{H_0}}. \]

The first intermediate-redshift measurement already tests the direction and magnitude of this result. MUSE-DARK III reports \(g_\dagger(z\sim1)/g_\dagger(0)\approx1.98\pm0.45\); the parameter-free correspondence gives \(H(1)/H_0=1.76\), while a fixed scale gives \(1\). The measurement is consistent with the correspondence and lies roughly \(2\sigma\) above the fixed value, although one model-dependent data point does not decide the question.

This is a correspondence with a falsifiable consequence, not yet a weak-field derivation. It makes the relation to conformal gravity especially useful: your theory already contains a complete field-equation route by which global cosmology acts inside galaxies. The question is whether the reciprocal result is compatible with that structure, ruled out by it, or already present in another form.

Your recent RAR analysis predicts a different quantity—a low-\(g_{\rm obs}\) acceleration floor fixed by cosmology—through conformal gravity's linear and quadratic potentials. Does that floor remain fixed at earlier epochs, or does the full conformal cosmology make it evolve with \(H(z)\)? The answer would either expose a shared prediction reached by independent routes or provide a clean observational distinction between them.

Two routes from cosmology to galaxies

The overlap is physical, but the quantities should not be conflated. In particular, the low-acceleration floor recently emphasized in conformal gravity is not automatically the same observable as the RAR transition scale \(g_\dagger\). Conformal gravity obtains its rotation curves from local and cosmological linear potentials plus a global quadratic term; CSBHI proposes a separate square-root mixing law. The packet asks how those genuinely different mechanisms compare rather than recasting conformal gravity as MOND.

Questions raised

I am especially interested in the relation between the two constructions:

  • Does conformal gravity predict a redshift dependence for its universal linear term or its low-acceleration floor?
  • Are that floor and the RAR transition scale physically distinct, as this packet assumes?
  • Can the reciprocal local–global mixing law arise from the conformal-gravity field equations, or does their structure exclude it?
  • Does conformal gravity identify which cosmological boundary—a contemporaneous apparent horizon, an event horizon, or spatial curvature—sets the galactic scale?

A correction at any of these points would materially improve the programme. A connection to an existing result in conformal gravity would be equally valuable.
