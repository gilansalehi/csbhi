The point of contact

Professor Mannheim, your recent conversation with Curt Jaimungal emphasized an idea that also sits at the center of this project: galactic dynamics cannot be separated from the global universe. Your conformal-gravity programme gives that idea a complete dynamical form. The Robertson–Walker background and its inhomogeneities appear in the rest frame of a galaxy as universal linear and quadratic contributions to its rotation curve.

Conformal Scaling of Black Hole Interiors reaches the same local–global interface through a different geometric proposal. Its starting point is the reciprocal relation \(a=1/r\), connecting black-hole infall to interior cosmological expansion. Applied recursively at galactic scale, the proposal identifies a galaxy's global acceleration scale with the inverse radius of its cosmological horizon, \(A=c^2/R_{\rm hor}\). A separate symmetry argument then selects the deep-regime square-root form \(g_{\rm mix}=\kappa\sqrt{g_NA}\).

If the relevant boundary is the contemporaneous apparent horizon, \(R_{\rm hor}=c/H(z)\), the unknown coefficient cancels from the redshift ratio: \[ \boxed{\frac{g_\dagger(z)}{g_\dagger(0)}=\frac{H(z)}{H_0}}. \]

A recent intermediate-redshift measurement already tests the direction and magnitude of this result. MUSE-DARK III reports \(g_\dagger(z\sim1)/g_\dagger(0)\approx1.98\pm0.45\); the parameter-free correspondence gives \(H(1)/H_0=1.76\), while a fixed scale gives \(1\). The measurement is consistent with the correspondence and lies roughly \(2\sigma\) above the fixed value, although one model-dependent data point does not decide the question.

CSBHI presently reaches this result as a correspondence with a falsifiable consequence rather than through a complete weak-field derivation. This makes the relation to conformal gravity especially valuable: your theory already supplies a field-equation route by which global cosmology acts inside galaxies. I would be interested to know whether the reciprocal result is compatible with that structure or already appears there in another form.

Your recent RAR analysis predicts a different quantity—a low-\(g_{\rm obs}\) acceleration floor fixed by cosmology—through conformal gravity's linear and quadratic potentials. I am especially curious whether that floor remains fixed at earlier epochs or whether the full conformal cosmology makes it evolve with \(H(z)\). Either answer would be illuminating: the two routes may share a prediction, or observation may distinguish between them.

Two routes from cosmology to galaxies

The physical overlap becomes most informative when the quantities remain distinct. In particular, the low-acceleration floor recently emphasized in conformal gravity is not automatically the same observable as the RAR transition scale \(g_\dagger\). Conformal gravity obtains its rotation curves from local and cosmological linear potentials plus a global quadratic term; CSBHI proposes a separate square-root mixing law. The comparison below therefore places the two mechanisms side by side on their own terms.

Starting point A locally conformal, fourth-order metric theory with a dimensionless gravitational coupling. The geometric ansatz \(a=1/r\), followed by Einstein–Friedmann dynamics in the reciprocal interior.
Local–global link Local luminous matter and the exterior universe contribute distinct terms to one galactic potential. A scale-free exchange mixes local baryonic acceleration with a global horizon acceleration.
Galaxy law \(v^2=v_{\rm LOC}^2+\gamma_0c^2R/2-\kappa_{\rm CG}c^2R^2\), with universal cosmological terms. \(g_{\rm mix}=\kappa_{\rm GD}\sqrt{g_NcH}\), conditional on the stated symmetry and horizon correspondence.
Current empirical claim A cosmologically fixed floor in \(g_{\rm obs}\) near \(10^{-10.86}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\). An RAR transition scale whose ratio rises as \(H(z)/H_0\) if the apparent horizon is selected.
Question for comparison How does the full cosmology determine the redshift dependence of the universal galactic terms? Which horizon the completed weak-field geometry selects, and what fixes the absolute coefficient?

Points for discussion

I would be grateful for your perspective on the relation between the two constructions:

  • How does conformal gravity determine the redshift dependence of its universal linear term or its low-acceleration floor?
  • Would you regard that floor and the RAR transition scale as physically distinct quantities?
  • Might the reciprocal local–global mixing law arise as an effective limit of the conformal-gravity field equations?
  • Which cosmological structure in conformal gravity—a contemporaneous apparent horizon, an event horizon, or spatial curvature—most naturally sets the galactic scale?

Any guidance on these questions would materially improve the programme, and a connection to an existing result in conformal gravity would be especially valuable.
