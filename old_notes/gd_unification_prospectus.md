# Prospectus: Geometric Duality as a Unifying Regime for Horizon Gravity, de Sitter Geometry, and Galactic Dynamics

## Mission Statement

This project advances **Geometric Duality (GD)** as a unifying geometric framework for several major clues in modern gravitational physics that are usually treated separately: black-hole interior duality, de Sitter embedding, horizon thermodynamics, the MOND acceleration scale, the radial acceleration relation, and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation.

The central thesis is not that prior programs were “wrong,” but that they each illuminated different faces of a single underlying structure. In this view, the observable universe is naturally described as a **throat-centered spacetime** whose privileged horizon, Painlevé–Gullstrand flow, and 5D de Sitter embedding together provide the missing bridge between local baryonic gravity and cosmological curvature.

## Core Claim

The guiding claim of the project is:

> **The galaxy-scale anomaly, the empirical MOND scale \(a_0\sim cH\), the de Sitter horizon, the thermodynamic interpretation of gravity, and black-hole/cosmology duality are all manifestations of one underlying geometric regime.**

Geometric Duality supplies the bridge because it already provides:

- a **PG rewriting** of flat FLRW,
- a **preferred shell / horizon** with \(r=a(\tau)\),
- a **5D de Sitter throat** interpretation,
- a geometric route to \(\Lambda\sim 3/L^2\),
- and a natural cosmic acceleration scale \(a_0\sim cH\).

The long-term aim is to show that the MOND law and related galaxy phenomenology arise from **curvature mixing at the throat** rather than from dark matter or from a purely phenomenological force-law modification.

## Prior Visionaries and the Parts of the Elephant They Identified

### Willem de Sitter

de Sitter provided the ambient geometric backdrop: the 4D de Sitter spacetime as a hyperboloid embedded in 5D Minkowski space. In the GD framework, this becomes not just a standard cosmological background, but the natural geometric setting in which the cosmic horizon is interpreted as the **waist / throat** of the hyperboloid.

### Mordehai Milgrom

Milgrom identified the correct empirical scale: galaxy dynamics deviate from Newtonian expectations near an acceleration scale \(a_0\sim cH\). He also identified the deep-MOND scaling law and the significance of scale-freeness in the low-acceleration regime. GD agrees that Milgrom found a genuine clue, but seeks to derive the scale and the law from spacetime geometry rather than postulating a modified force law.

### Stacy McGaugh

McGaugh mapped the phenomenology with exceptional clarity: the radial acceleration relation and the baryonic Tully–Fisher law strongly suggest that galaxy dynamics are tightly tied to baryons plus a universal low-acceleration scale. In the GD program, these relations are not dismissed as fit formulae; they become target observables that any geometric derivation must recover.

### Erik Verlinde

Verlinde correctly recognized that the de Sitter horizon and entropy are relevant to galaxy dynamics. His program treats the cosmic horizon as an entropic reservoir whose response to baryonic matter produces an extra acceleration. GD agrees with the horizon insight and with the importance of \(a_0\sim cH\), but seeks to provide the geometric bridge that explains *why* the horizon matters: the horizon is not merely a thermodynamic screen, but a privileged **matching surface / throat geometry** in an embedded spacetime.

### T. Padmanabhan

Padmanabhan recognized that gravity, cosmology, and horizon thermodynamics are deeply linked, and repeatedly treated spacetime dynamics as emergent from holographic or thermodynamic bookkeeping at causal boundaries. GD incorporates that insight, but reinterprets the relevant boundary as a concrete geometric object: the same throat that appears in the de Sitter embedding and in the PG duality.

### Other Black-Hole / PG / LTB Contributors

Painlevé, Gullstrand, Lemaître, Tolman, Bondi, and the junction-condition literature each contribute essential technical pieces: horizon-regular slicings, infall coordinates, inhomogeneous dust collapse, and smooth matching. GD stands on these shoulders, combining them into a single cosmological interpretation.

## Current State of the Program

The project already has several working components.

### 1. Background GD paper

The foundational paper rewrites flat FLRW in Painlevé–Gullstrand form, identifies the shell relation \(r=a(\tau)\), and derives the infall structure

\[
F(r,\tau)=H(\tau)r,
\]

with the shell-specific form

\[
F(r)=\sqrt{\frac{8\pi G}{3}\bigl(\rho_{m,0}r^3+\rho_\Lambda\bigr)}.
\]

It also ties the interior horizon to a de Sitter-like late-time geometry and gives a numerical geometric estimate for \(\Lambda\).

### 2. Embedding note (Version C)

This note shows that the PG flow metric arises directly from the 5D de Sitter hyperboloid when written in flat slicing and converted to areal radius. The result is the de Sitter-sector derivation of

\[
F=Hr.
\]

### 3. Infall note (Version A)

This note shows that the GD infall function arises naturally once the Friedmann equation is rewritten in areal-radius / PG language. In effect, it explains how the matter and vacuum terms enter the flow field.

### 4. Throat-curvature mixing note

This note provides the first unification sketch for MOND-like dynamics by identifying two inverse-length scales at the throat,

\[
k_M\sim \frac{g_N}{c^2},
\qquad
k_H\sim \frac{H}{c},
\]

and proposing the simplest mixed scalar acceleration

\[
g_{\rm eff}\sim c^2\sqrt{k_Mk_H}\sim \sqrt{g_N cH}.
\]

This reproduces the deep-MOND \(1/r\) law and the baryonic Tully–Fisher scaling.

## The Hardest Open Problems

The program is now coherent enough that the remaining obstacles are sharply identifiable.

### Problem 1: Tensorial derivation of curvature mixing

The deepest unresolved question is whether the heuristic throat-curvature law

\[
g_{\rm eff}\sim \sqrt{g_N cH}
\]

can be derived from a genuine geometric invariant or field equation.

The likely candidates are:

- extrinsic curvature of the 4D GD hypersurface in 5D,
- Gauss–Codazzi relations,
- shell/junction invariants at the throat,
- or a perturbative weak-field limit around the embedded background.

This is the hard center of the project.

### Problem 2: The interpolation law

Deep-MOND scaling is not enough. A viable framework must explain how systems interpolate between

\[
g\approx g_N
\quad (g_N\gg a_0)
\]

and

\[
g\approx \sqrt{g_N a_0}
\quad (g_N\ll a_0).
\]

The question is whether GD geometry selects a natural interpolation function rather than merely reproducing the asymptotic low-acceleration limit.

### Problem 3: Lensing

Any alternative to particle dark matter must address lensing. Since GD is geometric from the outset, it has a better chance than purely phenomenological modified-force programs, but this remains unproven.

The required deliverable is an effective metric seen by null geodesics that reproduces galaxy-galaxy lensing and, if possible, strong-lensing constraints.

### Problem 4: Clusters

Cluster phenomenology is historically one of the toughest problems for MOND-like ideas. The GD program must determine whether:

- the throat-curvature effect strengthens in deeper environments,
- an additional geometric sector appears at cluster scale,
- or some residual unseen matter is still required.

A clean answer here would dramatically increase the credibility of the whole framework.

### Problem 5: Cosmological perturbations and structure formation

The background GD picture is strong, but a replacement or completion of \(\Lambda\)CDM must address:

- scalar perturbations,
- structure growth,
- matter power spectra,
- and the CMB acoustic structure.

The most natural route is to interpret perturbations as fluctuations of the embedded 4D hypersurface inside the 5D ambient space.

### Problem 6: Thermodynamic reconciliation

To complete the unification with Verlinde and Padmanabhan, one must show why the throat geometry naturally acquires a thermodynamic and informational interpretation.

The goal is not to discard horizon entropy but to derive its relevance geometrically: the throat should emerge as both

- a geometric matching locus, and
- a causal / information-theoretic boundary.

### Problem 7: Off-shell completion

The original GD derivation of the shell-specific infall function is exact only on the matching shell. A full interior theory requires the off-shell extension, likely through LTB or a related inhomogeneous solution. Without this step, the framework remains suggestive but incomplete.

## The Main Research Questions

The entire program can be reduced to a handful of sharp questions:

1. **Can the geometric mean law**
   \[
   g_{\rm eff}\sim \sqrt{g_N cH}
   \]
   **be derived tensorially from throat geometry?**

2. **What is the unique or natural interpolation law** selected by GD between Newtonian and deep-throat regimes?

3. **What metric do photons see**, and does it reproduce observed lensing?

4. **How do local baryonic perturbations modify the embedded hypersurface?**

5. **How do thermodynamic horizon arguments arise as the coarse-grained face of the same throat geometry?**

These are the decisive tests.

## Why This Program Is Worth Pursuing

The project is worth pursuing because it does not merely add another competitor to an already crowded landscape. It attempts something rarer and more valuable: a synthesis.

It says, in effect:

- Milgrom found the right scale.
- McGaugh found the right empirical law.
- Verlinde found the right horizon clue.
- Padmanabhan found the right thermodynamic clue.
- de Sitter supplied the right ambient geometry.
- GD may be the framework that explains why all of those clues coexist.

If successful, the program would not just offer an alternative to dark matter. It would explain why so many different approaches were all converging on the same scales and structures.

## Closing Statement

This prospectus advances GD not as an isolated conjecture, but as a candidate **unification layer** for several of the strongest ideas in modern gravitational physics. The working hypothesis is that horizon thermodynamics, de Sitter embedding, PG flow, black-hole/cosmology duality, and galactic low-acceleration phenomenology are not separate mysteries, but projections of the same deeper throat geometry.

The decisive challenge now is to turn that synthesis from a compelling picture into a predictive mathematical framework.
