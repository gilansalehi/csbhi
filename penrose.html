A note for Sir Roger Penrose

The Successor Aeon as a Black-Hole Interior

Conformal Cyclic Cosmology as the interior geometry of black holes.

August 2026

Dear Sir Roger,

Conformal Cyclic Cosmology has, I believe, a realization inside black-hole physics that removes the commitments it must otherwise carry at the crossover. The identification is one line: the successor aeon is the interior of a black hole in the present one.

For an observer crossing the horizon, the interior spatial scale and the exterior areal radius are reciprocal, \(a=1/r\). The parent's descent to \(r\to0\) is the child's expansion to \(a\to\infty\): what is a singularity in the exterior description is the smooth conformal future in the interior one. Your conformal reciprocation \(\Omega\to1/\Omega\) is this map, carried at a horizon rather than imposed at the boundary.

The low-Weyl beginning then follows from collapse, not from selection. Homogeneous collapse — Oppenheimer–Snyder — has a Friedmann interior whose Weyl tensor vanishes identically even as its density diverges. A universe born from such a collapse inherits a smooth start, and the previous aeon whose future seeds it is, concretely, the parent black hole. Because a smooth universe is what forms the smooth overdensities that collapse into smooth children, low initial Weyl is a fixed point of the succession rather than an improbable choice. That is the Weyl Curvature Hypothesis, obtained as a property of the formation process rather than a condition placed upon it.

Three commitments Conformal Cyclic Cosmology carries at the crossover then become unnecessary. Matter need not become massless, because it crosses by falling through a horizon rather than by reaching a null infinity as radiation. Black holes need not have evaporated, because they are the aeons; the boundary continues through them. And the information need not be destroyed: where you are content to let the phase space contract at the singularity, one may instead let it re-open as the child's.

What secures the last point is a single calculation. On the reciprocal interior the residual Weyl dilutes as \(a^{-2}\), which is its conformal weight; under the compactification the rescaled Weyl is therefore finite at the boundary while the physical Weyl vanishes. That is exactly the condition that lets a black hole sit on the crossover without the divergence that would otherwise require it gone — the property you secure by evaporation, secured instead by the geometry.

One conjecture remains: that the geometry of the succession forces Weyl smoothness on every aeon by self-consistency, rather than fixing it as a fine-tuned parameter. This is the claim I am now working to prove, in a small geometric program inspired by yours — the Conformal Scaling of Black Hole Interiors (gilansalehi.com/csbhi). More than anything, I hope you find it worth your time.

Best,
Gilan Salehi
