The Initial Conditions Problem ΛCDM requires a highly special early universe: smooth, nearly flat, low-entropy, and tuned for later structure formation. Homogeneous collapse gives the child a Weyl-free interior — an exact low-Weyl anchor (Oppenheimer–Snyder) — so Penrose's low-Weyl condition looks like an output of formation rather than a tuned start. The Arrow of Time Standard cosmology describes a preferred temporal direction but does not fully explain why the universe begins in a low-entropy state. Proposed reading: each universe is born far below an inherited entropy ceiling — the parent horizon's entropy reappearing as the child's available phase space (\(S_{\rm BH}=S_{\rm dS}\), conditional on the matched \(\Lambda\)) — giving an arrow without destroying information. The exchange remains interpretive. candidate mechanism gravitational entropy
The Entropy of Cosmic Horizons Horizon thermodynamics assigns entropy and temperature to causal boundaries, but its relation to cosmic expansion remains conceptually deep. The Nature of the Big Bang Standard cosmology extrapolates backward toward a hot dense beginning, while classical GR raises the specter of an initial singularity. The beginning is read as the interior of a parent collapse — the child born at collapse, not at a point of origin. Cosmic Topology and the Observable Patch ΛCDM describes the observable universe extremely well, but the relation between our finite causal patch and any larger global geometry remains open.
