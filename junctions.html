Abstract

The reciprocal interior has a boundary problem.

The primary CSBHI paper introduced the reciprocal ansatz \(a(\tau)=1/r(\tau)\), while the first paper in this technical sequence derived the field equations of Alice's spatially flat matter–radiation–\(\Lambda\) interior. Those results relate black-hole infall to cosmological expansion, but they do not join Alice's interior four-geometry to Bob's exterior. Here we isolate that missing junction and test its timelike, null, thermodynamic, and dynamical limits.

A comoving timelike FLRW boundary has shared areal radius \(\bar R=aR_0\). Matching it to Kottler geometry fixes the exterior mass parameter. When the cosmological constant agrees on both sides, its volume term cancels exactly: \[ M_K=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho\,\bar R^3. \] A stress-free static match further requires vanishing non-vacuum pressure. Dust plus a shared cosmological constant therefore matches smoothly; radiation supplies the remaining pressure residual, which decays as \(a^{-1}\) in the required mass.

In the direct stationary Schwarzschild–FLRW null geometry studied here, continuity of the spherical induced metric and the null condition give \[ R_S=R_A=\frac{c}{H_A}. \] If the junction remains stationary, Alice's side must be de Sitter and \(\Lambda=3/R_S^2\). The transverse derivatives do not match under the simplest Schwarzschild–de Sitter soldering. Their jump is fixed, however: at Alice's apparent horizon it equals her interior work density projected from volume to area, \[ \Delta\kappa=\frac{4\pi G R_A}{c^2}W, \qquad \gamma\,dA=W\,dV. \]

Finally, Alice's cosmological event-horizon radius \(B(\tau)\) obeys the exact kinematic law \[ H_A=\frac{c+\dot B}{B}. \] The measured expansion history therefore reconstructs the boundary history that a completed parent geometry must produce independently. The junction problem has not disappeared; it has been reduced to a precise target: derive a parent-side area history that maps to \(B(\tau)\) and a finite throat whose transverse geometry carries the work term above. In the stationary de Sitter limit, the curvature radius, causal-horizon radius, and junction radius coincide. Away from that limit they are distinct, and their relationship must be derived by the completed throat geometry.

The Missing Bridge

GD1 begins with two ingredients [1]. Bob assigns Alice a horizon-normalized areal coordinate \(r(\tau)\), decreasing from \(r=1\) toward \(r=0\), while Alice's interior scale factor is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocal} \boxed{a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)}}. \end{equation}

Alice's proposed interior metric is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:alice-metric} ds_A^2 = -d\tau^2 + a(\tau)^2 \left[ \frac{dR^2}{1-kR^2} + R^2d\Omega^2 \right]. \end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:reciprocal} is a dictionary between Bob's infall description and Alice's spatial scale. Equation \eqref{eq:alice-metric} specifies Alice's intrinsic geometry. Neither equation supplies the four-dimensional region that connects them.

That region cannot be replaced by a change of coordinates. Vacuum Schwarzschild has anisotropic Kantowski–Sachs evolution and nonzero Weyl curvature, whereas FLRW is isotropic and conformally flat [2, 3, 4]. A regular scalar conformal rescaling preserves whether the Weyl tensor vanishes. The reciprocal scalar may be smooth at \(r=1\), but smoothness of one scalar does not make the joined four-metric differentiable.

The horizon map must therefore provide three things [19]:

  1. a common intrinsic geometry on the matching surface;
  2. the transverse or extrinsic geometry on both sides; and
  3. the surface or distributed stress-energy required when those derivatives differ.

The calculations below determine how much of that programme already follows from standard junction geometry, and where new geometry must enter.

Surfaces, Radii, and the Horizon Map

Several geometrically different objects meet in this problem. They must remain distinct even when their radii happen to agree.

Symbol Object Role
\(R_S\) Bob's Schwarzschild scale Parent black-hole radius in the isolated exterior limit
\(R_A=c/H_A\) Alice's apparent-horizon radius Local marginal surface selected by the flat Friedmann geometry
\(B(\tau)\) Alice's cosmological event-horizon radius Global null boundary fixed by the complete future history
\(R_\Lambda=c/H_\Lambda\) de Sitter curvature radius Invariant asymptotic curvature scale
\(\Sigma\) Matching history Null surface, timelike layer, or finite throat to be derived

In exact de Sitter space, \(R_A\), \(B\), and \(R_\Lambda\) have the same numerical value. They remain a quasi-local marginal radius, a global causal radius, and a curvature radius respectively [13, 18]. At finite matter or radiation density, \(R_A\neq B\).

The same distinction applies to the matching history. A timelike worldtube cannot be a null event horizon. A null junction can coincide with a horizon, but its degenerate induced metric and transverse curvature require the null-shell formalism rather than the ordinary timelike Israel equations [5, 6, 7]. We therefore analyze the timelike and null limits separately.

The Timelike Junction

The first condition fixes the shared radius

Place a comoving timelike boundary at \(R=R_0\) in Equation \eqref{eq:alice-metric}. Its induced metric is

\[ ds_\Sigma^2=-d\tau^2+\bar R(\tau)^2d\Omega^2, \qquad \bar R(\tau)=a(\tau)R_0. \]

The area \(4\pi\bar R^2\) is intrinsic to the boundary. Any spherically symmetric exterior joined to it must assign the same areal radius. This shell identity is exact:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:shell-identity} \boxed{\bar R_\Sigma(\tau)=a(\tau)R_0}. \end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:shell-identity} is not the reciprocal ansatz. Alice's shell radius \(\bar R\) grows with \(a\); Bob's normalized trajectory label \(r=1/a\) decreases. Confusing them erases the junction problem before it has been solved.

Angular matching fixes the exterior mass

Take the exterior to be Kottler,

\[ ds_+^2 = -f(\bar R)dT^2 + \frac{d\bar R^2}{f(\bar R)} + \bar R^2d\Omega^2, \qquad f(\bar R) = 1-\frac{2GM_K}{\bar R}-\frac{\Lambda_+\bar R^2}{3}. \]

Let the FLRW and Kottler cosmological constants be \(\Lambda_-\) and \(\Lambda_+\), while \(\rho\) and \(p\) denote non-vacuum density and pressure. Continuity of the angular extrinsic curvature gives [8, 9, 21, 22]

\begin{equation}\label{eq:kottler-mass} \boxed{ M_K = \frac{4\pi}{3}\rho\,\bar R^3 + \frac{\Lambda_--\Lambda_+}{6G}\bar R^3 }. \end{equation}

When the same cosmological constant belongs to both geometries, its contribution cancels:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:vacuum-cancellation} \boxed{ M_K=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho\,\bar R^3 } \qquad(\Lambda_-=\Lambda_+). \end{equation}

The cancellation is geometric. The Misner–Sharp mass on each side contains the same \(\Lambda\bar R^3/(6G)\) term. Comparing the complete interior mass with only the bare Kottler parameter counts vacuum curvature on one side and omits it on the other.

Temporal matching fixes the pressure

Continuity of the temporal extrinsic curvature adds

\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressure-condition} \boxed{ p=\frac{\Lambda_--\Lambda_+}{8\pi G} }. \end{equation}

For a shared cosmological constant, \(p=0\). A comoving dust–\(\Lambda\) region therefore joins smoothly to a static Kottler exterior, recovering the Oppenheimer–Snyder and Einstein–Straus matching structure [10, 11, 12]. Radiation does not satisfy the pressure condition. For independently conserved matter and radiation,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:radiation-residual} \boxed{ M_K(a)=M_m+\frac{M_{r0}}{a} = M_m+M_{r0}r }. \end{equation}

The radiation obstruction decays continuously as \(a\to\infty\), or \(r\to0\). The local timelike junction conditions therefore close exactly for dust plus a shared \(\Lambda\), and approach that result in a controlled way when radiation is retained. This local match does not construct the reciprocal map or extend the boundary through a null black-hole horizon.

The Null Junction

The first condition fixes the horizon rate

Use ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates for an isolated Schwarzschild exterior,

\[ ds_+^2 = -\left(1-\frac{R_S}{R}\right)dv^2 +2\,dv\,dR + R^2d\Omega^2. \]

The induced metric on its future event horizon \(R=R_S\) is \(q_+=R_S^2d\Omega^2\). On Alice's side, an ingoing radial null hypersurface \(\chi=\chi(\tau)\) obeys

\[ \dot\chi=-\frac{c}{a}, \qquad \mathcal R(\tau)=a(\tau)\chi(\tau), \qquad \dot{\mathcal R}=H_A\mathcal R-c. \]

Continuity of the induced metric along a stationary Schwarzschild horizon requires \(\mathcal R=R_S\) and \(\dot{\mathcal R}=0\). Therefore [20]

\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-null} \boxed{ H_A=\frac{c}{R_S}, \qquad R_A=\frac{c}{H_A}=R_S }. \end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:first-null} follows from nullness and the first junction condition. It does not assume a Friedmann density. If the identification persists along the stationary horizon, \(H_A\) is constant. Einstein's equations then select vacuum pressure:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-vacuum} \boxed{ \dot H_A=0, \qquad P=-\rho c^2, \qquad \Lambda=\frac{3}{R_S^2} }. \end{equation}

Here \(\rho\) is total mass-equivalent density, so \(\rho c^2\) is the corresponding energy density.

The direct stationary null match therefore reaches the exact de Sitter endpoint already identified in GD1. It does not reproduce the finite-time matter and radiation eras.

The second condition finds the obstruction

In the stationary limit, write the Schwarzschild and de Sitter radial functions as

\[ f_+(R)=1-\frac{R_S}{R}, \qquad f_-(R)=1-\frac{R^2}{R_S^2}. \]

Both vanish at \(R_S\), so the intrinsic horizon geometry matches. Their transverse derivatives do not:

\[ f_+'(R_S)=\frac{1}{R_S}, \qquad f_-'(R_S)=-\frac{2}{R_S}. \]

Under the same-radial-orientation static soldering, the Barrabès–Israel transverse curvature has

\begin{equation}\label{eq:null-jump} \boxed{ [\mathcal C_{vv}] = \frac{3}{2R_S}, \qquad [\kappa] = \frac{3c^2}{2R_S} }. \end{equation}

Reversing the orientation to map a future black-hole horizon to a future cosmological horizon redistributes the mismatch between longitudinal and angular transverse curvature; it does not remove it. The split also depends on the normalization of the null generators. The invariant conclusion is the presence of distributional curvature in the abrupt piecewise geometry.

The Transverse Work Term

The derivative jump in Equation \eqref{eq:null-jump} is not an arbitrary extra source. Horizon thermodynamics identifies the interior quantity it represents [13, 14, 15, 23].

For Alice's spatially flat FLRW geometry, define the apparent-horizon radius, deceleration parameter, and work density by

\[ R_A=\frac{c}{H_A}, \qquad q=-\frac{\ddot a}{aH_A^2}, \qquad W=\frac{\epsilon-P}{2}. \]

The Hayward–Kodama surface gravity at \(R_A\) is

\[ \kappa_A=-\frac{cH_A}{2}(1-q), \]

while a vacuum Schwarzschild horizon of the same instantaneous radius has \(\kappa_B=cH_A/2\). Their signed difference is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:work-jump} \boxed{ \Delta\kappa \equiv \kappa_B-\kappa_A = \frac{cH_A}{2}(2-q) = \frac{4\pi G R_A}{c^2}W }. \end{equation}

Define the corresponding effective tension by \(\gamma=c^2\Delta\kappa/(8\pi G)\). Since \(dV/dA=R_A/2\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:work-projection} \boxed{ \gamma=\frac{R_AW}{2}, \qquad \gamma\,dA=W\,dV }. \end{equation}

In the stationary de Sitter limit, Equation \eqref{eq:work-projection} reproduces the same-orientation null-shell tension in Equation \eqref{eq:null-jump}. The sharp shell compresses the interior pressure–volume work onto a surface. A finite throat may distribute the same work through a region of changing curvature.

This accounting explains the transverse term; it does not make the abrupt metric \(C^1\). A completed throat must reproduce Equation \eqref{eq:work-jump} through its Einstein tensor and conservation law rather than attaching the identity to an otherwise discontinuous geometry.

The Dynamic Causal Boundary

The apparent horizon \(R_A\) carries the local Friedmann constraint. Alice's cosmological event horizon carries her global future causal history. Its areal radius is [13, 18, 24]

\[ B(\tau) = a(\tau)c \int_\tau^\infty\frac{d\tau'}{a(\tau')}. \]

Differentiation gives the exact moving-boundary identity

\begin{equation}\label{eq:moving-boundary} \boxed{ \dot B=H_AB-c, \qquad H_A=\frac{c+\dot B}{B} }. \end{equation}

A second derivative gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-acceleration} \boxed{ \frac{\ddot a}{a} = \frac{\ddot B}{B} + \frac{c(c+\dot B)}{B^2} }. \end{equation}

Equations \eqref{eq:moving-boundary}–\eqref{eq:boundary-acceleration} turn any independently derived boundary history into a homogeneous expansion history. Running them backward from an observed \(H_A(a)\) reconstructs the target \(B(\tau)\); it does not predict it.

The local and global horizons are also thermodynamically distinct. Let \(x=B/R_A=H_AB/c\). Evaluating the apparent-horizon equilibrium identity on the event-horizon sphere leaves the exact residual [25]

\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonequilibrium} \boxed{ \dot{\mathcal Q}_{\rm neq} = -\frac{c^5}{G}x^2(1+qx) = \frac{c^5}{G}x^2\frac{\ddot B}{cH_A} }. \end{equation}

The residual vanishes when the event and apparent horizons settle into the stationary de Sitter limit. It is a geometric balance term, not a luminosity. Together, Equations \eqref{eq:work-projection} and \eqref{eq:nonequilibrium} give a finite throat two exact targets: carry the local transverse work and reproduce the global departure from equilibrium.

Parent-side dynamical-horizon balance laws already relate changing black-hole area to matter and gravitational flux [16]. They supply candidate input, not the map itself: the parent event horizon is null, while a quasi-local trapping or dynamical horizon can have a different causal character. The completed geometry must select the parent surface and derive how its area history becomes \(B(\tau)\).

The Recursive Parent Geometry

Isolated Schwarzschild omits the parent universe in which Bob's black hole forms. The simplest stationary correction is a Kottler exterior with parent cosmological constant \(\Lambda_P\). Let Alice's child de Sitter curvature radius be

\[ R_{\Lambda,C} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{\Lambda_C}}. \]

If this radius is required to be a root of Bob's parent Kottler function, then [26]

\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursive-root} \boxed{ \frac{M}{M_{\Lambda,C}} = 1-\frac{\Lambda_P}{\Lambda_C}, \qquad M_{\Lambda,C} = \frac{c^2R_{\Lambda,C}}{2G} }. \end{equation}

The sign of the Kottler derivative identifies the branch:

\[ \frac{\Lambda_P}{\Lambda_C}<\frac13 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{black-hole root}, \] \[ \frac{\Lambda_P}{\Lambda_C}=\frac13 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{degenerate Nariai root}, \] \[ \frac{\Lambda_P}{\Lambda_C}>\frac13 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{cosmological root}. \]

Equation \eqref{eq:recursive-root} is conditional on the common-root identification. It shows how parent curvature changes the isolated-Schwarzschild mass relation, but it does not derive which parent horizon maps to Alice's causal boundary.

More importantly, the Kottler correction does not erase the transverse problem. Under the standard static normalization, the parent and child radial derivatives still leave

\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursive-jump} \boxed{ [\kappa] = \frac{3c^2}{2R_{\Lambda,C}} \left( 1-\frac{\Lambda_P}{\Lambda_C} \right) }. \end{equation}

The recursive exterior solves a common-root problem, not the complete junction. It also lacks an asymptotically flat infinity that would fix the normalization of its static Killing field. Both the normalization and the transverse geometry must come from the completed parent–child construction.

Three Distinct Geometric Structures

The junction problem contains three objects that no single identity can collapse:

\[ L \;\leftrightarrow\; \mathcal H \;\leftrightarrow\; \Sigma. \]

  • \(L\) is the invariant de Sitter curvature scale.
  • \(\mathcal H\) is the relevant observer-causal null horizon.
  • \(\Sigma\) is the matching history or finite throat joining the parent and child descriptions.

In the stationary de Sitter limit, several associated radii coincide:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-radii} \boxed{ R_S=R_A=B=R_\Lambda=L } \qquad \text{(stationary de Sitter radii)}. \end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:stationary-radii} is an equality of radii in a symmetric limit, not an identity of geometric objects. Curvature, causal access, and a matching surface remain different mathematical structures.

At finite matter and radiation density, the apparent horizon and cosmological event horizon separate. Equation \eqref{eq:moving-boundary} governs the latter, Equation \eqref{eq:work-jump} governs the former, and the parent map must relate both to \(\Sigma\). The constructive target is therefore:

One throat geometry derives the curvature scale, the causal boundary, and the matching history as different aspects of the same solution.

The present calculations identify the stationary coincidence of radii and the differential data such a geometry must reproduce. They provide constraints for the construction, not the construction itself.

Discussion

The junction problem began as a broad objection: the reciprocal ansatz specifies Alice's scale but not the geometry connecting her to Bob. It now has a much narrower form.

The timelike route is not obstructed by a common cosmological constant. Vacuum curvature cancels from the Kottler mass condition, dust joins smoothly, and radiation leaves a decaying pressure term. The null route fixes the horizon rate without a density assumption and selects de Sitter when held stationary. Its failure occurs one derivative later, in the transverse curvature.

That derivative mismatch carries recognizable physics. At Alice's apparent horizon it is exactly her interior work density projected onto area. A sharp junction represents that work as surface stress; a smooth throat must distribute it geometrically. Meanwhile, Alice's cosmological event horizon provides a second, global condition through its motion and nonequilibrium residual.

These results rule out two shortcuts. First, smoothness of \(a=1/r\) cannot substitute for four-metric matching. Second, equality of horizon radii in de Sitter cannot be extended as literal equality through the matter and radiation eras. The apparent horizon, event horizon, and matching history carry different data.

They also identify the next constructive move. A candidate throat metric must interpolate between parent and child curvature, reproduce the induced spherical geometry, and generate the transverse work in Equation \eqref{eq:work-projection}. Its parent-side area law must then map to a child event-horizon history satisfying Equation \eqref{eq:moving-boundary}. Deriving those two facts from one metric would convert the reconstructed cosmological history into a prediction.

Conclusion

The reciprocal interior admits a clean local timelike dust–\(\Lambda\) junction and an exact intrinsic match in the stated direct null geometry. That null match fixes the Schwarzschild and apparent-horizon radii, while stationarity fixes the de Sitter curvature. The remaining transverse derivative is neither arbitrary nor hidden: it is the boundary form of Alice's interior work density.

A dynamical cosmological event horizon then extends the problem beyond the stationary limit. Its measured history is known, its kinematic relation to expansion is exact, and its departure from apparent-horizon equilibrium is calculable. What remains is to derive that history and its transverse work from the parent geometry.

The remaining task is a concrete construction problem: find one throat geometry whose distinct curvature, causal-boundary, and junction data reduce to the relations established here.
