How Collapse Becomes Cosmology

Formation and conformal scale transfer in Geometric Duality

GD1 asks us to view inward motion in a black hole and expansion in a child universe as two descriptions of one geometry. Here we follow that idea back to the moment the black hole forms.

You Are Here

In GD1, Alice falls through the event horizon of a black hole while Bob describes her motion using a horizon-normalized radial coordinate \(r\). At the horizon, \(r=1\). As Alice continues inward, \(r\rightarrow0\).

Geometric Duality assigns Alice's interior a reciprocal scale factor

\[ a=\frac1r. \]

Bob therefore describes a shrinking radial coordinate while Alice describes a growing spatial scale. Once that reciprocal branch has been established, a spatially flat FLRW metric and Einstein's equations give the familiar radiation, matter, and vacuum expansion histories.

But GD1 begins with a black hole that is already there. A child cosmology requires more than a mature reciprocal law. It requires a beginning.

How does an ordinary collapsing star produce the causal history, the nearly homogeneous geometry, and the scale evolution that Alice later experiences as a universe?

Repeat the Descent

Let Alice arrive while the parent black hole is forming. The event horizon has only just appeared near the center of the collapsing star. Alice crosses it and continues inward.

A little later, a second Alice repeats the journey. By then the event horizon has grown through more of the star. A third Alice follows later still. In the limit, a continuous family of freely falling observers samples the complete history of horizon formation.

Each Alice normalizes her own crossing in the same way:

\[ r_i=1, \qquad \alpha_i=\frac1{r_i}=1. \]

This does not place every Alice at the same global child time. If \(T_i\) is the child epoch associated with the \(i\)-th crossing, then her subsequent reciprocal expansion is

\[ \frac{A_C(T)}{A_C(T_i)} =\frac1{r_i(\tau_i)}. \]

Every crossing is locally ordinary, but the crossings are globally ordered. The object GD2 seeks is therefore a map between histories of events. The event horizon supplies the null history. The freely falling Alices supply the timelike histories that continue after crossing.

If all of these observers are to inhabit one homogeneous child universe, their parent contraction must share one fractional background rate. In spherical symmetry this gives the separable form

\[ R_P(t,q)=S(t)F(q). \]

Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse satisfies this relation exactly. More realistic collapse departs from it, and those departures become the natural place for child perturbations to enter.

The Birth of a Horizon

The homogeneous Oppenheimer–Snyder model gives us an exact place to begin. Inside the star, the geometry is a contracting closed-FLRW spacetime,

\[ ds_P^2 =S^2(\eta_P) \left[-d\eta_P^2+dq^2+\sin^2q\,d\Omega^2\right]. \]

Shift conformal time so that the past endpoint of the event horizon is at \(\bar\eta=0\). The outgoing horizon generator inside the matter is then simply

\[ \bar\eta=q. \]

The standard conformal map from the closed Einstein cylinder to a flat spherical Minkowski patch is

\[ T_M-\rho_M=\tan\frac{\bar\eta-q}{2}, \qquad T_M+\rho_M=\tan\frac{\bar\eta+q}{2}. \]

On the event horizon, it becomes

\[ T_M=\rho_M=\frac12\tan q. \]

The point where the parent event horizon begins maps to the origin. As the horizon grows outward through the star, its complete history maps to an outgoing light cone.

The conformal map establishes this outgoing null history as the child's birth cone in the OS benchmark. CSBHI proposes that the completed child boundary extends it into the child's global particle horizon.

The map fixes the light cone, while the formation geometry must fix its physical calibration. A vertex-preserving conformal family can change how events are parameterized along the cone. The null geometry therefore supplies the causal ordering; the throat supplies the absolute child scale and formation-era equation of state.

In the conventional OS interval beginning at maximum expansion, the horizon vertex lies inside the modeled collapse when the stellar surface coordinate satisfies \(q_0<\pi/3\).

Inside the Formation Throat

A forming black hole contains more than one kind of horizon. The event horizon is global: it separates events that can still communicate with the distant parent exterior from those that cannot. A trapping horizon is local: it marks a surface on which the outgoing family of spherical light rays has zero area expansion.

These horizons coincide when a spherical black hole becomes stationary. During formation they are separated. In the ingoing Vaidya benchmark, for example,

\[ \frac{dR_E}{dv} =\frac12\left(1-\frac{R_T}{R_E}\right). \]

If \(R_E=R_T\), the event horizon stops growing. A finite separation is therefore part of the ordinary geometry of accretion, not an extra layer placed into the spacetime.

Generic smooth spherical collapse can also create marginal surfaces in pairs. Let

\[ C=\frac{2m}{R} \]

be the compactness, with \(m\) the Misner–Sharp mass, \(\Gamma_{\rm MS}=D_\ell R\), and \(G=c=1\) for this calculation. At first trapping, the maximum of the compactness profile touches unity:

\[ C_b=1, \qquad D_\ell C_b=0, \qquad D_\ell C =\frac{\Gamma_{\rm MS}}{R}\left(8\pi R^2\rho-C\right). \]

It follows that \(8\pi R_b^2\rho_b=1\). Near this first contact the two marginal branches open as

\[ \Delta\ell_\pm =\pm\sqrt{\frac{2A}{B}}\sqrt{t-t_b}+O(t-t_b). \]

The square root gives the branches a formally infinite velocity if \(t\) is used as the parameter. With \(\sigma=\sqrt{t-t_b}\), both branches are smooth. The geometry is an ordinary fold.

After the pair forms, a representative ordering is

\[ \underbrace{R_E}_{\text{global null history}} \;>\; \underbrace{R_+}_{\text{outer marginal branch}} \;>\; \underbrace{R_-}_{\text{inward branch}}. \]

The inward branch begins at \(R_b\) and moves toward smaller areal radius. This gives it the direction and normalization required of the GD1 variable:

\[ r\stackrel{?}{=}\frac{R_-}{R_b}. \]

GD2 proposes this inward branch as the parent history underlying \(r\). The complete timelike map must show that it reaches \(r\rightarrow0\) only at infinite child proper time.

The finite region surrounding these horizons also carries the matter information that a pure null-flux model leaves out. For a perfect fluid and normalized radial null directions,

\[ T_{\ell\ell}=\frac{\rho+p}{2}, \qquad T_{\ell n}=\frac{\rho-p}{2}. \]

Along-null focusing measures \(\rho+p\), while cross-focusing measures \(\rho-p\). Together they recover both density and pressure. The throat is therefore where the causal clock and the local equation-of-state data can first meet.

A Smooth Beginning

Weyl curvature measures the tidal part of the gravitational field: the part associated with clumping, distortion, and freely propagating gravitational influence. An exact FLRW background has no Weyl curvature. A physical universe cannot be exactly FLRW, because the perturbations from which structure grows carry a small tidal field.

The CSBHI target is therefore a Weyl-free background, not a Weyl-free universe:

\[ \overline{\mathcal W}=0, \qquad \delta\mathcal W\neq0, \qquad R_*^2\lVert\delta\mathcal W\rVert\ll1. \]

In a regular spherical perfect fluid, a useful Weyl amplitude is

\[ \mathcal W =\frac{m}{R^3}-\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho. \]

Smooth spherical density and pressure profiles contain only even powers of radius near the center. It follows that

\[ \mathcal W=O(R_E^2). \]

Pressure gradients, heat flux, and anisotropic stress alter the coefficient of this term without removing its quadratic suppression in the regular spherical case. Rotation adds a new possibility: a finite quadrupolar residue can appear at \(O(\chi^2)\). The child may therefore begin extremely smooth without being exactly featureless.

The exact homogeneous limit still gives an important result. On a marginal sphere, \(2m=R\), and

\[ \mathcal W=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \rho_{\rm local}=\bar\rho. \]

At that limit the parent surface-gravity expression takes the same dimensionless form as the flat-FLRW apparent horizon:

\[ \frac{\kappa_A R_A}{c^2} =\frac{3w_{\rm eff}-1}{4}. \]

Under the proposed handoff, the mapped radius fixes the Hubble scale, while \(\kappa_AR_A/c^2\) fixes the background equation-of-state character. Together they form the local horizon two-jet required to begin a Friedmann history.

First trapping and homogeneous handoff are not the same event. Pair creation gives \(8\pi R_b^2\rho_b=1\); the exactly Weyl-flat marginal benchmark gives \(8\pi R_*^2\rho_*=3\). The formation geometry must carry the system from one condition to the other while preserving the small nonzero perturbative residue.

Marginality must also be tested in the child metric. If a local relation \(g_C=\Omega_{\rm W}^2g_P\) holds through the relevant region, a child marginal surface obeys

\[ \theta_P+2k_P(\ln\Omega_{\rm W})=0. \]

The derivative of the local Weyl factor matters. Parent marginality by itself is not enough.

Infall Becomes Scale

Once the homogeneous reciprocal branch has formed, compare one parent ruler and its child dual. Normalize them to the same reference length \(\ell_*\) at the handoff:

\[ \ell_P=\ell_*r, \qquad \ell_C=\ell_*a. \]

Their relative scale is

\[ \Gamma \equiv\frac{\ell_C}{\ell_P} =\frac ar =a^2 =r^{-2}. \]

Big Gamma is the mature parent-to-child ruler separation. It is not, by definition, a Weyl factor relating an entire Schwarzschild or Kerr spacetime to FLRW. Where a common homogeneous conformal sector exists, it may coincide with such a factor; the throat must establish that relation.

Because \(\Gamma=a^2\), its velocity is twice the Hubble rate:

\[ \frac{\dot\Gamma}{\Gamma}=2H. \]

Differentiating once more and using the flat Friedmann equations gives

\[ \frac{\ddot\Gamma}{\Gamma} =\frac{8\pi G}{3} \left(\rho-\frac{3p}{c^2}\right). \]

The acceleration of the dual scale therefore reads the trace of the stress-energy tensor. Radiation is trace-free, so \(\Gamma\propto\tau_C\) and \(a\propto\tau_C^{1/2}\). Matter gives \(\Gamma\propto\tau_C^{4/3}\) and \(a\propto\tau_C^{2/3}\). Vacuum gives \(\Gamma\propto e^{2H_\Lambda\tau_C}\) and \(a\propto e^{H_\Lambda\tau_C}\).

The throat does not need to contain a universe that is already cosmological in size. It needs to launch the scale evolution. Since

\[ \ln\Gamma=2\ln a, \]

every e-fold of child expansion contributes two e-folds of relative ruler separation. The hierarchy is accumulated by the reciprocal cosmology itself.

Why radiation is special

Suppose the throat supplies a parent ruler related to the child apparent-horizon radius by \(R_A^C=\Gamma R_P\), together with the required child-time map. Flat Friedmann evolution then gives

\[ \frac{\dot R_P}{R_P} =\frac{3w_{\rm eff}-1}{2}H. \]

A stationary mapped ruler therefore selects

\[ \dot R_P=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad w_{\rm eff}=\frac13. \]

Radiation is also the state for which the stress-energy trace vanishes, \(\ddot\Gamma=0\), \(\kappa_AR_A=0\), and the flat-FLRW apparent horizon is null. These relations select radiation as the natural first mature child regime. CSBHI proposes this radiation handoff; the ruler correspondence and time map supplied by the throat determine whether it occurs.

What Crosses the Throat

The event horizon, the trapping geometry, and the reciprocal branch carry different information.

The event horizon carries a global null history. Let \(v_H\) parameterize one of its generators, with non-affinity \(\kappa_P\), and let \(\eta=\Phi(v_H)\) be the corresponding child conformal time. Their affine parameters obey

\[ a^2[\Phi(v_H)]\Phi'(v_H) =C\,\mathcal T(v_H) \exp\!\left(\int^{v_H}\kappa_P(s)\,ds\right). \]

Here \(\mathcal T=d\lambda_C/d\lambda_P\) is the null affine transfer. Wherever the two formation regions are genuinely related by \(g_C=\Omega_{\rm W}^2g_P\), conformal geometry gives \(\mathcal T=C_\lambda\Omega_{{\rm W},H}^2\). The physical \(\Omega_{\rm W}\) and its connection to the mature ruler scale \(\Gamma\) must come from the same throat solution.

The trapping geometry carries local density and pressure through focusing, marginality, and surface gravity. Its small Weyl and shear residue carries the candidate seeds of structure. Once the mature branch exists, the total homogeneous equation-of-state ledger can be written

\[ P(r)\equiv\frac{H^2}{H_\Lambda^2} =1+\eta_mr^3+\eta_rr^4. \]

The powers have a direct meaning: \(r^n\) corresponds to \(n=3(1+w)\). Vacuum contributes \(n=0\), dust \(n=3\), and radiation \(n=4\). The same history becomes a single first integral for the mature scale:

\[ \frac{\dot\Gamma^2}{4H_\Lambda^2} =\Gamma^2 +\eta_m\sqrt{\Gamma} +\eta_r. \]

The separate coefficients depend on the normalization of \(r\). Their combination \(\mathcal J=\eta_r^3/\eta_m^4\) does not. A completed dressed-horizon map may therefore determine the total matter-and-radiation ledger from the parent collapse. It will not, by geometry alone, distinguish species with the same equation of state. Baryon abundance, dark-sector identity, and particle asymmetries remain questions of microphysics and transport.

A common causal history

A positive conformal scaling preserves light cones. It does not create causal contact between events that were never connected. The possible resolution of the cosmological horizon problem comes instead from a shared history before the mature FLRW clock begins.

\[ \chi_{\rm causal} =\Delta\eta_{\rm formation} +\int_{\rm mature}\frac{c\,d\tau_C}{a(\tau_C)}. \]

Regions that later lie outside one another's FLRW-only particle horizons may still descend from one connected formation domain. This becomes a solution of the horizon problem only if the independently derived optical map makes \(\Delta\eta_{\rm formation}\) large enough.

How Far the Result Survives

Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse is homogeneous, pressureless, spherical, and nonrotating. The formation campaign added the missing physics one layer at a time and asked the same question at every step: does a regular contribution force an order-unity breakdown at birth, or does it enter as background evolution and perturbation data?

Level Collapse model What it tests Present result
0 OS dust Homogeneous birth-cone map Exact benchmark passed
1 Homogeneous perfect fluid Pressure without gradients Structural pass
2 Perturbed LTB dust Density, velocity, and Weyl modes Partial pass; calibration open
3 Spherical perfect fluid Pressure gradients and Alice–fluid tilt Structural pass near the vertex
4 Dissipative spherical fluid Heat flux and anisotropic stress Structural pass if regular
5 Slow rotation and creases Broken spherical symmetry Local pass; retains spin memory

The pattern is consistent. Pressure, density gradients, dissipation, and rotation do not disappear. They survive as changes to the background or as perturbation data. In the regular spherical models, scalar departures are suppressed near the center. Farther through the star they can become nonlinear, and rotation can retain a dimensionless preferred-axis memory.

These structural results establish that the formation idea survives the analytic models in the campaign. Numerical collapse must determine the amplitudes produced by a realistic star, while the nonspherical conformal map remains a mathematical task.

The Remaining Calculation

General relativity supplies the growing event horizon, the trapping-horizon pair, the focusing equations, and the regularity conditions that protect the central geometry. The OS solution supplies an exact conformal birth-cone benchmark. GD1 supplies the reciprocal law once the mature interior has formed.

GD2 proposes that these structures are successive parts of one construction: the event horizon carries the global causal history, the finite throat supplies the local curvature and stress-energy data, and the inward trapping branch becomes the radial history that the child experiences reciprocally.

One calculation must now connect them. Starting from a realistic collapse solution, it must derive

\[ \text{collapse geometry} \longrightarrow \left(r,\tau_C,\Omega_{\rm W},\mathcal T\right) \longrightarrow \text{mature reciprocal cosmology}. \]

In particular, it must prove the identification \(r=R_-/R_b\), evolve the geometry from first trapping to a low-Weyl handoff, derive the child clock and local Weyl factor, and show that the null affine transfer is the corresponding projection of the same spacetime.

If one collapse geometry supplies all four pieces without importing a child cosmology as calibration, the formation problem closes. The expansion that follows is no longer a second structure attached to the black hole. It is the reciprocal continuation of the collapse itself.
