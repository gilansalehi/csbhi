01 · Starting point

The question left by GD1

Geometric Duality begins with two descriptions of one interior history. In the parent description, a normalized radial scale \(r\) decreases from \(1\) toward \(0\). In the child description, the corresponding cosmological scale grows according to \(a=1/r\).

Once a homogeneous child branch exists, the proposal becomes mathematically economical: specify its matter, radiation, and vacuum content, and ordinary Friedmann equations determine the background expansion. The difficult question lies one step earlier. What feature of a forming black hole selects the radial history, supplies a cosmological clock, and produces initial data close enough to FLRW for that mature description to begin?

Formation hypothesis

Black-hole formation supplies a finite four-dimensional transition region in which global causal history, local horizon dynamics, and parent-to-child scale transfer are assembled together.

This transition region is called the formation throat. Here “throat” means a dynamical causal bottleneck. It does not by itself denote a traversable wormhole.

02 · The experiment

Repeat the descent

Imagine a continuous family of freely falling observers—Alices—crossing while the parent black hole forms. The first crosses when the event horizon is tiny; later Alices cross after it has grown through more of the collapsing matter.

Each Alice, locally

The same beginning

\[ r_i=1, \qquad \alpha_i=\frac1{r_i}=1 \]

Every observer normalizes her own crossing as the beginning of her reciprocal evolution.

All Alices, globally

Different child epochs

\[ \frac{A_C(T)}{A_C(T_i)}=\frac1{r_i(\tau_i)} \]

The local normalizations must coexist inside one global child scale factor \(A_C\).

This changes the object being mapped. GD2 is not trying to identify one complete parent horizon surface with one complete child surface. It associates a history of crossing events with a child causal history, while each timelike Alice continues inward after crossing. The null horizon history and the timelike reciprocal history are related, but they are not the same curve.

03 · Exact benchmark

A growing horizon becomes a birth cone

Oppenheimer–Snyder collapse supplies the clean analytic example. Its homogeneous stellar interior is a contracting closed-FLRW geometry. With conformal time shifted so the event-horizon vertex is at the origin, the interior event horizon follows

\[ \bar\eta=q. \]

The standard conformal map from the closed Einstein cylinder to a flat spherical Minkowski patch is

\[ T_M-\rho_M=\tan\frac{\bar\eta-q}{2}, \qquad T_M+\rho_M=\tan\frac{\bar\eta+q}{2}. \]

On the event horizon this reduces exactly to

Exact OS conformal geometry \[ T_M=\rho_M=\frac12\tan q. \]

The point where the spherical event horizon begins maps to the origin; its subsequent null history maps to an outgoing light cone. GD2 proposes that light cone as the child's birth cone. Calling it the global particle horizon requires the completed child boundary and is therefore a stronger claim.

This result establishes the causal shape, not a unique physical child cosmology. A vertex-preserving conformal family can reparameterize the same cone, so neither its absolute scale nor a formation-era equation of state is fixed by the null geometry alone.

What the homogeneous benchmark also requires

If every already-crossed cohort is required to share one synchronous child background, the parent collapse must be homologous at background order,

\[ R_P(t,q)=S(t)F(q). \]

OS satisfies this with \(F(q)=\sin q\). For the standard OS interval to contain the horizon vertex after the initial maximum-expansion slice, its surface coordinate must satisfy \(q_0<\pi/3\). More generally, a shear-free parent congruence with spatially homogeneous fractional contraction supplies the covariant background condition. Departures from it are not discarded; they enter the child perturbation sector.

04 · Local formation geometry

Formation has finite width

A dynamical black hole carries both a global event horizon and local trapping horizons. They answer different questions, and during formation they generally do not coincide.

The event horizon asks which signals can ever escape to the distant parent exterior. A trapping horizon marks where the outgoing family of spherical light rays has zero area expansion. Their separation is a physical region in which focusing, matter flux, pressure, and curvature evolve.

Why a forming horizon has width

In the ingoing Vaidya benchmark, the event horizon obeys

\[ \frac{dR_E}{dv} =\frac12\left(1-\frac{R_T}{R_E}\right). \]

Setting \(R_E=R_T\) makes the right-hand side vanish: coincidence is the stationary limit. During accretion, the global and local horizons separate. Vaidya captures the clock supplied by null flux, while a timelike fluid is needed to supply both \(\rho+p\) and \(\rho-p\), and therefore the complete local density-and-pressure ledger.

First trapping opens the local throat

In spherical collapse define the compactness \(C=2m/R\), where \(m\) is the Misner–Sharp mass and units \(G=c=1\) are used in this local calculation. At a generic first marginal sphere, the maximum of the compactness profile touches unity:

\[ C_b=1, \qquad D_\ell C_b=0, \qquad D_\ell C=\frac{\Gamma_R}{R}\left(8\pi R^2\rho-C\right). \]

Hence \(8\pi R_b^2\rho_b=1\). Near this first contact, the two marginal branches open as

\[ \Delta\ell_\pm =\pm\sqrt{\frac{2A}{B}}\sqrt{t-t_b}+O(t-t_b). \]

This is an ordinary fold bifurcation; the geometry is smooth when parameterized by \(\sigma=\sqrt{t-t_b}\). The inward branch begins at \(R_b\) and moves toward smaller radius, giving the candidate correspondence

Theorem target \[ r\stackrel{?}{=}\frac{R_-}{R_b}. \]

Its direction and normalization match GD1. A complete proof must still show that this branch reaches the required reciprocal endpoint under the child-time map, including infinite child proper time as \(r\rightarrow0\).

05 · Initial geometry

An FLRW background with a primordial residue

FLRW is conformally flat: its background Weyl tensor vanishes. The physical universe is not exactly FLRW, however. Its small primordial perturbations carry nonzero tidal curvature. The CSBHI target is therefore a homogeneous background together with a small inherited residue—not a perfectly featureless child.

Background limit

FLRW geometry

\[ \overline{\mathcal W}=0 \]

The zeroth-order child geometry is homogeneous, isotropic, and shear-free.

Physical prediction

Inherited structure

\[ \delta\mathcal W\neq0, \qquad R_*^2\lVert\delta\mathcal W\rVert\ll1 \]

Small tidal and shear data survive as candidates for primordial structure.

In regular spherical collapse, the convenient Weyl amplitude

\[ \mathcal W=\frac{m}{R^3}-\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho \]

behaves as \(\mathcal W=O(R_E^2)\) near the central birth region. Density gradients, pressure gradients, dissipation, and anisotropic stress change its coefficient without removing this quadratic protection in the regular spherical case. Slow rotation is different: it can permit a finite \(O(\chi^2)\) quadrupolar residue, whose observable amplitude remains to be calculated.

The exact homogeneous benchmark

On a spherical marginal surface, \(2m=R\). If its background is exactly Weyl-flat, then

\[ \mathcal W=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \rho_{\rm local}=\bar\rho. \]

The surface-gravity expression then agrees with the flat-FLRW apparent-horizon form,

\[ \frac{\kappa_A R_A}{c^2}=\frac{3w_{\rm eff}-1}{4}. \]

For a nearly homogeneous sphere, these relations acquire controlled corrections proportional to the dimensionless Weyl residue. The exact equality is the background benchmark, not a demand that physical perturbations vanish.

First trapping and homogeneous handoff are distinct conditions. Pair creation gives \(8\pi R_b^2\rho_b=1\), whereas the exactly Weyl-flat marginal benchmark gives \(8\pi R_*^2\rho_*=3\). The throat must describe the evolution between them. A completed handoff must also impose marginality in the child metric. Wherever a local Weyl relation \(g_C=\Omega_{\rm W}^2g_P\) exists, the relevant condition is

\[ \theta_P+2k_P(\ln\Omega_{\rm W})=0. \]

The mapped radius and Hayward–Kodama surface gravity then form a horizon two-jet: the first fixes the local Hubble scale, while the dimensionless product \(\kappa_AR_A/c^2\) fixes the background equation-of-state character.

07 · Child inheritance

What the mature branch can carry

In a chosen reciprocal normalization, the flat vacuum–matter–radiation background can be written as a radial ledger,

\[ P(r)\equiv\frac{H^2}{H_\Lambda^2} =1+\eta_mr^3+\eta_rr^4. \]

A power \(r^n\) corresponds to a constant equation-of-state sector through \(n=3(1+w)\): vacuum contributes \(n=0\), dust \(n=3\), and radiation \(n=4\). Geometry can therefore organize total equation-of-state sectors. It cannot by itself distinguish particle species with the same \(w\), such as baryons and cold dark matter.

\[ \frac{\dot\Gamma_{\rm GD}^2}{4H_\Lambda^2} =\Gamma_{\rm GD}^2+\eta_m\sqrt{\Gamma_{\rm GD}}+\eta_r. \]

The coefficients depend on scale-factor normalization, while \(\mathcal J=\eta_r^3/\eta_m^4\) is normalization-independent. A completed dressed trapping-horizon map may determine this total ledger from the parent collapse; species abundances, asymmetries, and thermalization remain microphysical questions.

Radiation is a distinguished candidate handoff

Suppose the throat identifies a parent ruler through \(R_A^C=\Gamma_{\rm GD}R_P\) and supplies the required child-time map. Flat Friedmann evolution then gives

\[ \frac{\dot R_P}{R_P}=\frac{3w_{\rm eff}-1}{2}H. \]
Conditional theorem \[ \dot R_P=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad w_{\rm eff}=\frac13. \]

Radiation is also trace-free, makes \(\ddot\Gamma_{\rm GD}=0\), gives \(\kappa_AR_A/c^2=0\), and makes the flat-FLRW apparent horizon null. This convergence makes radiation a compelling handoff candidate, but the formation geometry must still select the mapped ruler and its clock.

A shared causal ancestry

Positive conformal scaling preserves light cones. It cannot create causal contact. CSBHI instead proposes that regions widely separated in the mature child can inherit conditions from one connected formation domain before the ordinary FLRW clock begins.

\[ \chi_{\rm causal} =\Delta\eta_{\rm formation} +\int_{\rm mature}\frac{c\,d\tau_C}{a(\tau_C)}. \]

This addresses the cosmological horizon problem only if an independently derived affine map makes \(\Delta\eta_{\rm formation}\) large enough. Large scale separation alone is not causal contact.

The null clock equation

Let \(v_H\) parameterize the parent event-horizon generator with non-affinity \(\kappa_P\), and let \(\eta=\Phi(v_H)\) be the proposed child event map. Their affine parameters give

\[ a^2[\Phi(v_H)]\Phi'(v_H) =C\,\mathcal T(v_H) \exp\!\left(\int^{v_H}\kappa_P(s)\,ds\right). \]

Wherever the formation regions are genuinely related by \(g_C=\Omega_{\rm W}^2g_P\), conformal geometry gives \(\mathcal T=C_\lambda\Omega_{{\rm W},H}^2\). The remaining task is to derive the physical \(\Omega_{\rm W}\) through the throat and connect its null projection to the mature ruler scale \(\Gamma_{\rm GD}\).
