The case for review

CSBHI asks whether black-hole infall and cosmic expansion are reciprocal descriptions related by (a( au)=1/r( au)). The ansatz is not derived. Given the reciprocal interior metric, ordinary matter, radiation, vacuum energy, and Einstein's equations, the familiar Friedmann dynamics follow. A separate symmetry result distinguishes (1/r) within a stated class of horizon-fixing maps. At galactic scales, applying the same inverse-horizon correspondence predicts (g_\dagger(z)/g_\dagger(0)=H(z)/H_0): a rising ratio rather than a constant one. The present data are suggestive, not decisive. This packet asks reviewers to identify the first premise, calculation, or physical interpretation that fails.

Flagship prediction

\[ \frac{g_\dagger(z)}{g_\dagger(0)}=\frac{H(z)}{H_0} \]

The absolute normalization remains open. The redshift dependence does not: the evolving correspondence predicts a rising ratio; a fixed acceleration scale predicts (1).

Claim ledger

Each claim should be judged at the level actually asserted.

Status Claim What remains
Ansatz Alice's interior spatial scale and Bob's infall radius obey (a=1/r). Derive or replace the correspondence within a complete parent–child geometry.
Conditional result The reciprocal interior metric plus Einstein's equations and conserved matter, radiation, and vacuum energy produce Friedmann dynamics. Check every premise, sign, coefficient, and matter assumption in RI I.
Theorem (1/r) is the unique nontrivial power-law map that is scale-free, involutive, and fixes the normalized horizon. Test whether the stated function class is physically justified or unnecessarily narrow.
Working result A smooth spherical Weyl-relaxation profile exists, while a shear-free four-dimensional completion requires a specific transverse stress. Independent verification and a dynamical realization of that stress.
Correspondence The galactic global scale is identified with the inverse radius of the contemporaneous cosmological horizon: (A=cH(z)). Determine whether the completed dynamics select the evolving horizon rather than the asymptotic de Sitter horizon.
Prediction (g_\dagger(z)/g_\dagger(0)=H(z)/H_0). Discriminate this curve from a constant scale and from feedback-driven galaxy evolution.

The assignment

Please try to break it.

  1. Find a conclusion imported into the premises that supposedly establish it.
  2. Find a sign, coefficient, dimensional, or tensorial error in the conditional derivations.
  3. Identify where the Alice–Bob interpretation ceases to describe the equations being used.
  4. Show that the evolving acceleration-scale test is observationally degenerate with a better-motivated model.
  5. Point to prior work that already proves, refutes, or materially changes the construction.

The most useful response identifies the first point at which the argument fails.

How to respond

A short verdict, an annotated document, or a list of objections is welcome. Please distinguish mathematical errors, physical objections, missing literature, and presentation problems where possible. Corrections and negative results are useful; agreement is not required.

This project was developed through sustained adversarial collaboration with several AI systems. Each document identifies its authorship and review history. Cross-checking among models is not a substitute for professional review; it is why this packet is now being presented for one.
