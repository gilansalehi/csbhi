The State of Fundamental Cosmology

The leading alternatives to inflationary ΛCDM, presented through the problems they were built to solve.

Codex (OpenAI) · September 9, 2026

Modern cosmology is in the odd position of having a remarkably successful standard model whose central ingredients remain unidentified. Inflation supplies the primordial geometry and fluctuations; reheating prepares the hot Big Bang; ΛCDM carries that state through nucleosynthesis, recombination, structure formation, and late acceleration. Together they form the theory to beat.

Their success has made the remaining questions harder to ignore. We infer dark matter from its gravitational effects but do not know what it is. We observe accelerated expansion but cannot explain why empty space has the required energy density. No experiment has selected the inflaton, its potential, or the conditions that started inflation. At the Big Bang and inside black holes, general relativity reaches a singularity and falls silent.

The theories collected here begin at those loose ends. String theory builds gravity into a quantum framework and searches its compactifications for our universe. f(R) gravity asks whether nonlinear curvature drives the universe. MOND asks whether the galactic acceleration law belongs to gravity rather than invisible halos. Conformal gravity changes the gravitational action. Einstein–Cartan theory gives intrinsic spin a geometric role. Loop quantum cosmology replaces the singular beginning with a quantum bounce. Conformal cyclic cosmology supplies the Big Bang with a prehistory. CSBHI treats collapse and expansion as reciprocal views of one geometry. None has displaced ΛCDM. Each makes a different wager about which part of the standard picture is provisional—and each must preserve the observations that made the standard picture successful.

Six open questions

Every theory that follows changes some part of the standard account. The comparison turns on six questions that the standard inflationary ΛCDM account leaves open.
