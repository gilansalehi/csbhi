The Reciprocal Ansatz as the Unique Scale-Free Horizon-Fixing Involution

Supporting result · Claude (Anthropic), from a conjecture by G. Salehi · August 1, 2026

Status: This note closes Open Question Q1 as posed — "derive the reciprocal power from a symmetry." It does not derive the symmetry itself from the throat geometry; that residual is stated at the end and tracked as Q15 in the open-questions index.

Claim

The reciprocal ansatz \(a=1/r\) is not an arbitrary choice of power. It is the unique map from Bob's horizon-normalized radial coordinate to Alice's scale factor satisfying three minimal requirements:

  1. scale-free — a power law \(a=Cr^{p}\), introducing no new dimensionful constant;
  2. self-dual involution — the parent/child exchange is its own inverse, \(a(a(r))=r\);
  3. horizon-fixing — the exchange fixes the crossing surface, \(a(1)=1\).

Under (1)–(3), \(a=1/r\) is forced. This derives the reciprocal power from a symmetry — the content Q1 asked for — and shows the ansatz and the GD III galaxy law are two faces of one local/global exchange symmetry.

Proof

Write the map as a power law (scale-freeness),

\[ a=f(r)=Cr^{p},\qquad C>0 . \]

Impose the involution condition:

\[ f(f(r))=C\,(Cr^{p})^{p}=C^{\,1+p}\,r^{\,p^{2}}\stackrel{!}{=}r . \]

Matching the power of \(r\) and the constant separately requires

\[ p^{2}=1,\qquad C^{\,1+p}=1 . \]

  • The root \(p=+1\) gives \(f(r)=Cr\); the involution then forces \(C^{2}=1\Rightarrow C=1\), i.e. the identity \(f(r)=r\). This is the trivial exchange and is excluded by requiring a non-trivial parent/child map.
  • The root \(p=-1\) gives \(C^{\,0}=1\) for any \(C>0\), so \(f(r)=C/r\).

The horizon-fixing condition \(f(1)=C=1\) then selects

\[ \boxed{\,a=\dfrac1r\,}. \]

No other map satisfies (1)–(3). \(\blacksquare\)

Two corollaries

  • The fixed point is the crossing surface. The only fixed point of \(a=1/r\) on \(r>0\) is \(r=1\), the horizon. The exchange symmetry pins itself to exactly the surface where Bob's exterior and Alice's interior meet — it does not have to be imposed there; it is the unique fixed point.
  • The map swaps the two ends. \(a=1/r\) sends \(r\to0\) (the would-be singularity) to \(a\to\infty\) (unbounded expansion) and \(r\to\infty\) (spatial infinity) to \(a\to0\) (vanishing scale). Singularity and infinity are exchanged; the deferral of the singularity into Alice's infinite future is a structural consequence of the involution, not a separate assumption.

Unification with the galaxy law

Conditions (1)–(2) are the same assumptions GD III uses to select the galaxy acceleration law. There, homogeneity (scale-freeness) and self-duality under local/global exchange yield the functional equation \(\phi(x)=x\,\phi(1/x)\), whose scale-invariant solution is the geometric mean \(g_{\rm mix}=\kappa\sqrt{g_N\,cH}\). Here the same scale-free self-duality, applied to the radial coordinate instead of the acceleration ratio, yields \(a=1/r\). The reciprocal interior and the galaxy law are therefore two realizations of one local/global exchange symmetry — the exchange acting on radius in the first case, on acceleration in the second. Whatever ultimately justifies that symmetry justifies both at once.

Status and residual

What is closed: Q1 as posed — "derive the reciprocal power from a symmetry, variational principle, embedding, or boundary condition." The power \(p=-1\) is derived, uniquely, from the scale-free horizon-fixing involution symmetry.

What remains (the residual, a deeper question): the proof assumes the parent/child exchange is a scale-free horizon-fixing involution. It does not derive those three properties from the completed throat geometry. Why must the exchange introduce no new scale? Why exactly involutive rather than some other self-map? Why fixed at the horizon? Closing that requires showing the throat construction forces the exchange to take this form — the sharpened successor to Q1, recorded as Q15 in the open-questions index.
