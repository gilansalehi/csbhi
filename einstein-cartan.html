Einstein–Cartan Cosmology

Matter carries intrinsic spin as well as energy. Einstein–Cartan gravity gives that spin a geometric response, turning fermion density into resistance against singular collapse.

The case for Einstein–Cartan

Matter tells spacetime how to twist

General relativity teaches spacetime to respond to energy and momentum, but matter carries intrinsic angular momentum as well. Einstein–Cartan gravity completes the geometry: energy curves spacetime, while spin produces torsion. No new long-range field is required, and ordinary vacuum gravity remains exactly Einsteinian.[1, 2]

Torsion hides at ordinary density and becomes dominant precisely where classical gravity predicts a singularity. Compress fermions far enough and their spin generates a repulsive contact interaction that grows faster than radiation or dust. Collapse can stop at finite density, pass through a bounce, and enter an expanding phase without an exotic scalar field.[3, 4]

Nikodem Popławski follows that mechanism inside a black hole. The collapsing interior becomes a closed child universe; particle production supplies matter and entropy, and the bounce can open into inflation. If the construction closes, the spin already carried by ordinary fermions removes the singularity and turns black holes into engines of cosmogenesis.[5–7]

Central move
Make the affine connection independent and let intrinsic spin source its antisymmetric part.
Exact result
Torsion is algebraically fixed by spin and produces a gravitational four-fermion interaction.
Cosmological result
In an unpolarized spin fluid, the negative spin correction scales as \(a^{-6}\) and can create a finite-density bounce.
Empirical status
Quantitative background and fluctuation models exist; no distinctive torsion signal has been confirmed.

The construction

One extra field equation, no propagating torsion

Varying the Einstein–Hilbert action with respect to the metric gives the curvature equation. Varying it with respect to the independent connection gives the Cartan equation. In the minimal theory, the second equation is algebraic:

\[ \text{torsion}=\kappa\times\text{spin density}, \qquad \kappa=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}. \]

Torsion therefore has no vacuum wave of its own. Substitution back into the metric equation adds terms quadratic in spin. For a randomly oriented Weyssenhoff fluid, the derivative terms average away and the effective density and pressure become[2, 3]

\[ \epsilon_{\rm eff}=\epsilon-\frac{\kappa s^2}{4}, \qquad p_{\rm eff}=p-\frac{\kappa s^2}{4}. \]

Fermion number density dilutes as \(n\propto a^{-3}\), while \(s^2\propto n^2\). The torsion contribution is consequently negative and grows faster during contraction than radiation or dust:

\[ \epsilon_S=-\frac{\kappa s^2}{4}\propto-a^{-6}. \]

At ordinary density the correction disappears. During extreme contraction it can cancel the positive matter density at a nonzero scale factor, forcing \(H=0\) before a singularity forms. The bounce follows from the chosen spin-fluid closure; it is not a theorem for every possible source in every Einstein–Cartan spacetime.

Black-hole cosmogenesis

Popławski's strongest realization

Popławski identifies the contracting branch with matter collapsing inside a parent black hole. Spin–torsion repulsion replaces the singular endpoint with a bounce; the expanding branch becomes a new, causally hidden region. Particle production near the bounce supplies matter and entropy and can generate a finite inflationary phase without an inflaton.[3–5]

The homogeneous model is analytically clean. The black-hole interior is not. A Schwarzschild interior is anisotropic, and its shear can grow faster than the \(a^{-6}\) spin term. Popławski's Kantowski–Sachs calculation finds that a bounce then requires particle production to increase the fermion density rapidly enough for torsion to overtake shear. Inflation subsequently drives isotropization.[7]

The paper marks its own approximation: a successful nonsingular interior never reaches exact vacuum Kantowski–Sachs geometry. A more general collapsing solution must evolve toward a closed FLRW child. That spacetime-level construction remains the central geometric calculation.

Explanatory gains

What Einstein–Cartan adds to GR

Spin becomes a gravitational source

General relativity couples geometry to stress-energy. Einstein–Cartan completes the parallel between translation and rotation by coupling torsion to intrinsic angular momentum.

The GR limit is automatic

Minimal torsion is algebraic and vanishes with spin density. The theory preserves every vacuum solution of GR without introducing a second long-range gravitational field.

Singularity avoidance has a material cause

Repulsion does not come from an inserted exotic scalar potential. The same fermions already present in matter source the high-density correction.

Collapse can acquire a cosmological continuation

The contracting and expanding branches give black-hole cosmology a concrete dynamical mechanism: collapse, finite-density bounce, child expansion.

Several early-universe problems share one sector

Torsion and particle production have been used to address the singularity, flatness, horizon, entropy, inflation, and matter–antimatter problems within one research program.[3, 5, 6]

Open problems

What remains unresolved

1. The bounce is matter-model dependent

The \(a^{-6}\) correction follows from an unpolarized spin-fluid average with conserved fermion number. Different spin tensors, polarization assumptions, particle production laws, and microscopic fermion states change the effective source. Einstein–Cartan supplies the coupling; the cosmological closure still has to be earned.[2, 3, 8]

2. Shear can beat torsion

In the anisotropic black-hole model, shear grows faster during contraction than the conserved spin density. Torsion alone then fails to guarantee a bounce. The successful branch needs sufficiently strong particle production.[7]

3. The particle-production coefficient is not derived

A phenomenological coefficient \(\beta\) controls whether the universe remains singular, bounces and inflates, cycles, or enters eternal inflation. For Standard Model degrees of freedom the model finds \(\beta_{\rm cr}\approx1/929\), but no completed quantum calculation fixes the realized \(\beta\).[5, 7]

4. The black-hole-to-FLRW geometry remains approximate

The homogeneous bounce and the anisotropic interior are controlled models, not one finished collapse spacetime. Rotating collapse, trapped surfaces, topology change, junction geometry, and the approach to a closed FLRW region must be solved together.[5, 7]

5. The primordial spectrum is not yet a blind forecast

The scale-factor uncertainty calculation obtains the order of the CMB temperature fluctuation as a function of \(\beta\); the observed \(\delta T/T\sim2\times10^{-5}\) then determines that parameter. Separate work finds a compatible inflationary regime. A fixed microphysical \(\beta\) and a full scalar–tensor transfer calculation are still needed for a genuine out-of-sample prediction.[6]

6. The clean GR limit hides the new physics

Minimal torsion vanishes in vacuum and becomes important only far above nuclear density. That correspondence is theoretically attractive, but it leaves no established low-energy anomaly that requires Einstein–Cartan rather than GR.[2, 3]

Evidence and tests

The primordial-spectrum test

Quantitative result

\(\Omega_S=-8.6\times10^{-70}\)

Using the relic-neutrino density, the 2010 model obtains a present torsion density parameter with no new gravitational coupling and a finite minimum radius \(a_{\min}\approx9\,\mu\mathrm m\). The numerical radius inherits that paper's closed-universe normalization and cosmological inputs.[3]

Quantitative threshold

\(\beta_{\rm cr}\approx1/929\)

For the assumed Standard Model degrees of freedom, this separates finite inflation from the eternal-inflation branch. The actual production coefficient remains a phenomenological input.[5]

Decisive test

Predict the CMB before fitting \(\beta\)

Derive the particle-production law microscopically, freeze \(\beta\), and calculate \(A_s\), \(n_s\), its running, non-Gaussianity, and \(r\) through the bounce. The targets include \(n_s=0.9649\pm0.0042\) and \(r_{0.05}<0.036\).[9, 10]

Missing calculation

Complete rotating collapse

A Kerr-like parent must evolve through spin-bearing, particle-producing matter into the expanding child without borrowing homogeneity or isotropy at the stage where both are under test.

The questions

What Einstein–Cartan answers—and leaves open

Gravity

Intrinsic spin receives a geometric source equation and GR is recovered in vacuum. The theory remains classical and does not quantize gravity.[1, 2]
Singularities

Specified spin-fluid models replace singularity formation with a bounce. Anisotropic collapse shows why the result is substantial but not universal.[4, 7]
Expansion

Torsion can drive an early accelerated phase, but the observed late acceleration still requires Λ or another sector.[5, 8]
Beginning

The hot beginning gains a contracting past and a finite-density bounce; its black-hole origin and perturbation transfer remain incomplete.[3–7]
Matter

Fermion spin, particle creation, entropy, and possible matter asymmetry enter one mechanism. The required production law is not yet derived from the Standard Model in the collapsing geometry.[5–7]
Constants

The minimal torsion coupling is fixed by \(G\), but the cosmological model retains Λ, particle parameters, and the production coefficient rather than deriving them.[3, 5]

What comes next

From a bounce to predictions

Einstein–Cartan theory earns attention before cosmology enters the story. Energy has intrinsic angular momentum, and the geometry has a natural place to put it. The extension introduces no propagating vacuum field, leaves tested GR intact, and becomes consequential precisely where classical gravity fails most violently.

Popławski's programme shows how much that single change might buy: a bounce, a child universe, inflation, entropy, and a route to primordial fluctuations. The programme has advanced beyond a verbal singularity cure, but its strongest observable still depends on the particle-production parameter that the observation is used to fix. Deriving that rate and completing the collapse geometry would convert an elegant mechanism into a cosmological prediction.

The central calculation therefore joins the two unfinished halves: solve a rotating, spin-bearing collapse with quantum particle production, then propagate gauge-invariant scalar and tensor perturbations from the parent through the bounce into the child. A microphysical production rate would remove the fitted \(\beta\), decide whether torsion beats shear, and turn the CMB amplitude and polarization into blind predictions.
