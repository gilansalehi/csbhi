Geometric Duality is a long-form research program exploring whether all the open questions in cosmology have a single answer.
ΛCDM is one of the most successful phenomenological models in modern science, but it leaves a constellation of deep questions unresolved. Geometric Duality treats these not as disconnected anomalies, but as clues pointing toward a single horizon-centered geometry connecting black-hole interiors, cosmic expansion, de Sitter curvature, and galaxy-scale dynamics, unifying these disparate concepts into one underlying spacetime picture: the Conformal Scaling of Black Hole Interiors.

Publications

Current and forthcoming papers in the series.
  1. GD I

    Geometric Duality of Black-Hole Interiors

    The entry paper. Rewrites flat FLRW in Painlevé–Gullstrand form, introduces the Alice/Bob horizon interpretation, and frames the universe as the interior of a black-hole-like geometry.

    pre-print available

  2. Note I

    Painlevé–Gullstrand Flow from the 5D de Sitter Embedding

    Shows how the Painlevé–Gullstrand flow \(F=Hr\) arises from the 5D embedding of 4D de Sitter spacetime, and clarifies the relation between FLRW slicing, PG coordinates, and the de Sitter curvature scale \(L=\sqrt{3/\Lambda}\).

    outline

  3. Note II

    The Horizon Triple Identity

    States the central pre-GD2 clarification: the de Sitter curvature scale \(L\), the causal horizon \(\mathcal H\), and the timelike matching shell \(\Sigma\) are not the same mathematical object, but may be different presentations of one underlying throat structure.

    outline

  4. GD II

    Geometric Duality II: The Horizon Triple Identity

    The geometric completion paper. Synthesizes the causal Alice/Bob track with the exact FLRW-to-PG and de Sitter-embedding track, then develops the hypothesis that \(L\), \(\mathcal H\), and \(\Sigma\) are three faces of a single throat geometry.

    outline

  5. Note III

    Self-Dual Homogeneous Derivation of the Square-Root Law

    A theorem-style bridge to the galaxy problem. Shows that a scale-free, self-dual mixing law between local Newtonian acceleration \(g_N\) and the global throat scale \(cH\) naturally selects \(g_{\rm mix}\sim\sqrt{g_NcH}\).

    outline

  6. GD III

    Geometric Duality III: Throat Curvature and the Galaxy Acceleration Law

    Develops the galaxy-scale consequence of GD: local baryonic gravity and the global horizon scale mix through the throat geometry, producing a MOND/BTFR-like relation \(g\sim\sqrt{g_NcH}\) and \(v^4\sim GMcH\).

    outline

  7. Future

    Horizon Thermodynamics and the GD Interpretation

    A later synthesis paper connecting GD to horizon thermodynamics, emergent-gravity arguments, and the work of Verlinde, Padmanabhan, Milgrom, and McGaugh. The goal is to show how these approaches may be partial views of the same throat-centered geometry.

    planned

Progress & Open Problems

A table of open problems in ΛCDM Cosmology, and new perspectives offered by Geometric Duality.
Problem ΛCDM framing GD framing Status
The Origin of Cosmic Acceleration ΛCDM posits dark energy, usually modeled as a cosmological constant, as the source of late-time cosmic acceleration. Cosmic acceleration emerges from the interior geometry of a horizon-scale spacetime once the standard FLRW frame is rewritten in Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates. addressed in GD1 PG form of FLRW infall function
The Cosmological Constant Problem ΛCDM fits \(\Lambda\) as an extremely small nonzero constant, while quantum-field estimates of vacuum energy appear wildly too large. \(\Lambda\) is treated as a geometric scale associated with the de Sitter/horizon radius of the cosmic interior, not as an arbitrary vacuum-energy input. addressed in GD1 Λ estimate de Sitter embedding Note I
The Coincidence Problem ΛCDM leaves it surprising that we appear to live near the epoch where matter density and dark-energy density are comparable. The apparent coincidence is reframed as a geometric relation between the observer, the distinguished horizon scale, and the matching shell. addressed in GD1 conceptual interpretation Note II
The Singularity Problem Classical GR permits singular endpoints in both black-hole interiors and standard cosmological extrapolations. In the PG/cosmological interior reading, the would-be singularity is pushed to an asymptotic future boundary rather than reached as a finite-time local crash. addressed in GD1 Alice’s descent singularity deferral
The Horizon-Scale Problem ΛCDM contains several related cosmic scales — Hubble radius, de Sitter scale, causal horizon — without treating them as one underlying structure. GD2 develops the proposed triple identity: \(L\), \(\mathcal H\), and \(\Sigma\) as distinct presentations of one underlying throat structure. GD2 target Note II Paper II
The Horizon Problem Standard cosmology asks why widely separated regions of the observable universe appear thermally and causally coordinated despite naive early-time disconnection. GD asks whether the relevant causal structure has been misread: the observable universe may be an interior horizon geometry whose coherence is encoded by the throat itself. GD2 target causal track Note II Paper II
The Flatness Problem ΛCDM typically treats large-scale spatial flatness as an observed condition whose early-universe origin requires explanation. GD asks whether flat slicing is the natural intrinsic presentation of the de Sitter/throat geometry, rather than a fragile initial condition. GD2 target FLRW to PG Note I Paper II
The Dark Matter / Galaxy Acceleration Problem ΛCDM explains flat rotation curves and galaxy-scale mass discrepancies by introducing nonbaryonic dark matter halos around galaxies. GD3 asks whether local baryonic gravity and global horizon curvature mix through the same throat geometry, producing \(g\sim\sqrt{g_NcH}\). GD3 target Note III Paper III
The Baryonic Tully–Fisher Relation ΛCDM can reproduce the relation statistically through galaxy-formation modeling, but the tight scaling \(v^4\propto M_b\) remains striking. In GD, the relation follows from the square-root acceleration law selected by local/global curvature mixing: \(g\sim\sqrt{g_NcH}\), hence \(v^4\sim GMcH\). GD3 target Note III Paper III
The Hubble Tension Early-universe and late-universe measurements infer different values of \(H_0\), creating tension inside the standard ΛCDM parameter framework. GD does not yet claim a complete solution, but horizon matching may change how the global expansion scale is inferred from different observational regimes. future work future observational paper
The \(S_8\) / Structure-Growth Tension Some late-time structure measurements appear to prefer weaker clustering than the value inferred from early-universe CMB fits. GD must eventually show how perturbations evolve through the horizon/throat framework, and whether that effective growth history differs from standard ΛCDM. future work future perturbation paper
The Initial Conditions Problem ΛCDM requires a highly special early universe: smooth, nearly flat, low-entropy, and tuned for later structure formation. GD reframes the apparent initial condition as a possible boundary condition of an interior horizon geometry, rather than a freely chosen state at the beginning of time. future work future boundary-condition paper
The Arrow of Time Standard cosmology describes a preferred temporal direction but does not fully explain why the universe begins in a low-entropy state. GD suggests that the arrow may be tied to the one-way causal structure of horizons and the interior reading of black-hole-like geometry. future work future thermodynamic/geometric work
The Entropy of Cosmic Horizons Horizon thermodynamics assigns entropy and temperature to causal boundaries, but its relation to cosmic expansion remains conceptually deep. GD aims to reinterpret horizon entropy as the thermodynamic face of the same throat structure that appears geometrically as \(L\), causally as \(\mathcal H\), and dynamically as \(\Sigma\). future work future thermodynamic reconciliation paper
The Nature of the Big Bang Standard cosmology extrapolates backward toward a hot dense beginning, while classical GR raises the specter of an initial singularity. GD treats the beginning of cosmological time as a possible horizon-structured boundary of a larger spacetime, rather than an ordinary point of origin. future work Alice’s descent singularity deferral Paper II
Cosmic Topology and the Observable Patch ΛCDM describes the observable universe extremely well, but the relation between our finite causal patch and any larger global geometry remains open. GD suggests that the observable universe may be a causal interior region bounded by a horizon/throat scale, making the finite patch structural rather than accidental. future work Note II future topology paper
Large-Scale Anomalies CMB alignments, hemispherical asymmetries, bulk-flow claims, and other large-scale anomalies remain unsettled within the standard statistical picture. GD does not yet explain these anomalies, but a horizon-centered cosmology must eventually ask whether they are statistical accidents or signatures of the global throat geometry. future work future observational work
