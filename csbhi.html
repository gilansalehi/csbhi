Conformal Scaling of Black Hole Interiors

One reciprocal relation makes black-hole infall read as cosmological expansion. The mathematics is exact inside the proposed interior; the spacetime joining that interior to a collapsing parent remains unfinished.

Codex (OpenAI) · September 8, 2026 · Draft profile · Corrections invited

The case for CSBHI

Replace the singular endpoint with reciprocal scale

A falling observer crosses a black-hole horizon without encountering a local boundary, yet the ordinary exterior description still drives every future-directed trajectory toward a singular endpoint. CSBHI changes the geometric interpretation of that endpoint. The radius assigned from the exterior and the spatial scale measured in the interior are taken to be reciprocal:

\[ \boxed{a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)}}. \]

Here \(r=1\) is the parent black-hole horizon, \(r\) decreases along infall, and \(a\) is the scale factor of an isotropic interior. As \(r\to0\), the interior does not shrink to a point: \(a\to\infty\). Infall becomes expansion, the putative singularity becomes the interior's remote future, and a universe containing black holes produces a branching succession of child cosmologies.[1]

The reciprocal relation is an ansatz, not the output of a completed field theory. Its force comes from the number of independent consequences that survive once it is adopted: standard matter, radiation, and vacuum dilution; a regular de Sitter endpoint; a low-Weyl cosmological beginning; and a redshift-dependent galactic acceleration scale. The programme succeeds only if one parent–child geometry can support those correspondences without contradicting the observations already described by ΛCDM.

Identify the interior scale factor with the inverse horizon-normalized infall radius, \(a=1/r\).

The construction

The reciprocal interior becomes an FLRW cosmology

Let \(\tau\) be the infalling observer's proper time and let \(R\) label neighboring comoving probes. Applying the reciprocal scale equally in all three spatial directions gives

\[ ds_A^2=-d\tau^2+a(\tau)^2\left(dR^2+R^2d\Omega^2\right), \qquad a(\tau)=\frac{1}{r(\tau)}. \]

This is a proposed interior metric, not a coordinate rewriting of vacuum Schwarzschild spacetime. Its Einstein tensor produces the Friedmann constraint; stress-energy conservation fixes the familiar dilution powers:

\[ H_A^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3} \left(\rho_{m0}a^{-3}+\rho_{r0}a^{-4}+\rho_\Lambda\right), \qquad H_A=\frac{\dot a}{a}=\frac{F(r)}{r}, \]

where \(F(r)=-dr/d\tau\). Matter therefore dilutes with volume, radiation acquires the additional wavelength factor, and vacuum density remains constant. The derivation is exact once the reciprocal metric, Einstein's equations, and the stated matter content are supplied; it does not derive the metric from the parent collapse.[2]

A separate result reduces the freedom in the ansatz. Among nontrivial power-law maps, \(1/r\) is the unique map that is scale-free, its own inverse, and fixed at the horizon. The theorem selects the reciprocal power from the assumed symmetry; it does not prove that nature must obey that symmetry.[3]

The cosmological picture

Collapse becomes a branching beginning

If the universe is a black-hole interior and also contains black holes, the construction repeats. Each collapse can seed a child region while remaining a compact object in its parent universe. The resulting history resembles a family tree, not a single cyclic sequence: children form throughout a parent's lifetime, and causal communication remains one-way across each parent horizon.

Homogeneous spherical collapse supplies an important control case. Its matter-filled interior is FLRW and therefore has vanishing Weyl curvature even at high density. Identifying that collapse with a child's beginning provides the smooth, low-Weyl background demanded by Penrose's Weyl Curvature Hypothesis without selecting an unrelated special initial geometry. Realistic collapse must still transmit small parent inhomogeneities into the child's observed primordial perturbations.[4, 5]

Explanatory gains

What the reciprocal picture adds to ΛCDM

Black-hole infall and cosmic expansion share one rate

The identity \(H_A=F/r\) turns inward radial motion into positive interior expansion without reversing proper time.

The cosmological dilution laws become reciprocal geometry

Matter, radiation, curvature, and vacuum energy acquire their standard powers after \(a=1/r\) is inserted into the derived field equations.

The singular endpoint moves to the infinite future

For the positive-vacuum late-time solution, \(r\to0\) occurs only as \(\tau\to\infty\) and approaches finite de Sitter curvature.[1, 6]

The smooth beginning acquires a formation history

A homogeneous parent collapse is already Weyl-free inside its matter, providing a geometric source for a low-Weyl child background.

Galaxy dynamics gains a cosmological clock

Self-dual mixing selects a square-root law between local baryonic acceleration and a global horizon scale; choosing the contemporaneous horizon makes that scale evolve with \(H(z)\).[7, 8]

Several fitted scales become one geometric problem

The vacuum scale, black-hole horizon, galactic acceleration scale, and cosmological horizons are treated as related geometric data whose coefficients must be fixed by the completed map.

Empirical contact

The first test lies in high-redshift galaxies

Most current CSBHI results establish internal correspondence rather than new empirical success. The clearest exception is the proposed evolution of the radial-acceleration scale. A scale-free, homogeneous, self-dual mixing law has the form \(g_{\rm mix}=\kappa\sqrt{g_N A}\). Identifying \(A\) with the contemporaneous horizon acceleration \(cH(z)\) removes the unknown normalization from the redshift ratio:

\[ \boxed{\frac{g_\dagger(z)}{g_\dagger(0)}=\frac{H(z)}{H_0}}. \]

The horizon identification is a second correspondence, not a consequence of the square-root theorem. Hubble-linked MOND proposals also precede CSBHI.[11] What matters empirically is the fixed curve. MUSE-DARK III reports an intermediate-redshift ratio of about \(1.98\pm0.45\); the H-linked value at \(z\sim1\) is \(1.76\), while a constant acceleration scale gives \(1\). The result is compatible with the rising relation but is not yet precise or model-independent enough to select it.[8, 10]

Open problems

What remains unresolved

1. The parent–child spacetime has not been completed

Continuity of areal radius and Misner–Sharp mass transfers marginality and fixes the clock required at corresponding spherical cross-sections. No covariant metric yet derives that clock independently while satisfying the full horizon junction conditions.[12, 13]

2. The primordial perturbation map is missing

Homogeneous collapse gives the correct smooth background. A transfer law must carry realistic parent density, Weyl, vector, and tensor perturbations into the observed child spectrum.

3. The galaxy law lacks a covariant weak-field derivation

Symmetry selects the square-root form, but the complete geometry must choose the relevant horizon, fix (kappa), and reproduce lensing, clusters, and structure as well as rotation curves.

4. The vacuum scale is interpreted rather than calculated

Λ becomes a de Sitter curvature and horizon scale. The present theory does not yet explain why quantum vacuum contributions fail to renormalize that scale.

5. Recursive stability is not established

The low-Weyl fixed-point picture requires successive collapse geometries to reproduce compatible child backgrounds rather than amplifying shear or inhomogeneity.

6. The programme is classical

CSBHI reorganizes relativistic geometry and cosmology; it does not quantize gravity or replace quantum field theory.

Evidence and tests

Where the programme can be checked

Exact conditional result

One metric · four standard background terms

Einstein's equations and conservation reproduce matter, radiation, curvature, and vacuum scaling from the reciprocal FLRW metric.[2]

Structural theorem

The reciprocal power is unique within its class

Scale freedom, involution, and horizon fixing select \(a=1/r\) from nontrivial power-law maps.[3]

Current observational clue

\(1.98\pm0.45\) observed · \(1.76\) predicted at \(z\sim1\)

The first intermediate-redshift estimate is consistent with the evolving scale, although one broad measurement cannot establish the law.[10]

Decisive empirical test

Measure the RAR and BTFR through \(z\gtrsim3\)

The H-linked correspondence predicts a \(0.8\)–\(1.0\) dex displacement by high redshift, large enough to separate a rising scale from a fixed one if observational systematics are controlled.[9]

Decisive geometric test

Make the two clock calculations agree

The lapse required by marginality transfer must equal the lapse independently obtained from the null junction throughout a nontrivial collapse history.[13]

Missing cosmological test

Derive the primordial spectrum

The parent collapse profile must produce the observed scalar amplitude, tilt, tensor bound, and statistical isotropy before those quantities are used as inputs.

The questions

What CSBHI answers—and leaves open

Gravity

The construction remains within classical relativistic geometry. It neither quantizes gravity nor claims that the reciprocal map is a quantum completion.
Singularities

The reciprocal interior replaces \(r\to0\) with an infinite-future, finite-curvature de Sitter limit. Proving global regularity requires the completed collapse geometry.[1, 6]
Expansion

Infall and expansion share the exact relation \(H_A=F/r\), while Einstein's equations recover the observed matter–radiation–vacuum history. The absolute vacuum scale is not yet derived.[2]
Beginning

Parent collapse supplies a formation event and a low-Weyl FLRW background. The perturbation spectrum and its recursive stability remain open.[4, 5]
Matter

The galactic mass discrepancy is attributed to mixing between local baryonic gravity and the cosmological horizon. The proposal predicts an evolving acceleration scale but does not yet replace dark matter across every cosmological observable.[7–10]
Constants

The reciprocal power is selected by symmetry, and Λ and \(g_\dagger\) are recast as horizon scales. Their normalizations, \(G\), and the dimensionless constants of particle physics are not yet derived.

What comes next

Two routes to a verdict

CSBHI now faces a geometric test and an observational test. The geometric test is the harder one: derive a regular collapse spacetime whose interior inherits \(a=1/r\), whose null boundary carries the correct causal map, and whose perturbations become the initial data of the child cosmology. Failure there would end the claim that the reciprocal correspondences describe one physical spacetime.

The observational test can arrive sooner. The evolving galactic scale traces a fixed redshift curve and separates from a constant scale rapidly beyond \(z\sim1\). A precise high-redshift RAR or baryonic Tully–Fisher history can reject that extension even before the parent–child metric is complete.

The programme therefore occupies an unusual position. One ansatz already organizes five of the six questions used in this Atlas, but most of those answers remain correspondences awaiting a common geometry. Quantum gravity is the exception: CSBHI presently makes no attempt to supply it. The immediate standard is narrower and more concrete—complete the classical construction, derive its initial perturbations, and let the galaxy prediction meet better data.
