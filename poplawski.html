A note for Professor Nikodem Popławski

A Reciprocal Route to the Universe in a Black Hole

A smooth reciprocal interior in ordinary general relativity, set beside the Einstein–Rosen bridge and the torsion bounce as a third route toward the universe in a black hole.

August 2026

Dear Professor Popławski,

Your 2010 Einstein–Rosen paper constructs a regular black-hole interior in GR with geodesically complete infall and proper time extending to infinity — and you suggested each bridge may hold a new universe [1]. Your later Einstein–Cartan work reached a child universe by a different mechanism, a spin–torsion bounce at finite density [2]. I have arrived on a geometric model somewhere between them, and I would value your insight and criticism.

The proposal is one line: for an infalling observer the interior spatial scale and the exterior areal radius are reciprocal, \(a=1/r\), so the parent's descent to \(r\to0\) is the child's expansion to \(a\to\infty\).

This is not a coordinate change or an ordinary conformal rescaling of Schwarzschild, it is a new interior-geometric ansatz, and the resulting geodesic congruence \(r\to0\) is reached only at infinite proper time, with every curvature invariant approaching a finite de Sitter value. The endpoint \(r=0\) is not a singularity but a future conformal boundary. It therefore realizes the same causal outcome your 2010 bridge identified — geodesically complete infall in place of finite-time termination — through a different asymptotic geometry. Your bridge opens into a region physically equivalent to an asymptotically flat white-hole exterior; whereas the reciprocal interior approaches a de Sitter cosmological future.

The torsion route is where we most differ. You remove the singularity dynamically: spin–torsion repulsion bounces the collapse at finite density, and particle production near the bounce can generate a finite inflationary phase and abundant matter [3]. Your torsion programme separately supplies a proposed mechanism for matter–antimatter asymmetry [4]. The reciprocal interior removes the endpoint geometrically, in ordinary general relativity with no torsion, by deferral to infinite proper time.

I found two consequences which may interest you for their own sake. Homogeneous (Oppenheimer–Snyder) collapse hands its child a Weyl-free interior, which makes low initial Weyl a candidate fixed point of the succession — a recursion-stability mechanism I am working to establish, and the inherited arrow you also draw from the parent collapse. And the same reciprocal instinct, applied one level down to galaxies, yields a parameter-free and falsifiable prediction: the galactic acceleration scale evolves, \(g_\dagger(z)/g_\dagger(0)=H(z)/H_0\).

Your child universes are slightly closed — a finite collapse begetting a closed cosmology. Your Kantowski–Sachs analysis models the anisotropic interior and argues that the fuller geometry should tend toward a three-sphere [5]. The reciprocal interior's clean case is spatially flat, with curvature entering only as an integration constant. The work is a geometric research program — the Conformal Scaling of Black Hole Interiors (gilansalehi.com/csbhi) — and more than anything I hope you find it worth a read.

Sincerely,
Gilan Salehi

Questions for you

  • Could the reciprocal relation \(a=1/r\) describe the post-bounce expansion of an Einstein–Cartan black-hole universe, with torsion providing the missing formation mechanism?
  • CSBHI predicts that the galactic acceleration scale evolves with the cosmological expansion, \(g_\dagger(z)=cH(z)\), rather than remaining constant. Does Einstein–Cartan black-hole cosmology imply a corresponding horizon-controlled acceleration scale, or any mechanism that would fix its evolution?
  • In a recursive black-hole cosmology, each generation must inherit unusually low Weyl curvature despite forming through gravitational collapse. CSBHI derives a reciprocal local-to-global Weyl correspondence that may supply this boundary condition. Could spin–torsion dynamics preserve or enforce that low-Weyl state through the bounce?
