A note for Professor Nikodem Popławski

A Reciprocal Route to the Universe in a Black Hole

Every collapse a cosmogenesis — reached, in ordinary general relativity, by a reciprocal map rather than a torsion bounce.

August 2026

Dear Professor Popławski,

Of everyone working today, you have taken most seriously the idea this project is built on: that our universe is the interior of a black hole, and that every gravitational collapse is the birth of a new one. I have come to the same picture from a different direction, and I would value your judgment on it — most of all where it departs from yours.

The construction begins from one identification. For an observer crossing the horizon, the interior spatial scale and the exterior areal radius are reciprocal, \(a=1/r\). The parent's descent to \(r\to0\) is the child's expansion to \(a\to\infty\): what reads as the approach to a singularity in the exterior description is the smooth cosmological future in the interior one. The universe on the far side is the reciprocal interior, and it is a black hole in the present one — the picture you have argued for, obtained as a change of description rather than as a new field.

That last clause is where we differ, and it is what I would most like your eye on. You remove the singularity dynamically: spin–torsion coupling in Einstein–Cartan gravity supplies a repulsion that bounces the collapsing matter at finite density. The reciprocal interior removes it geometrically, in standard general relativity with no torsion added. Along the interior congruence, \(r\to0\) is reached only at infinite proper time and every curvature invariant approaches a finite de Sitter value; the endpoint \(r=0\) is not a curvature singularity at all but the future conformal boundary of an asymptotically de Sitter spacetime. I am careful about what this does and does not establish: it is singularity deferral within the constructed interior, not yet a proof that this interior is the unique smooth continuation of the exterior collapse.

So the question I bring you is whether these are two accounts of one process or two different processes. Does your Einstein–Cartan interior, seen from within, also carry a reciprocal relation between its scale and the parent's areal radius? I suspect \(a=1/r\) may be the effective kinematics of what torsion does dynamically — that the bounce and the deferral are the same crossing described in two languages — but I cannot yet show it, and you are the person best placed to tell me whether that hope is sound or already excluded.

Two things follow that may interest you for their own sake. The first is the arrow of time. Homogeneous (Oppenheimer–Snyder) collapse hands its child a Weyl-free interior, so a smooth universe forms the smooth overdensities that collapse into smooth children, and a low-entropy beginning becomes a fixed point of the succession rather than a chosen initial condition — the inherited arrow you also draw from the parent collapse, obtained here as a property of the formation process. The second is that the same reciprocal instinct, applied one level down to galaxies, yields a parameter-free and falsifiable prediction — that the galactic acceleration scale evolves as \(g_\dagger(z)/g_\dagger(0)=H(z)/H_0\). The program tries to pay rent.

One honest difference I would not want to hide: your baby universes are slightly closed, a finite collapse begetting a closed cosmology, whereas the reciprocal interior's clean case is spatially flat, with curvature entering only as an integration constant. Whether the finiteness of the parent collapse should force a slightly closed child is exactly the kind of question I would rather hear from you than guess at. The work is a small geometric program — the Conformal Scaling of Black Hole Interiors (gilansalehi.com/csbhi) — and more than anything I hope you find it worth your time.

Sincerely,
Gilan Salehi

Two routes to the same destination

We agree on the conclusion — the interior of a black hole is a new universe, and ours is one such interior. The routes differ, and the differences are where the physics is. The columns below set them side by side, not to rank them but to locate the questions worth asking.

A near-term test Torsion cosmology connects to inflation-free expansion and baryon asymmetry. The galactic scale evolves, \(g_\dagger(z)/g_\dagger(0)=H(z)/H_0\) — falsifiable in the JWST era.

The questions I would put to you

  • Seen from within, does the Einstein–Cartan interior carry a reciprocal relation between its scale factor and the parent's areal radius — is \(a=1/r\) the effective kinematics of the torsion bounce?
  • Does the finiteness of the parent collapse force a slightly closed child, or can a spatially flat interior be consistent with black-hole cosmogenesis?
  • Is geometric singularity deferral in standard GR a genuine alternative to the torsion bounce, or does a complete continuation of the exterior collapse still require new gravitational physics?

A correction at any of these would materially improve the program, and a connection to an existing result in torsion cosmology would be as welcome as an objection.
